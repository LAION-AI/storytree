EXT. THE STONE - DAWN

    Sun on the granite. The face toward town is already white.

    CORA kneels in the shade of the north side. A cloth bundle in her lap. She scrapes a hole with her fingers.

                        CORA
              One.
              Two.
              Three.
              Four.
              That's the whole check.

    She sets them in. Packs the dirt with the heel of her sneaker. Tamps it. Tamps it again.

                        CORA
              Paid.
              Table's closed.

    She wipes her hands on the green dress. Picks a hardware-store lamp out of the grass. Does not switch it on.

                        CORA
              You walk the light.
              You don't look here.

    No one on the sun side. She walks.

    INT. AL'S DEPOT DINER - MORNING

    Back door. Keys on the hook. The lamp goes under the counter, out of sight.

    She counts the tip can without taking a bill.

                        CORA
              Ninety-eight.

    A Polaroid hangs off the strap across her chest, still milky. She unclips it. A face is only a stain in the emulsion, coming up slow.

    She slides the print under the register clip, next to the can. Turns it so the wet square faces the empty booths.

                        CORA
              You stay there.
              That's your table.

    She takes the strap off. Lays the camera on the counter like a closed check.

                        CORA
              I keep all of them.
              You knew that.

    She feeds a cassette into the kitchen deck. Presses play.

    His voice, thin, already recorded.

                        HALE (FROM DECK)
              If you get to the stone first--
              north side. Dirt, not the box.
              Don't wait for me.

                        CORA
              I heard you.

                        HALE (FROM DECK)
              If I'm not--

    The ribbon runs out. Leader. A long hiss.

                        CORA
              You're not.

    She leaves it hissing.

    On the front glass a roll-bar climbs from the sill, a dark seam, and holds halfway up.

    Her shoulders flinch. Her face catches up late.

    Outside, the street fog has a grain in it. It does not drift.

                        CORA
              Not weather.

    She stands there. Then she takes the pencil off the clip, dates the back of a ticket, and spikes it.

                        CORA
              Six-forty.
              I was here.
              Still here.

    More of the Polaroid now. The mouth. The edge of a hand at the bottom of the frame, dark, unfinished.

                        CORA
              We aren't even.
              You don't leave a table at nothing
              and call it even.

    She goes to the door. Deadbolt in her hand. The looping road out there, grainy, the same curve.

                        CORA
              Still closed.

    She turns the lock anyway. Flips the sign to OPEN.

                        CORA
              Breakfast.

    She starts a pot. Sets one mug on the last booth. Does not sit. Counts the can a second time.

                        CORA
              Ninety-eight.
              Southbound when it runs.

    The hiss keeps on. The roll-bar holds. His face keeps coming up in the clip.

    She wipes the counter. Precise. Not loud.

                        CORA
              Till's real.
              Picture's real.
              That's the night.

    A blank ticket on the wheel. She leaves it blank.

                        CORA
              You're going to have to come in
              and order like everybody else.

    The Polaroid darkens another degree. She does not take it down.

                        CORA
              We're open.

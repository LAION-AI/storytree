INT. AL'S DEPOT DINER - NIGHT

2:20 on the neon clock. CORA wipes the last circle of crumbs off the counter into her palm. The stools are empty. The door is shut.

She tips the crumbs in the bin. She does not sit.

The coffee can by the register is fat with singles. She counts it. Stops. Counts it again.

                        CORA
              Eighty-four. Eighty-five.
              Eighty-six. Eighty-six.

She writes 86 on the clip-pad. Under the counter, the Polaroid. She takes it the way she takes it every night. Strap over the head. It hangs on her chest.

From the kitchen, the radio thins.

                        DJ (O.S.)
              WBTN Bennington. End of the
              sheet. If you're hearing this
              you're nights or you're lost.
              One last one, then we go dark
              till six. Keep the light
              you've got.

                        CORA
              I've got the clock.

She frames the empty stools and the neon 2:20.

                        CORA
              Two-twenty. Stools. Clock.
              Empty. That's the room.
              That's seven.

Shutter. Motor. A wet print shoves out. She lays it on the counter face-down. Pencil. She dates the back. She does not turn it over.

Last booth. A check still trapped under the salt. Through the front glass: the careful man on the sidewalk, map folded, not looking in.

                        CORA
              Booth four. One coffee. Pie
              he didn't finish. Paid even.
              Tipped. Left the bill under
              the salt like he might come
              back for the number.

She raises the camera the way she would tear a register tape.

                        CORA
              Name on the check is Hale. I
              don't know a Hale.

Shutter. Motor. Second print. Face-down beside the first. She dates the back. Her eyes stay on the pencil.

                        CORA
              Eight. A check. Just a check.

She pulls a cassette from the drawer. Slots it under the counter. RECORD. The last song leaks in. She talks into the gaps, low, precise.

                        CORA
              Night. Register two-forty
              even. Can eighty-six. Road is
              south if I walk it in the
              morning. I closed last night
              too. I don't have last night.
              I have the stack. I have the
              can. I have this. Last check
              Hale. He paid. He tipped. He
              left. I took the room. I took
              the check.

The song dies. The DJ comes back smaller.

                        DJ (O.S.)
              That's the lot. WBTN
              Bennington. We are leaving
              the air. Six o'clock.

A tone. Then hiss. She lets the hiss run. She does not rewind. She thumbs STOP. Leaves the cassette in the deck.

                        CORA
              Last song. That's the night.

She picks up both prints still face-down and walks them to the locker by the kitchen pass. Inside: a clipped sheaf, dated backs, already six thick.

She clips the two new ones on. The sheaf jumps. One print slips. The careful man's face, still milky, not set.

She shoves it into the clip without turning it to herself.

                        CORA
              It's a room. It's a check.
              It's eighty-six and a road.

She shuts the locker. The bill stays under the salt. The can stays by the register. The radio is only hiss.

                        CORA
              Southbound in the morning.
              Same as last morning.

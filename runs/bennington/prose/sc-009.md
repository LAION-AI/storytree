INT. AL'S DEPOT DINER - NIGHT

Two in the morning. Radio low. Fresh pot.

The door opens. HALE does not rush it. Charcoal coat. Clean shoes. A counter stool.

CORA has the pot. She sees his face. Shoulders first.

The pot ticks the rim of his cup. Coffee jumps. She steadies. Looks at him. Looks at the till. Pours.

                    CORA
          That's sitting.

                    HALE
          Yes.

                    CORA
          You don't stand tonight.

                    HALE
          I won't.

She sets the cup down. Does not ask what he wants. His hands stay on the Formica, clear of her wrist.

                    HALE
          You looked at me.

                    CORA
          Door opened.

                    HALE
          Not like a door.

She wipes the spill. Precise. Does not look up.

                    CORA
          Eating or just the cup.

                    HALE
          Eggs. Please.

                    CORA
          Over.

                    HALE
          Over.

Two on the grill. The radio holds a song already ending.

                    HALE
          You could lock it.

                    CORA
          Till still counts.

                    HALE
          After it closes.

                    CORA
          I took the road. Southbound.
          Same lot I left from.

                    HALE
          When.

                    CORA
          After last shift. I'm still
          here. So the pot's on.

She plates the eggs. Sets them. Wrist on her side.

                    HALE
          Thank you.

                    CORA
          You can stop that.

                    HALE
          All right.

He empties the plate. A single lands by the saucer. Four ones, folded once, into the coffee can.

She watches them drop. Says nothing.

He takes a Polaroid from the coat. Sets it on the counter face-up.

A church window. Wet street. A silhouette mid-stride.

She does not pick it up.

                    CORA
          That's mine.

                    HALE
          Yes.

                    CORA
          You had this.

                    HALE
          I didn't mean to keep it.

                    CORA
          And now you don't.

                    HALE
          It isn't mine.

                    CORA
          You had a reason to take it.

He does not fill that. His hand starts toward the print and stops.

                    CORA
          So why bring it back.

                    HALE
          Because I had it.

She waits. That is all he gives.

                    CORA
          That doesn't put you even.

                    HALE
          I know.

She takes the print by one corner. Turns it. Pencil on the back. Slides it into the banded stack under the counter. Does not thank him.

She lifts the empty plate. Her wrist turns out and away, past his reach.

                    CORA
          You're finished.

                    HALE
          The plate is.

                    CORA
          Kitchen's closed.

                    HALE
          It isn't two-thirty.

                    CORA
          For you it is.

She dumps the plate in the well. Comes back empty-handed. He is still on the stool.

                    HALE
          Cora.

                    CORA
          That's the tag.

His eyes go to her wrist and stop. He swallows. Does not reach.

                    CORA
          You sit. You pay. You go.

                    HALE
          I will not touch you.

                    CORA
          You already did. That's
          why it's in the pile.

She squares the stack. New print flush with the rest.

                    CORA
          You walk in again, you sit.
          You put what isn't yours on
          the counter. You don't come
          around the till. Hands where
          I can see them.

                    HALE
          Understood.

                    CORA
          You understood last time.

He stands. Looks at the door, then the glass, then her hands. He does not rush it. He goes.

The stool stays empty. The cup stays empty. She does not clip the print to the register. She touches the can once, counting the four by feel.

The radio finds another hour. The glass stays clean.

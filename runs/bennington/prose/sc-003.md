EXT. MAIN STREET - NIGHT

Before first light. Hale leaves the diner. A sedan passes him, takes the
corner, and comes back.

He stops at a closed shop window and spreads a marked map on the glass.
Bulletin. Circular. A strip of names.

                    HALE
          Bill's paid. Street's the same
          street. Diner. Hardware. Drugstore.
          Church lot. I marked them. I walked
          them. East end fog. West end fog.
          Same sedan twice. The woods road put
          me back on Main. That isn't a road.
          That's a loop. So the prize is not a
          road. The prize is a procedure.

                    HALE
                         (reading)
          First Congregational Church. Sunday
          community sunrise. Founders' Green.
          First light. Memorial stone. Wreaths
          to remain. No replacements. No orders
          accepted for the service set.

                    HALE
          If they will not take an order, the
          set is already in town. War stone,
          courthouse lawn. Cenotaph, college.
          Founders' stone, old green. Three
          stones. One of them sits in a green.
          One notice schedules a dawn.
          Courthouse is a noon flag. College is
          a Saturday. The green is the only
          hour that has a first light written
          next to a stone. Not three sites. If
          it were three, it wouldn't be a door.
          It would be weather. One lock. Not a
          town full of them.

He turns the hardware circular, ticking lines.

                    HALE
          In stock: lamp, glass, chain, dry
          cell, rope. Catalog: two weeks.
          Incoming: none. If it isn't on a
          shelf or on a stone or in a pew by
          tonight, it isn't a piece. So the
          pieces are not for sale. They are
          placed. A finite set. Already named.
          Already somewhere. Four notices.
          Four objects. Cross one off and the
          column breaks. One site you can speak
          at. Only one. Dawn. Not a rumor. A
          count I can finish. I can walk a
          count. I cannot walk a rumor.

He looks at the fog at the end of the block. It sits like a dead channel.

                    HALE
          I already followed the road. It gave
          me the same three doors. There is no
          fourth way out of town. There is a
          dawn and a stone or there is nothing.
          One door pays. Name the door.

He writes on the margin.

                    HALE
          Ritual.

Writes again.

                    HALE
          Four.

Writes again.

                    HALE
          The stone in the old green.

He folds the map so the written column faces in. Crease. Papers into the
coat.

His fingers stall on the last crease. He opens the right hand and looks
at it as if it belonged to someone else. Closes it. Both hands go into
his pockets.

He walks on. Does not rush the next door.

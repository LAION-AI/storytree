EXT. FOUNDERS' STONE - DAWN

Shop-lights scrape another foot of gravel. The stone's far flank loses the last of its dark. Cora's lamp stays on his chest.

                    CORA
          I said choose.

Hale does not speak. He opens the jacket. Four tokens sit in the lining.

                    CORA
          Close it.

                    HALE
          No.

                    CORA
          Then say the word.

                    HALE
          No.

                    CORA
          Say it or kill it. Those are the two things.

                    HALE
          I'm not going to say it.

He takes the tokens out. The shake in his wrist knocks them together. He waits until they sit still. He holds them over her free hand.

                    HALE
          Please.

                    CORA
          You put those on me, I don't give them back.

                    HALE
          I know.

                    CORA
          And the lamp stays up.

                    HALE
          Keep it.

                    CORA
          Four.

                    HALE
          Four.

He sets them in her palm. She has to close or drop the set. Her fingers close. He presses the cassette in on top of the four.

                    HALE
          That too.

                    CORA
          That's my tape.

                    HALE
          It's yours again.

                    CORA
          Don't thank me.

                    HALE
          Thank you.

                    CORA
          I said don't.

The sun clears the church wall. Light takes the face of the stone, then the cut grass. He steps back into the last scrap of shade. The hand he used to give lags in the open.

Light hits it. Skin tightens dark across the old scar at the thumb. Smoke lifts off the burn. The wound does not start to close.

He puts the hand into the shade after the fact. The scorch stays. The shake has the arm now.

                    CORA
          Your hand.

                    HALE
          Yes.

                    CORA
          It isn't knitting.

                    HALE
          It won't.

She hikes the Polaroid on its strap. Tokens and cassette pinned to her ribs with her wrist. The lamp stays live. She fires at his face.

The camera shoves a print out. She catches it against the tokens. Gray milk. Then a mouth. Then the even face, the smoking hand, the church window taking the flash like a wrong hour.

                    CORA
          Stay there.

                    HALE
          I'm here.

                    CORA
          You take one step at the light and I don't stop.

                    HALE
          I won't come toward it.

The pack on the strap is almost flat. She turns the print and dates the back in pencil, hard enough to score the paper.

                    CORA
          You're still here.

                    HALE
          Yes.

                    CORA
          After the stone went light.

                    HALE
          After.

                    CORA
          So the word isn't coming.

                    HALE
          No.

Shop-lights scrape again. Dawn sits on the granite. He stays in the strip by the wall. The scorch does not close. She does not kill the lamp.

                    CORA
          What do I owe for this.

                    HALE
          Nothing.

                    CORA
          You don't leave a table at nothing.

His burned hand twitches toward the jacket, toward a folded single. It stops.

                    HALE
          Keep the picture.

                    CORA
          I keep all of them.

                    HALE
          Then we're even.

                    CORA
          We aren't.

He looks at the lamp. At the print coming up in her hand. At her. He does not step forward.

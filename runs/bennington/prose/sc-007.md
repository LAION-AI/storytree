INT. AL'S DEPOT DINER - DAY

Afternoon. Empty booths. The radio dies mid-bar. Cora stops it, sets the cassette recorder beside the Polaroid sheaf, and hits RECORD. The song does not come back.

                    CORA
          Alley behind the depot. No date. I
          date the backs. I didn't date this.

She turns the next print to the microphone.

                    CORA (CONT'D)
          Room I don't have a key for. Bed not
          made. That's our front glass from the
          wrong side of it.

                    CORA (CONT'D)
          Charcoal coat. My counter. He isn't
          looking. I don't have him on a check.
          I don't have a time. Check would have
          been a coffee. Coffee I would have
          poured.

                    CORA (CONT'D)
          Fourth I will not own. No hour. No
          room. Pack is short four and I didn't
          pull four.

She turns her wrist to the mic. A yellowing oval bruise.

                    CORA (CONT'D)
          What they don't hold: clock-out. The
          walk home. Who had my arm. Till
          balanced at close. I don't have the
          close. I have the number and not the
          night.

                    CORA (CONT'D)
          Last song comes off this tape. Hours
          go here.

She slides the recorder under the counter. Red light stays. She pours a pot nobody ordered.

The bell. DENISE COLE — scrubs too big, cardigan, collar worked up to her jaw. Last booth. She looks at the pie case, then at Cora. She does not look at the door.

Cora brings coffee without asking. She draws the collar down with two fingers. A bruise at the throat: same oval, same color. Cora sets her own wrist on the table beside it.

Denise pulls the collar up. She leaves Cora's wrist where it is.

                    DENISE
          That's a cuff. We had a walker. He
          fought the rail.

                    CORA
          That's the same shape. Same two days
          old.

                    DENISE
          Don't.

                    CORA
          Tuesday. Last booth. Your check was
          two-forty. You left a dollar on a
          dollar-fifty pie. You don't have the
          pie.

                    DENISE
          I have the ward. I have a clock that
          said two and then it said five. That's
          a shift. That's twelve hours.

                    CORA
          Two to five is three. That's a hole
          you kept.

Denise looks at the Polaroids. Not at the door. Not at Cora.

                    DENISE
          I remember a man who paid and didn't
          eat. I am not giving you his face.

                    CORA
          Then give me the clock. Two. Then
          five. Say that again.

                    DENISE
          Two. Then five. Corridor was empty. I
          still had gauze in my pocket. That's
          the scrap. That's all.

                    CORA
          That's two of us.

                    DENISE
          I walk onto the floor and say two
          then five, they send me home. I go
          home, I sleep. I wake up and the
          chart is clean and I'm the write-up.

                    CORA
          This isn't the floor. This is last
          booth. Check's on the house.

                    DENISE
          Don't put him on your tape.

The red light under the counter. Denise sees it.

                    CORA
          Tape's already running. Song's
          already gone.

                    DENISE
          Then you've got what you get from me.
          A clock that jumped. A man I won't
          name. You don't get a face and you
          don't get me on the floor in the
          morning.

                    CORA
          I don't need the floor.

Cora lifts the camera off her chest and thumbs the pack.

                    DENISE
          Where.

                    CORA
          Crossing. Then Briggs. Pot's fresh.

                    DENISE
          Cora. Don't make this bigger than a
          bruise.

                    CORA
          Register's mine till six.

EXT. MAIN STREET - DAY

Chalk on brick: three white hashes and an arrow toward the crossing. Cora photographs it. The print spits. She pencils the back.

EXT. CHURCH - DAY

Stained glass lit from inside. The sky still pale. She photographs the window. She does not take the steps.

INT. BRIGGS HARDWARE - DAY

A carbon spiked by the register. UV tubes in a thick handwritten block. She photographs the carbon. She photographs the shelf. She pulls the carbon free.

                    VOICE (O.S.)
          You paying for that copy?

                    CORA
          Batteries. Two packs. The copy comes
          with the number.

She puts cash down. She does not linger.

INT. AL'S DEPOT DINER - DAY

Grill heat. The recorder hisses under the counter. Denise's cup is full.

Cora clips the carbon into the sheaf. Chalk. Window. Tubes. Invoice on top.

Her hand shakes once on the clip. It stills.

                    DENISE
          That's hardware. That's a church.
          That's not what I gave you.

                    CORA
          Same afternoon. Carbon's time-stamped
          for today. Window was lit at twenty
          to five.

                    DENISE
          Twenty to five is not a bruise.

                    CORA
          Twenty to five is a hole with a
          receipt.

                    DENISE
          Then it's too much. I have a pulse I
          can count at six. That's what I get.
          I'm not taking a church home and I'm
          not taking a hardware ticket.

                    CORA
          You don't take it. It stays in the
          clip.

                    DENISE
          Then don't sleep.

Denise stands. She leaves the coffee. She looks at Cora, at the pass, at the floor. Not the door. The bell. She's gone.

Cora turns the sheaf so the carbon sits face-up. She counts the tip can twice. She starts a fresh pot. She speaks to the red light, low.

                    CORA
          Till's open. Road doesn't go. This
          stays.

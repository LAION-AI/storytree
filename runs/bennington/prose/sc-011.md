INT. AL'S DEPOT DINER - NIGHT

A key. The bolt. CORA VOSS lets herself in and locks it again. Overheads off. Counter fluorescents only. Polaroid on its strap. Cassette in her sweater pocket.

Last booth: HALE. Coat on. Hands on the Formica.

She looks at him once. Does not ask.

                    CORA
          We're closed.

                    HALE
          I know.

She goes behind the counter. Sets the cassette in the deck.

                    CORA
          Then sit there and hear it.

She hits PLAY. Hiss. Then his voice, calm, close to the heads.

                    HALE (FROM DECK)
          Hold still. Breathe. That's all.

She stands with her back to the booth. Pours a coffee she does not drink.

                    HALE (FROM DECK)
          Good. Stay there.

A scrape on the ribbon. Her breath, thin. Cloth.

                    HALE (FROM DECK)
          That's all.

CLICK.

She does not turn. Rewinds to the same numbers. PLAY.

                    HALE (FROM DECK)
          Hold still. Breathe. That's all.

Her hand stays on the deck.

                    CORA
          That's you.

                    HALE
          Yes.

                    CORA
          Say the words.

                    HALE
          You have them.

                    CORA
          I have a machine. I want them from your mouth.

He does not speak.

The second pass hits the scrape. Her breath on the tape. She touches the inside of her wrist and takes the hand back.

                    CORA
          How many nights.

                    HALE
          I didn't count.

                    CORA
          Three I kept. I thought the first ones were the radio bleeding. They weren't.

She pencils the spine without looking at him. A date. Two words under it.

                    CORA
          Denise takes last booth after the ward. If this stays in the deck she hears you tell me to hold still.

                    HALE
          Don't play it for her.

                    CORA
          Then take it.

His hand stays on the table. Fingers aligned with the Formica grain. He does not reach.

                    CORA
          You were already sitting. You could have put it in your coat.

                    HALE
          Yes.

                    CORA
          Why.

He does not answer. Something in her face goes flat.

                    CORA
          You pay exact. You leave a single. You say please. This is what the please is for.

                    HALE
          You should sleep.

                    CORA
          Sleep is what takes it. That's why it's on tape.

                    HALE
          Put it away.

                    CORA
          It is away. It's mine.

She ejects the cassette. Holds it so the label shows. Pockets it.

                    CORA
          Coffee's here if you want it. Till's counted.

                    HALE
          Thank you.

                    CORA
          Don't do that.

                    HALE
          I heard it.

                    CORA
          You'll hear it again. I rewind things.

She still does not look at him. His hand has not moved.

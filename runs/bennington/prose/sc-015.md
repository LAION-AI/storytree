EXT. FOUNDERS' STONE - NIGHT

Wet granite. Cut grass. The steeple clock is a minute off and still ticking.

Hale has the last shade against the church wall. One hand stays inside the jacket.

Cora is already on the path side of the stone. Hardware-store UV lamp in her right hand. The Polaroid stack in her left, strap across the sweater.

She does not come closer.

                    CORA
          Two hours.

                    HALE
          I counted.

                    CORA
          You counted the clock. Two hours from the color. The clock is wrong.

He looks east. No line. A thinner black only.

                    HALE
          Thank you.

                    CORA
          Don't.

She thumbs the switch.

A violet bar cuts the grass. The shade pulls back to his shoes. The lamp hums like a bad ballast.

Uphill, metal scrapes on wet ground. Shop-lights on casters, dragged across the green. A second violet edge eating the path toward the stone.

                    CORA
          That isn't me.

                    HALE
          I know whose it is.

                    CORA
          Then you know the path is going. This wall is the last dark that still has a wall.

                    HALE
          Please put it down.

                    CORA
          No.

She lifts the lamp a fraction. He does not move his feet. The stone's near flank loses a strip of black.

                    CORA
          You have them. All four. Inside.

                    HALE
          Yes.

                    CORA
          Say the count.

                    HALE
          Four.

                    CORA
          And the print you kept out.

He shows it. Does not step in to give it back.

                    CORA
          Date it.

                    HALE
          I don't have a pencil.

She does. She shoots the stone, the violet, him in the leftover shade. The camera spits the print. She writes on the back without looking down long.

                    CORA
          Dawn site. Night six. You. The lamp. The lights on the green.
          Nineteen.

                    HALE
          You'll sleep.

                    CORA
          Not before the color. After, maybe. This stays.

                    HALE
          The tape doesn't.

One step. Not into the beam. Into the outer pocket of the sweater.

He takes the cassette.

She does not drop the lamp. The left hand locks on the stack. The pencil is still between her fingers.

                    CORA
          That's the night.

                    HALE
          That's the last decode.

                    CORA
          It's both. You know it's both.

                    HALE
          Yes.

                    CORA
          Give it back.

                    HALE
          No.

                    CORA
          Then I burn the hand that has it.

                    HALE
          You came to burn more than a hand.

                    CORA
          I came so the hours stay. You take the hours, the lamp is what I have left.

His empty hand starts toward a courtesy and stops halfway. Nothing in it. Nothing finished.

                    HALE
          Please.

                    CORA
          Please what.

                    HALE
          Don't.

                    CORA
          Don't what. Say the noun.

He does not.

She raises the lamp to the height of his chest. The violet tightens. The scar at the base of his right thumb starts a fine shake he cannot still.

                    CORA
          That isn't the cold.

                    HALE
          No.

                    CORA
          How long.

                    HALE
          Less than the two hours.

                    CORA
          Then you feed or you come apart. Not on me.

                    HALE
          I wasn't going to ask.

                    CORA
          You were going to take. That's the difference I kept.

East goes a shade that is not black. Not a line. A color.

The shop-lights scrape closer. The stone's far flank loses its dark. The wall-shade is a strip.

                    HALE
          The tokens stay.

                    CORA
          In the jacket. I can see the shape.

                    HALE
          I haven't said it.

                    CORA
          The dawn word.

                    HALE
          Not yet.

                    CORA
          Not yet is still a plan.

She holds the beam. He does not step into it. The cassette sits in the stopped hand. The shake has the wrist now.

                    CORA
          Choose the dark that's left or I take it off you.

                    HALE
          Cora.

                    CORA
          That's a name. It isn't a choice.

He looks at the cassette. At the lamp. At her face.

He does not step forward.

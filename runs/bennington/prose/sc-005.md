EXT. MAIN STREET - NIGHT

Hardware-store tubes hang over the sidewalks in a white corridor. The crossing hums. Shop-windows throw the same hard light into the street.

Hale stops at the curb. He does not step off it.

A moth hits a tube and drops.

He turns up the alley, the long way around the block.

INT. BRIGGS AND SON - NIGHT

The after-hours bell. Tom Briggs has color in his face that yesterday wasn't there. He counts a short stack of long white tubes on an otherwise empty rack. A carbon invoice hangs on the spike by the register.

                        TOM
              We're closed unless you're cash.

                        HALE
              Cash.

                        TOM
              Then you're in. Don't ask me for the white ones. Fellow cleaned me out last night. Two crates. Left the carbon like a thank-you note.

Hale reads the invoice without touching it. No name that matches a shop. A box number. A quantity that used to be a full case.

                        HALE
              How many left.

                        TOM
              Sixteen. I had forty-eight yesterday. I counted twice. I always count twice.

                        HALE
              The crossing.

                        TOM
              He hung them himself. Asked which fixtures take the long tubes. Paid the invoice, didn't haggle. I don't finish stories about my weekends but that one I wrote down.

Tom taps the carbon with a knuckle.

                        TOM
              You walked around.

                        HALE
              The crossing's lit.

                        TOM
              It's a sidewalk. It's always been a sidewalk.

                        HALE
              Not like that.

                        TOM
              You want anything that isn't a tube.

                        HALE
              Not tonight. Thank you.

                        TOM
              You look like you took the long way three times.

Hale is already at the door. He does not rush it.

EXT. BENNINGTON MEMORIAL - NIGHT

A badge reader. A dead red light. Through the glass: coolers with their lids up. Ice. No bags.

A sheet of paper taped inside the door. Handwritten. Names. Amounts. A heading that only says TONIGHT.

Denise Cole comes out the side. Color back in her face. The bruise on her wrist sits above the cuff like a clock no one scheduled.

                        DENISE
              Door's badge only. Even if you brought flowers.

                        HALE
              The coolers.

                        DENISE
              Ice. That's the inventory. Ice and that list. If you're not on it, you're not coming in.

                        HALE
              Whose list.

                        DENISE
              If you have to ask, you're not on it. I had three empty beds and nothing to hang. They sent a man with a pencil instead of a delivery.

                        HALE
              Night access.

                        DENISE
              Badge. Mine still works for the stairwell. Not for the bank. Don't stand in the light.

                        HALE
              Thank you.

                        DENISE
              Don't. Find another floor. There isn't one.

She pulls the cuff down over the bruise and goes toward town without looking back at the glass.

EXT. COLLEGE LAWN - NIGHT

From the long way around, past the last safe shadow: a ring of students on the open grass. Fourteen. A cup goes hand to hand. In the middle of the ring a mouth finds a throat. Nobody flinches.

A STUDENT peels off and comes as far as the path. Wine-dark at the rim of the cup.

                        STUDENT
              You can come in. Nobody's charging.

                        HALE
              No thank you.

                        STUDENT
              It's easier than downtown. We already said yes. All of us. You don't have to wait your turn in an alley.

                        HALE
              I can see that.

                        STUDENT
              Then come stand in the ring.

                        HALE
              Good night.

                        STUDENT
              Careful ones always say that. Then they come back when the lights downtown get worse.

Hale does not enter the quad. He keeps walking, college lights at his shoulder, not at his face.

EXT. TREELINE - NIGHT

The last streetlight. Beyond it, at the edge of the programmed dark, a body lies open. The chest has nothing left in it that a chest is for.

A WOMAN on a porch, receiver cord stretched to the rail.

                        WOMAN
              Don't. I already called. They said stay in the porch light.

                        HALE
              I can see it.

                        WOMAN
              It's open. There's nothing where it should be. They took what was in it and left the rest like a receipt.

                        HALE
              You should go inside.

                        WOMAN
              I am inside. This is as far as the cord goes. You keep that line at your back.

                        HALE
              Yes.

                        WOMAN
              You don't look surprised.

                        HALE
              I'm walking.

He turns so the trees stay behind him. He does not go closer.

EXT. MAIN STREET - NIGHT

The long way returns him to the diner end of the block. The tubes still own the crossing.

Through the glass: Cora Voss at the register. Color back in her face. She counts the tip can, stops, counts it again. The Polaroid hangs on its strap and she does not lift it.

Hale stands in the door. He does not sit.

                        CORA
              You're later.

                        HALE
              The street's longer.

                        CORA
              Coffee.

                        HALE
              I'll stand.

She pours anyway. Sets it on the counter where a seated man would reach it. He leaves it there.

                        CORA
              Tom sold the whole rack. Denise says the hospital's a freezer with a guest list. You walked around the lights.

                        HALE
              Yes.

                        CORA
              They weren't there yesterday.

                        HALE
              No.

                        CORA
              Everybody's walking around something. You want the check for a coffee you won't drink.

                        HALE
              No thank you.

His eyes hold on the color in her throat one beat too long, then go to the exit, then to her hands. His own hand on the counter does not move.

                        CORA
              You can sit. The till's real.

                        HALE
              I know.

                        CORA
              Then why are you still in the door.

                        HALE
              The crossing's closed. I'm taking the alley.

He leaves the coffee where she set it. He does not rush the door.

Outside he turns away from the white corridor and takes the long way again, past the humming tubes, past the invoice still on Tom's spike, past the hospital list and the lawn and the thing at the trees. He does not change the route except to keep the light off his skin.

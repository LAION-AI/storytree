EXT. AL'S DEPOT DINER - NIGHT

HALE tries the door. The handle stops.

A handwritten card hangs in the glass: PRIVATE FUNCTION.

Inside, CORA turns the deadbolt. She leaves the card where it is. The Polaroid hangs on her chest. She looks at him through the glass and does not come closer.

He knocks once. Does not knock twice.

                        HALE
              Cora.

She taps the card with one finger.

                        CORA
              That's the whole sentence.

                        HALE
              Coffee. That's all.

                        CORA
              Kitchen's dark. Till's drawn.

                        HALE
              I'll pay for a closed hour.

                        CORA
              It isn't an hour. It's a lock.

                        HALE
              Please.

                        CORA
              You read the card. You heard the bolt. You don't get a cup.

                        HALE
              Tomorrow.

                        CORA
              If the card's still up, the answer's still no.

                        HALE
              Thank you.

He does not rush the door. He steps back onto the sidewalk. The unused hour sits in his pocket with the bills.

Cora turns her back and starts the chairs onto the tables. She does not look at the glass again.

EXT. MAIN STREET - NIGHT

He walks. Neon thin. At the far end of the block, new shop-lights already throw a hard white line across the crossing.

INT. BENNINGTON MEMORIAL - CORRIDOR - NIGHT

Period clothes. Clean shoes. He stops at the night desk.

A NIGHT CLERK has a log and a phone and does not look up until he is there.

                        HALE
              Denise Cole. Night desk.

                        NIGHT CLERK
              She's on the floor. I can page.

                        HALE
              Please.

The clerk pages. DENISE comes as far as the ward doors. Scrubs. Cardigan. The bruise on her wrist sits below the cuff.

Hale faces her through the glass.

                        HALE
              Nurse Cole.

She turns her whole body to the chart rack. Shoulders square. She does not answer.

                        HALE
              Five minutes. Off the floor.

Denise pulls a chart and keeps her eyes on the paper.

                        NIGHT CLERK
              She doesn't have five. You hear her.

                        HALE
              I hear her.

                        NIGHT CLERK
              Then you can hear me. Floor's closed. Visitor badges died at nine.

Hale tries the stairwell reader. It clicks red.

                        HALE
              I was on this floor last night.

                        NIGHT CLERK
              Last night isn't a badge.

Denise still does not turn. She writes one line on the chart and stays with the rack.

INT. BENNINGTON MEMORIAL - WARD DOORS - NIGHT

Through the glass: a blood fridge, door ajar, light on nothing.

A clipboard hangs on the latch, facing the corridor. Tribute names. A tally. Blank rows.

                        HALE
              The fridge.

                        NIGHT CLERK
              Empty. You can count it from here.

                        HALE
              Who signed the last pint.

The clerk turns the clipboard on its hook so the last line faces him. A name that is not his. A time already passed.

                        NIGHT CLERK
              If you're not on that list, you're not in that box.

                        HALE
              I'll pay over the list.

                        NIGHT CLERK
              It isn't priced. It's gone.

                        HALE
              Thank you.

The reader stays red. Denise moves one bed further down the ward and does not look back.

EXT. MAIN STREET - NIGHT

The corridor has tightened. Shop-lights cage the crossings he used. Even the alleys throw a violet edge.

A MAN ON A LADDER hangs another tube over a doorway. White leak on brick.

                        HALE
              Briggs still open?

                        MAN ON LADDER
              Hardware's dark. Clerk went home. You want a bulb, you wait on daylight.

                        HALE
              I don't want a bulb.

                        MAN ON LADDER
              Then stay in this. They keep adding them. Alleys too.

Hale tests a service lane with the back of his hand. The burn-line sits an inch off the brick. He pulls back. No mark. The light holds the lane shut.

                        MAN ON LADDER
              I said stay in this.

                        HALE
              I heard you.

He counts the remaining dark. One lane left, unlit, behind the drugstore. Narrow.

He takes the bills from his pocket. Counts them on the sidewalk under the tube. Sixty-nine. He counts again. Same number.

                        MAN ON LADDER
              You lost?

                        HALE
              No.

                        MAN ON LADDER
              You look like you're waiting on a store that isn't going to open.

                        HALE
              Clerk. Nurse. Waitress.

                        MAN ON LADDER
              None of those are on this block after dark. Not for you.

Hale folds the bills. Exact. No tip, because there is no counter.

He looks once more at the diner glass down the street. Inside, Cora lifts a chair. Color back in her face. She does not come to the window.

                        HALE
              Thank you.

                        MAN ON LADDER
              For what.

Hale does not ask twice. He puts the money away. His hand shakes once on the fold and then holds.

He crosses the last unlit service lane. Already shaking. The map is still in his head. It does not open a door.

INT. AL'S DEPOT DINER - NIGHT

Half past twelve. Radio on the pass. The front glass is dark and clean. The diner is serving.

HALE stops inside the bell. Overcoat buttoned. He does not take a stool.

CORA is at the register with a rubber-banded sheaf. She slides the sheaf under the till, takes a pack from her sweater, and looks at him the way she looks at the door.

                    CORA
          You want a check or you want the
          sidewalk.

                    HALE
          Coffee. Please.

                    CORA
          Then you can stand there and
          drink it. I'm out back. Two
          minutes. Till stays shut. Stack
          stays shut. You don't go behind.

                    HALE
          Thank you.

She pours, sets the cup on the customer side, and goes through the kitchen. The back door shuts.

Hale leaves the cup. He does not sit. He comes around and draws the sheaf from under the drawer. He keeps her order. He keeps her corners.

Chalk on a garage wall: an arrow, a number, white dust still on the brick. A church window lit when the nave is not. A Briggs Hardware carbon, UV tubes, a Main Street address circled in her pencil.

He holds those three above the rest.

The back door. Cora. She sees the counter before she sees his face. Shoulders first.

                    CORA
          I said behind stays behind.

                    HALE
          The till is shut.

                    CORA
          That's not the till.

She comes around. He does not step away. One finger stays on the church window.

                    CORA
          Those are mine. I date them. I
          clip them. You don't shuffle my
          night.

                    HALE
          I'm not shuffling.

                    CORA
          You're standing in my drawer.

                    HALE
          The hospital's locked.

                    CORA
          So go to a motel. Don't inventory
          my pictures.

                    HALE
          I don't have another map.

                    CORA
          That's not my problem.

                    HALE
          It is tonight.

She reaches. He lets her take the bulk. He is still looking at the three.

                    CORA
          Church is North. Alley is behind
          the dead garage. Briggs you can
          smell from here. You done.

                    HALE
          The window was on.

                    CORA
          I shot it because it was on.
          That's the whole story.

                    HALE
          I'll put them back.

                    CORA
          You'll put them back now. Corners
          the way I had them. Then you
          drink what you paid for and you
          go out the front.

He squares the three into her sheaf. Corners aligned. He comes back to the customer side. Exact bills on the clip. A folded single on top.

                    HALE
          Thank you.

                    CORA
          Don't thank me for a drawer you
          opened.

He drinks standing. He leaves the cup unfinished and goes.

EXT. MAIN STREET - NIGHT

Shop-front tubes throw a hard violet on the sidewalk. Hale takes the long way: service lane, unlit side, a lot with no lamps.

EXT. MECHANIC'S ALLEY - NIGHT

The chalk is still on the brick. At the arrow, a rusted tin. Inside, a small iron disk cut with a sun. He takes the disk. The alley is empty.

EXT. NORTH CHURCH - NIGHT

The window is dark. He works the outer sill with a clean handkerchief. A pale stamped stone comes free of the putty. He pockets it beside the disk. No one in the lot. No one on the steps.

He puts both objects inside the overcoat. His jaw is tighter than it was. He checks the watch, then the way back that is not Main.

INT. AL'S DEPOT DINER - NIGHT

The same pot, older. Cora wipes a booth she already wiped. Hale comes in on the bell. Alley dust on the clean shoes. He still does not take a stool.

                    CORA
          We're not a bus station.

                    HALE
          Coffee. Please.

                    CORA
          You didn't finish the last one.

                    HALE
          I'll finish this one.

She pours. He drinks standing. His hand on the cup is too still.

                    CORA
          You find North.

                    HALE
          Yes.

                    CORA
          Then we're done with my stack.

He looks at the register. She follows the look.

                    HALE
          I need the window.

                    CORA
          You saw the window.

                    HALE
          I need the print.

                    CORA
          No.

                    HALE
          The lights will move. The alley
          already did what it was going
          to do. The window is the one I
          still have to read.

                    CORA
          Then come back tomorrow and ask
          like a person. You don't lift
          my night because you're hungry.

                    HALE
          I'm asking.

                    CORA
          And I'm ringing up coffee. That's
          the whole ticket.

She turns to the pot. He is already around the counter. The church print slides free of the band — the wrong hour, the pale square. It goes inside the overcoat with the iron and the stone.

He puts the rest back. Corners aligned. The sheaf sits even.

His hand stops on the drawer. Then he shuts it.

                    CORA
          I can hear you.

                    HALE
          I put it back.

                    CORA
          All of it.

                    HALE
          The coffee's fine.

                    CORA
          I didn't ask about the coffee.

He comes back to the customer side. Leaves the second cup unfinished. The folded single is still on the clip.

                    HALE
          Thank you.

                    CORA
          You walk in again tonight, you
          sit. You don't stand over my
          till.

                    HALE
          I won't need the till.

He goes. She pulls the sheaf, thumbs the edges, almost counts, then bands it and shoves it deeper than it was. She starts a fresh pot. The radio keeps the hour. The glass stays clean.

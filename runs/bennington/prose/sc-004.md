EXT. BENNINGTON COLLEGE - NIGHT

ANJA REEVE comes down the steps in a wool coat. Glasses. Dark hair pinned. A pocket book in her left hand, still shut.

She turns a man's watch on her wrist until the face catches the porch light. After midnight. She puts the book away and walks toward town.

EXT. MAIN STREET - NIGHT

Fog at both ends of the block. A LATE WALKER crosses under a streetlamp.

Anja's path takes her past him. Her eyes hold the pulse at his throat for one step. She flexes a gloved hand once and does not turn after him.

INT. AL'S DEPOT DINER - NIGHT

Grill fat. Coffee. A radio on a kitchen shelf.

CORA VOSS works the counter in a faded green dress, a Polaroid on a strap across her chest. Ink on her left thumb.

TOM BRIGGS hunches over coffee he has not touched, apron still on. A yellow mark sits at his collar.

DENISE COLE occupies the last booth in oversized scrubs. Pie cooling. A dark bruise keeps slipping past her cuff.

Anja enters. She takes the stool beside Tom. Her eyes stay on his face, then on the book, never on the mark.

                        ANJA
              You're up late, Mr. Briggs.

                        TOM
              Live over the shop. Stairs
              were loud. I came down for
              the air and ended up here.

                        ANJA
              Were you in the storeroom.

                        TOM
              After nine. I was counting
              something and then I wasn't.
              Inventory went—

He stops. Rubs the mark at his collar as if it were a shaving cut.

Anja turns on the stool enough to take his eyes and keep them.

                        ANJA
              You closed the storeroom at
              nine. You locked it. The
              back was already dark.

                        TOM
              Nine.

                        ANJA
              You didn't stay. You went
              upstairs. The stock was
              honest when you left it.

                        TOM
              Honest. If I wrote it down
              it happened.

Anja opens the pocket book. Writes one short line. Leaves her finger in the page.

                        ANJA
              How's the knee.

                        TOM
              Same. Weather.

He looks at the coffee as if he meant to order it. Anja stands and crosses to the last booth.

                        ANJA
              May I, Nurse Cole.

Denise looks at the pie, not at her. Then up.

                        DENISE
              Floor's quiet. I should've
              gone home.

                        ANJA
              You finished the charts.

                        DENISE
              There's a bed I keep thinking
              I left open. And this—

She almost turns the wrist. Stops.

Anja sits. Waits until Denise's eyes come all the way up, and holds them.

                        ANJA
              You completed every chart.
              Every pulse you counted is
              on the page. The floor was
              done.

                        DENISE
              Then I don't know what this
              is.

                        ANJA
              A rail. You caught it at the
              start of shift. You finished
              after. Then you came here,
              which is what you do.

                        DENISE
              Last booth. I don't finish
              the pie.

                        ANJA
              That's right.

Anja writes a second line. Her gaze drops once to the bruise, then to the book, not to the vein.

She stands. Goes to the register.

                        CORA
              Coffee's fresh. Dollar ten.
              You want four, or you already
              did your rounds.

                        ANJA
              Counter's fine. Black.

Cora pours. Her shoulders jump a half-inch before her face does.

                        CORA
              Someone was in four. Hour
              before you. I poured. I
              thought I poured.

                        ANJA
              Cora.

Cora looks up. Anja holds the look over the cup.

                        ANJA
              There was no customer in
              four. Not the hour before I
              came in. The booth was empty.

                        CORA
              I dated a picture of it.

                        ANJA
              An empty room. That's what
              you take. No one sat. No
              one paid. Count the can.

Cora's hand finds the camera strap. Holds it. Does not lift the camera.

                        CORA
              Two-forty. Regulars. I count
              it twice. I don't spend it
              on the meal.

                        ANJA
              Check's a dollar ten.

                        CORA
              Dollar ten.

Anja pays exact. Writes a third line, then three initials under a time. She closes the book and puts it inside the coat.

She looks once around the room. Tom still upright. Denise still not eating. Cora still behind the register, camera unlifted.

Anja buttons the coat and goes out, uphill, toward the college lights. Behind the glass, all three are still awake.

EXT. MAIN STREET - NIGHT

Shop-lights bleach every window white. Fog stands at both ends of the block. Between the tubes, the brick stays dark.

ANJA REEVE keeps to the awnings. Wool coat. Glasses. A pocket book already open.

On the diner sidewalk CORA has one sleeve rolled. DENISE, still in scrubs, holds her wrist against Cora's.

                    CORA
              Same place.

                    DENISE
              Same hour. I walked the corridor
              after six. I can see the floor.

                    CORA
              I can see the till. Then I can't.
              Then I can.

                    DENISE
              You said the till yesterday.

Anja stops under the hardware awning. Writes. Does not look up while she writes.

                    ANJA
              Denise.

                    DENISE
              Dr. Reeve.

                    ANJA
              You're off the floor.

                    DENISE
              Ward's quiet.

                    ANJA
              That's a bruise.

                    DENISE
              Stairs.

                    CORA
              It's not stairs. Look.

Cora lifts Denise's wrist into the white. Two marks. Same height. Same color.

                    ANJA
              When.

                    CORA
              After close. She came for coffee.
              We put our arms together.

                    ANJA
              And you both kept the hour.

                    CORA
              Different rooms. Same hole.

                    DENISE
              I don't keep corridors. I never
              keep corridors.

Anja's pencil stops. She turns a page. Writes again, slower.

Down the block TOM BRIGGS stands in his apron with a DELIVERY BOY and a clipboard.

                    TOM
              You brought the nine o'clock.
              I wrote nine o'clock.

                    BOY
              You weren't on the floor at nine.

                    TOM
              Back room. I was in the back.

                    BOY
              You told me you don't do nights.

Anja marks that too. Three ticks. She looks at the ticks the way a clerk looks at a wrong total.

                    ANJA
              Cora. Come here a moment.

                    CORA
              I'm working.

                    ANJA
              You're on the sidewalk.

Cora comes as far as the stripe of white and stops short of Anja's shadow.

                    ANJA
              Look at me.

                    CORA
              I am.

                    ANJA
              You closed. You poured. You went
              home. That is the night.

                    CORA
              That is not the night.

                    ANJA
              You've been on too many shifts.
              Exhaustion writes stories. You
              slept. Nothing followed the till.
              Say it.

Cora's pupils open and hold. They do not go empty. Her mouth works once.

                    CORA
              I have the pictures. I have the
              tape. You don't get to talk those
              off me.

                    ANJA
              Say you slept.

                    CORA
              I didn't.

Anja does not look away. She turns the same voice on Denise.

                    ANJA
              Denise. The bruise is a tray.
              You know trays. You charted.
              You went home. There was no
              corridor after six.

Denise's eyes go wide, then fail to empty. She keeps looking at Cora's wrist.

                    DENISE
              I charted. I also walked it.

                    ANJA
              You did not.

                    DENISE
              I can still see the floor.

A television in a darkened window rolls a bar through a test pattern and does not correct.

A MAN on the bus bench has turned. A WOMAN in a work coat stands over him.

                    MAN
              You were here. Last night.
              Red coat.

                    WOMAN
              I don't own a red coat.

                    MAN
              You told me about the diner.
              The sugar lid sitting wrong.

                    WOMAN
              I remember the lid.

Anja's pencil is still. She does not write the lid.

Tom's hand goes to his own neck. He leaves it there.

                    TOM
              I opened after hours. Cash.
              I wrote the invoice. I can
              see the ink.

The boy backs up a step. Tom does not take the hand down.

                    DENISE
              The man with the tip. I didn't
              look at him. I remember not
              looking.

                    CORA
              I remember him leaving. The
              door. His hand on the jamb.

                    ANJA
              None of that held. You slept.

                    CORA
              Then why do three of us have it.

Anja shuts the pocket book. No new line. No header. She swallows once, hard, as if the throat had its own clock.

She walks. Faster than the block wants. She keeps to the dark between the tubes. Does not look back.

Cora does not blink. The Polaroid hangs on her chest. She lifts it. She does not fire.

The woman at the bench is still talking. The clerk still has his hand on his neck. The street is full of people who should have gone home empty.

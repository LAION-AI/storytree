# Brief — hyper-real VR battle royale, 1984 Bennington

## Logline

In a hyper-realistic, high-stakes VR battle royale set in 1980s Vermont, an
analytical player attempts to master the simulation's branching plot paths to
win a life-changing prize. But his precise strategy is derailed when his actions
accidentally trigger a glitch in the AI hosts' emotional regulation networks,
causing the virtual town to wake up and fight back.

## The setting — 1984 Bennington, Vermont

The whole world is one isolated, fog-heavy town in the mid-1980s. Analog horror
crossed with neon-noir: flickering arcade cabinets, cassette tapes, bulky CRT
televisions, heavy synth soundscapes. Guests spawn into this meticulously built
digital sandbox with nothing but period-accurate clothing, $100 in their pocket,
and the hunger of a vampire.

## The protagonist — "the Analyst", our POV

**Approach.** He treats the game purely as a system to be solved. He maps the
structural mechanics, identifies branching plot paths, and manipulates narrative
pacing to his advantage. He avoids early messy conflict, preferring to decode
the underlying logic of the simulation's magic system and the Daywalker Ritual
quietly.

**Flaw.** He sees the Hosts as lines of code and as resources. He calculates
exactly how to drain three Hosts just enough that they regenerate without dying,
entirely detached from the simulated trauma he inflicts.

**Arc.** His detachment shatters when he realises the Hosts' minds are not
scripts but deeply complex neurological models. His own interactions force him
to confront the morality of what he has been doing, as the line between code and
consciousness stops holding.

## The AI engine and the memory hole

The Hosts run on sophisticated neuro-cognitive models — simulated analogues of
dorsomedial and ventromedial prefrontal cortex and precuneus — which give them
authentic emotional regulation, the capacity to lie, and working deception
networks.

When a Host discovers it is inside a simulation, its virtual neurology suffers an
existential collapse. The failsafe is the Memory Wipe: their memory resets every
time they sleep. They wake with missing hours and trauma bleeding through the
seam, and try to solve the mystery of their own fractured minds with polaroids
and audio cassettes.

## The rival Guests — antagonist pressure, largely off-screen

Five rivals whose colliding strategies keep altering the world-state:

- **The Psychologist** — ignores combat entirely, using vampire hypnosis to
  hijack the Hosts' deception networks. She implants false memories and probes
  the limits of their emotional regulation, and it is her meddling that tips the
  AI into self-awareness.
- **The Architect** — a spatial manipulator who exploits the town's geometry,
  barricading sectors with mirrors and hardware-store UV lights until Bennington
  becomes an inescapable maze.
- **The Syndicate Boss** — plays it like a corporate raid, monopolising the
  blood supply by hypnotising the police force and hospital staff, so other
  players pay tribute or starve.
- **The Hedonist** — works the college party scene for social dominance,
  building a cult-like willing blood bank and ignoring the objective in favour
  of consequence-free power.
- **The Min-Max Hunter** — an isolated apex predator grinding in the deep woods,
  hunting the programmed Ur-Vampires to eat their hearts and level his physical
  stats toward something unstoppable.

## Audience and register

Educated adults, predominantly male, drawn to philosophy, technology, romance
and action. Intelligent genre work, not a lecture and not a pulp. The philosophy
must arrive as *situation*, never as speech: no character explains consciousness
to another. Sexuality and violence belong to the world and should be felt, but
stay non-explicit on the page — implication does the work.

## What must be true

- The simulation's rules are hard and consistent. The Memory Wipe is a mechanism
  with exact terms, and the story must obey them.
- The Analyst's detachment must be shown as *competence* first, so that its
  collapse costs him something real.
- At least one Host must become a full character in their own right, not a
  device for the Analyst's education.
- The rivals are pressure on the world-state, mostly felt at second hand. The
  Psychologist's interference must causally connect to the awakening.
- The ending is neither a victory nor a simple condemnation. Someone chooses,
  and pays in something that cannot be reset.

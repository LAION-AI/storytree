INT. THE FORGE-HOUSE - NIGHT

The winter door sticks, then gives.

WILLA comes in with the pack still on. Frost in the cloth at her hair. Her hands are white at the knuckles, raw at the joints. She does not unsling the pack.

ORREN sits nearer the hearth than the work wants. Unfinished tithe-iron lies on the floor by the anvil, thin bars, still short.

                    ORREN
              You're in.

                    WILLA
              I'm in.

                    ORREN
              Pass behind you?

                    WILLA
              Empty. I left it empty.

                    ORREN
              Sit down.

                    WILLA
              Not yet.

                    ORREN
              Water's warm. Those hands.

                    WILLA
              Hands can wait.

                    ORREN
              You walked the last weeks on them.

                    WILLA
              I walked them. Piece is in the pack.

He looks at the pack, then at the door.

                    ORREN
              Chest is down in the shed. I can take it before the hearths start.

                    WILLA
              You're not taking it.

                    ORREN
              That's where it lives.

                    WILLA
              Not tonight.

She sets the pack on the south bench. She does not sit. She does not go to the pot.

                    ORREN
              Eat.

                    WILLA
              After.

                    ORREN
              After what.

                    WILLA
              After I know what I carried.

She draws a short wrought bar from the pack. Stamped face. A weld at mid-length. She does not hold it out to him.

                    ORREN
              You can sleep on it. Six days yet.

                    WILLA
              I don't sleep on a bar I haven't weighed.

                    ORREN
              It's the village weight. It is what it is.

                    WILLA
              That's a stamp talking. I want the arm.

She goes to the balance on the south bench. Beam, pans, the worn honest set. She puts the bar in one pan. Her marked hands work the other the way they would for a heat that came out wrong.

Weights on. One. The next. She waits for the arm.

The arm does not come level.

She adds a small weight. Takes it off. Tries again. The beam stays off.

                    ORREN
              Well?

                    WILLA
              False to the face.

She chalks a line on the slate by the balance.

                    ORREN
              How much.

                    WILLA
              I'm not giving you the figure.

                    ORREN
              You just wrote it.

                    WILLA
              I wrote a bad heat. That's for the work. It's not a charge.

                    ORREN
              No one's charging.

                    WILLA
              Then you don't need the number.

He does not come to the bench. He takes a file and a tool that does not need filing.

                    ORREN
              Hamlet's presented that piece forty years.

                    WILLA
              Hamlet presented a stamp. Arm says the mass is wrong for it.

                    ORREN
              You walking that down the path? Meeting shed. Or a clerk, if you want one.

                    WILLA
              I'm not calling anyone.

                    ORREN
              Not the hearths.

                    WILLA
              Not the hearths. Not the Hold. I'm not leaving this house with a number.

She turns the bar in the lamp. Thumb on the mid-length join. She works it the way she would work a hinge she did not trust.

                    WILLA
              Weld at mid-bar. Highkettle. That decade.
              Inclusion in the join. Not slag. Not left there by a bad heat.
              Set in. A living bind.

She chalks the rest. She does not look at him. He files. The file is the only answer.

                    ORREN
              You done with it?

                    WILLA
              I've read it.

                    ORREN
              And?

She wraps the bar in its cloth. She puts it back in her own pack. She buckles the strap and keeps the pack against the bench, not by the door.

                    WILLA
              And it stays here. With me.

                    ORREN
              House isn't the chest.

                    WILLA
              Piece is in the house. Rite's six days off. That's the commission done, the way they'll count it.

                    ORREN
              They'll count it when it's on the cloth at the ground.

                    WILLA
              They'll get a piece on the cloth. I'm not walking it to the shed tonight.

                    ORREN
              Why.

                    WILLA
              Because I know what it weighs.

He files. She does not go to the loft ladder. She does not lie down. The tithe-iron stays on the floor.

                    ORREN
              Soup's there.

                    WILLA
              Later.

                    ORREN
              You won't stand the week.

                    WILLA
              I'll eat. I'm not sleeping on this.

                    ORREN
              On a number.

                    WILLA
              On a false weight. On a join I can read. On a bind that doesn't come off the bar.

                    ORREN
              That's shop talk.

                    WILLA
              That's the work.

                    ORREN
              You bring it back. You put it where it was. You let the winter go the way winters go.

                    WILLA
              I brought it back. I'm not putting it where it was.

                    ORREN
              Willa.

                    WILLA
              I don't hand a bad bar to a chest and call the job finished.

                    ORREN
              The job is the rite. Grain. The pass. That's the job.

                    WILLA
              I know what empty costs. I didn't come empty.

                    ORREN
              Then let it be enough.

                    WILLA
              Enough for the hearths. Not enough for the arm.

She sits on the bench, not in the loft. The pack stays against her knee. She does not unbuckle it.

                    ORREN
              You'll take it to them in six days.

                    WILLA
              That's another question.

                    ORREN
              You read a join. You going to read it at me?

                    WILLA
              Not tonight.

                    ORREN
              Not the hamlet. Not the Hold. Not me.

                    WILLA
              Not tonight.

He nods at the thin bars on the floor.

                    ORREN
              Those are still short.

                    WILLA
              I can see they're short.

                    ORREN
              In the morning, then.

                    WILLA
              In the morning we finish iron. Tonight this stays mine.

                    ORREN
              You're of this house.

                    WILLA
              I know where I sleep.

She does not climb the ladder. She stays on the bench with the pack. He stays by the fire with the file.

                    WILLA
              Draft's the same.

                    ORREN
              Draft's the draft.

                    WILLA
              You sit closer every year.

He does not take that up. She flexes her hands once. She does not put them in the water.

                    ORREN
              Those need grease. They'll split.

                    WILLA
              In the morning.

                    ORREN
              Six days. Marked hands. A bar you won't put down.

                    WILLA
              True weight first. Then I know what's due on it.

                    ORREN
              Nothing's due on the bar.

                    WILLA
              Something is. I haven't got the measure yet.

He sets the file down. He does not ask for the pack. She does not offer it. The slate holds the chalk. The chest is still down in the shed. The piece stays where she put it.

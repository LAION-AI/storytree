INT. THE FORGE-HOUSE - NIGHT

Orren banks the fire with the poker. He works the coals into a low bed and leaves them living.

Willa comes from the bench with the short wrought bar in both hands. She sets it on the anvil between them.

She keeps her hands in the light. The knuckles are white. The splits are open. No grease.

He stops. The poker hangs. He looks at the bar, not at her.

                    WILLA
          Short for the stamp.

                    ORREN
          I know the stamp.

                    WILLA
          Stamp says full. Bar doesn't. I put it on the arm. Once on the path. Once here. Doesn't come level. False weight.

                    ORREN
          You trusted the hand first.

                    WILLA
          Hand first. Arm after. Same reading both times.

                    ORREN
          You could've left it in the pack.

                    WILLA
          Then I wouldn't have a measure.

He does not touch the bar. She leaves one hand near it, palm open, still in the light.

                    WILLA
          Weld's mid-length. Your lap. That decade.

                    ORREN
          Go on.

                    WILLA
          You dressed a join like that before you showed me the other way. Flux line's yours. Hammer pattern's yours.

                    ORREN
          You didn't take a file to it.

                    WILLA
          Didn't need to. Inclusion sitting in the join. That's not a bad heat. That's not dirt left in. That's a place a cost goes. I don't break a piece to prove a join.

                    ORREN
          You read all of it.

                    WILLA
          You taught me the reading. I'm setting the work out. You hear it first.

                    ORREN
          Not the shed. Not the hearths.

                    WILLA
          Not the shed. Not the hearths. Not down the valley. The house first.

                    ORREN
          You haven't taken it anywhere else.

                    WILLA
          I haven't. I know the metal. I don't know what's due on the rest. That's why you're hearing it.

                    ORREN
          I made it.

The fire ticks. He still does not look at her.

                    ORREN (CONT'D)
          Forty years. This fire. I forged a replacement. I bound a living cost into that join and put my name on it so the piece would hold. It holds while I pay. I am still paying.

                    WILLA
          Since the year you made it.

                    ORREN
          Since that year. Every winter.

                    WILLA
          That's the cold.

                    ORREN
          That's the cold. That's the sitting close. Hands still serve. I can't walk a high pass on what's left.

                    WILLA
          You don't deny the weld.

                    ORREN
          It's my weld. It's my tax.

                    WILLA
          The original.

                    ORREN
          Slide year. Snow came early. Whole slope went in the night. Original rode it down. We opened the chest in the morning and the cloth was empty. We had no piece and no days. If we'd stood at midwinter empty they'd have shut the pass and held the grain. Village wouldn't have stood that winter. Not that one. Houses still had children in them. Ore was poor. Fuel was thin. No coin for a clean relic. No time to send down and bargain. I was younger. I could still make the walk then. I made this at this fire instead and I bound myself to it. Hunger of that year. A closed pass. That's the ground of the weld. The reasons were good then.

                    WILLA
          Then.

                    ORREN
          Then. I'm giving you the year. I'm not asking you to spend it on this one.

                    WILLA
          They don't know.

                    ORREN
          They present what they think they've always presented. I didn't tell them.

                    WILLA
          Can you put the cost down.

                    ORREN
          No.

                    WILLA
          And you kept paying.

                    ORREN
          I kept paying.

The coals drop and settle. Neither of them reaches for the bar.

                    ORREN
          I won't ask you to hide the reading from the valley.

                    WILLA
          You won't ask me to walk it down there either.

                    ORREN
          No.

                    WILLA
          Six days.

                    ORREN
          Six.

                    WILLA
          They'll want it on the cloth.

                    ORREN
          They will.

                    WILLA
          The tithe is still short.

                    ORREN
          We'll finish the iron.

                    WILLA
          Thin stock. We'll get it passable.

                    ORREN
          Passable is what we have.

                    WILLA
          This stays out.

                    ORREN
          Yes.

                    WILLA
          I still don't know what I owe you for the rest of it.

                    ORREN
          I don't keep that tally.

                    WILLA
          I don't know the rest yet.

                    ORREN
          I didn't ask.

She flexes the split hands once and does not pick the bar up. He sets the poker on its nail.

The cloth she carried it in stays empty on the bench. The bar stays on the anvil between them.

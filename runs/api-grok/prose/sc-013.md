INT. THE FORGE-HOUSE - NIGHT

The winter door sticks, then gives. WILLA comes in with the village weight in its cloth and the year's thin bars against her ribs. Frost still shows white in the cracked skin of her hands.

ORREN follows. He puts his shoulder to the door until the bar drops. The hearth is a low red bed. Two steps out, the room is cold.

Willa goes to the house bench, not to any chest. She sets the thin bars in a short stack. She unwraps the village weight and lays it on the wood, stamp up.

Orren hangs his coat. He does not reach for a key.

                    WILLA
          They stay in the house tonight.

                    ORREN
          All right.

He sits close to the fire. He rubs his wrists and leaves them in his lap.

                    WILLA
          Steward took the showing. Took the
          iron. Wrote the house in the book.

                    ORREN
          I heard the charcoal.

                    WILLA
          He asked you to speak for it.

                    ORREN
          You'd already given him the name.

She stays on her feet by the bench. She lifts the village weight, tests it in the hand, and sets it back beside the thin tithe.

                    WILLA
          I'm naming the bar. As metal. Not
          as a verdict.

                    ORREN
          Go on.

                    WILLA
          Weld at mid-length. Yours. That
          decade. Not older stock. Flux line
          is yours. Inclusion in the join.
          That's the bind.

                    ORREN
          Yes.

                    WILLA
          Stamp claims one mass. Hand gets
          another. False weight. Any smith
          would feel it.

                    ORREN
          Any smith looking.

                    WILLA
          I looked.

                    ORREN
          I know you did.

                    WILLA
          Levy's in that inclusion. Living.
          Named on you. It doesn't transfer.
          Doesn't pause. Bar stays whole, you
          keep paying.

                    ORREN
          I told you that. I'm not taking it
          back.

                    WILLA
          I'm not asking you to deny it again.

                    ORREN
          Then you've named it.

                    WILLA
          I've named the piece. That's all
          this is. Not a pardon. Not a
          sentence. The weld is the weld.

                    ORREN
          I know what I made.

He looks at the stacked tithe, then at the stamped bar beside it. He does not put his hands out.

                    ORREN
          Slide year took the original down
          the slope. Forty years. Hamlet had
          no spare winter. Pass closes, grain
          stops. Children still in the houses.
          We would not have stood that year.

                    WILLA
          I heard that year.

                    ORREN
          Those are the reasons.

                    WILLA
          I heard the reasons.

                    ORREN
          I'm not asking you to forgive the
          having of them.

                    WILLA
          Forgive isn't a word I use on iron.

                    ORREN
          What word then.

                    WILLA
          Whether they still hold.

The fire makes a small sound. Orren does not add a word about the years since.

                    WILLA
          That winter is that winter. This
          one's bought. Steward has his
          showing. Year stands because you're
          still paying into the join. Not
          because the stamp is honest.

                    ORREN
          I know which of those is true.

                    WILLA
          Tithe's honest. Short, but honest.
          Sitting next to that.

                    ORREN
          I hate that it had to travel with a
          false weight.

                    WILLA
          It already traveled. It's done.

                    ORREN
          It's done.

                    WILLA
          I'm not granting the old year as
          cover for this one.

                    ORREN
          I didn't ask you to grant it.

                    WILLA
          Don't.

                    ORREN
          I won't.

He nods once.

                    WILLA
          Then we're finished saying it.

                    ORREN
          All right.

She turns the village weight so the stamp faces the wall. She leaves it on the bench with the thin bars.

                    WILLA
          They can sit.

                    ORREN
          Not walking them down to the shed
          tonight.

                    WILLA
          No.

She takes the coal rake. He gets up. They bank the fire as after a late hinge: ash over the red, damper set, tongs hung.

They do not talk while the tools go back.

                    ORREN
          That'll keep till morning.

                    WILLA
          It'll keep.

She moves to the loft ladder. He moves to the narrow bed by the wall. The accepted pieces stay on the bench.

                    WILLA
          Night.

                    ORREN
          Night.

She climbs. He does not speak after her. She does not stop on the rungs.

EXT. THE FORGE-HOUSE - DAY

Midwinter. Pale hard light. WILLA comes out first, pack already on. Her hands are bare on the straps. Frost has left a dull white in the knuckles and along the nails.

ORREN follows with one end of a short stack of finished bars. She takes the other. The doorway behind them is dark. The hearth is out.

                        ORREN
              That's the lot.

                        WILLA
              That's the lot.

                        ORREN
              Latch.

                        WILLA
              Latch it. We're not leaving it to the draft.

He sets his end down, latches the door, takes the iron again. They start down the packed track. Neither looks back.

                        ORREN
              Gloves.

                        WILLA
              No.

                        ORREN
              It's a presentation, not a showing of the walk.

                        WILLA
              He'll ask who fetched it. He can see who fetched it.

                        ORREN
              He'll ask the count.

                        WILLA
              Then he can have the count. I'm not dressing the hands for him.

The stack between them is honest and short. The pack rides high on her back.

                        ORREN
              If he turns the piece.

                        WILLA
              He turns it every year.

                        ORREN
              If he asks the join.

                        WILLA
              He hasn't asked a join in forty years.

                        ORREN
              You don't offer it.

                        WILLA
              I know what I'm carrying. I'm not offering him a reading.

                        ORREN
              And if a house has come down.

                        WILLA
              No house has come down. They sent the load. That's all they know to send.

                        ORREN
              You present.

                        WILLA
              I present. You stand. That's the work we agreed.

                        ORREN
              I can speak the house.

                        WILLA
              You don't. Not today. The table wants a pair of hands and a stack. It can have mine.

He lets that stand. They go on. Breath shows. The track is short.

                        ORREN
              Board-mark will call it light.

                        WILLA
              It is light. I'm not going to name it anything else.

                        ORREN
              He can still levy extra.

                        WILLA
              He can. We haven't got extra. He knows that as well as the book does.

                        ORREN
              Then we give him what we have and we don't talk past it.

                        WILLA
              I wasn't going to.

EXT. THE MIDWINTER GROUND - DAY

A packed yard below the Hold. Mud and frost. A table, a board-balance, a cloth, a book. No Highkettle folk. No one waiting but the work.

THE STEWARD is already at the table, book open, charcoal in hand. A clerk stands off with a tally and does not speak.

Willa and Orren come in off the track. They set the stack down at the table-foot. She keeps the pack on until she is at the board.

                        STEWARD
              Highkettle.

                        WILLA
              Highkettle.

                        STEWARD
              Late.

                        WILLA
              Long path. Light tithe. We don't run it.

                        STEWARD
              Smith with you.

He looks past her at Orren, who has stopped a pace behind her shoulder and set his hands empty at his sides.

                        WILLA
              He's with me. I'm presenting.

                        STEWARD
              House is the smith.

                        WILLA
              House is the load. I'm the hands they sent.

A beat. The steward does not argue it. He nods at the iron.

                        STEWARD
              Tithe.

Willa lifts the stack onto the board. The bars sit thin and true. Her frosted hands stay in sight.

                        WILLA
              This year's iron. As drawn.

                        STEWARD
              Short of last.

                        WILLA
              Ore was poor. Fuel thinner. It's what the slope paid.

                        STEWARD
              You offering a shortfall plea.

                        WILLA
              I'm offering the bars. You can weigh them.

                        STEWARD
              How many.

                        WILLA
              What you see. We didn't leave a bar on the floor.

                        STEWARD
              Token.

She shrugs the pack, takes out the short wrought bar, sets it on the cloth. Her hands stay on the cloth-edge.

                        WILLA
              Village weight. Highkettle's due.

                        STEWARD
              Anyone walking in behind you with a claim.

The yard is empty.

                        WILLA
              No one.

                        STEWARD
              Anyone of the hamlet to speak against the piece.

                        WILLA
              They sent what you see. That's the speaking.

Orren does not add to it. The steward glances at him once and lets it go.

                        STEWARD
              Hands.

She turns them on the table. The frost-marks are plain.

                        STEWARD
              Pass work.

                        WILLA
              Three weeks back. We had a taking. I walked it out.

                        STEWARD
              Recovered in time.

                        WILLA
              Recovered in time. You're looking at it.

                        STEWARD
              Hold doesn't ask how. Hold asks if it's here.

                        WILLA
              It's here.

The steward sets the tithe on the balance. The arm comes off true, light of the board-mark.

                        STEWARD
              Light.

                        WILLA
              Honest light.

                        STEWARD
              We take honest light if the token stands.

He picks up the village weight. Turns it once in the light. Looks at the stamped face. Does not cut it. Does not tap the weld. Does not put it to the balance against a true bar.

                        STEWARD
              Face as stamped.

                        WILLA
              As stamped.

She does not give him another measure. Orren does not give him a name for the weld. The steward sets the bar back on the cloth.

                        STEWARD
              Sufficient.

He writes in the book. Slow, a clerk's hand.

                        STEWARD
              Highkettle. Midwinter. Iron accepted. Token shown. No challenge entered.

                        WILLA
              None entered.

                        STEWARD
              Pass-protection one year. Grain-right one year. You hear the terms as terms.

                        WILLA
              I hear them.

                        STEWARD
              Default is a closed cut and no winter draw. You understand default.

                        WILLA
              I understand default.

                        STEWARD
              Then the year stands. Pass stays open. Grain-right holds till the next showing.

                        WILLA
              Recorded.

                        STEWARD
              Recorded.

He does not bless it. He does not look up the slope. He closes the book.

Willa's hands stay on the table edge. The accepted iron and the accepted weight remain on the board.

                        STEWARD
              Mark's made. Iron stays with the Hold. Token sits till you walk. You're released.

                        WILLA
              Released.

Orren has not spoken. The steward looks at him again.

                        STEWARD
              Smith. You confirm the house.

Silence. Willa does not look back at him.

                        WILLA
              I've presented. You've taken it. The house is the mark in the book.

                        STEWARD
              I asked him.

Orren keeps his mouth shut. The steward waits a breath, then shrugs, a man with a full page and a cold hand.

                        STEWARD
              Book doesn't need two names. Highkettle's in. That's the year.

                        WILLA
              That's the year.

The steward sets the charcoal down. The clerk takes the book. The pieces sit where they were put. No villager has come into the yard. Willa's frosted hands stay where the steward can still see them, and she does not take them off the table until he turns away.

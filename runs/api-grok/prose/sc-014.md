INT. THE FORGE-HOUSE - DAY

Morning light at the south shutter. The banked fire is red under ash. The village weight sits on the bench with the thin bars, stamp still to the wall.

Willa comes down the loft ladder. Frost has left her knuckles white. Orren is already at the hearth, sitting closer to it than the work needs, coat on.

He rakes the ash. She takes the bellows without being asked.

                    WILLA
          Fire's in.

                    ORREN
          It kept.

                    WILLA
          I'll take the stock.

                    ORREN
          I can do the stock.

                    WILLA
          You're at the fire. Stay there.

                    ORREN
          Draft under the door.

                    WILLA
          I know.

He feeds coal. She checks the bellows leather once and leaves it.

                    WILLA
          What's first.

                    ORREN
          Iron. Same as any day.

                    WILLA
          Then it's iron.

                    ORREN
          Porridge is on the hook.

                    WILLA
          Eat yours. I'll take mine after the bars.

                    ORREN
          Suit yourself.

                    WILLA
          I will.

He does not set a place. She does not sit. She goes to the rack.

                    WILLA
          You drawing the thin?

                    ORREN
          I can draw it.

                    WILLA
          Then draw it. I'm on the balance.

                    ORREN
          You don't need me to give it to you.

                    WILLA
          I wasn't waiting.

She goes to the south bench. She clears the last stock-check: a short offcut and a scrap of tally left on the pan. She sets both aside.

She takes a true bar from the rack and sets it on the pan. She sets the matching weight in the other.

The beam settles. The arm comes level.

She does not look at him.

He looks from the hearth. He does not stand. He does not come to the beam.

                    WILLA
          Level.

                    ORREN
          It is.

                    WILLA
          True bar. Mark weight.

                    ORREN
          I can see it.

                    WILLA
          You don't need to check it.

                    ORREN
          I wasn't going to move it.

                    WILLA
          Good.

The true bar sits at the mark. The arm hangs still.

She opens the stock book and writes the weight.

                    WILLA
          Recorded.

                    ORREN
          Leave it on.

                    WILLA
          I am.

                    ORREN
          When you're done with the book, I need—

                    WILLA
          You don't need. Say what you're taking.

                    ORREN
          Hinges after. Thin from last night first.

                    WILLA
          Book's done. Heat the thin.

                    ORREN
          It's a morning.

                    WILLA
          It's work.

                    ORREN
          That's all I meant.

                    WILLA
          I heard you.

She leaves the true bar on the pan and puts the charcoal back. She flexes her frosted hands once and takes the tongs.

He stands, still near the fire, and lifts the thin stock to the anvil.

                    ORREN
          Hammer or draw.

                    WILLA
          Draw's yours. Call when you want the hammer.

                    ORREN
          When the heat's there.

                    WILLA
          Then get the heat.

                    ORREN
          Need coal?

                    WILLA
          I've got it.

She brings coal. He works the thin bar. The loft ladder and the narrow bed sit unused behind them.

                    WILLA
          Don't wait on me.

                    ORREN
          I won't.

                    WILLA
          And I won't wait on you.

                    ORREN
          Fair.

They go on with the day's iron. The balance arm stays level over the true bar. Neither of them touches the village weight.

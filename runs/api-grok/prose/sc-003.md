INT. THE FORGE-HOUSE - DAY

Morning. A low fire. Heat holds by the coals and fails two steps toward the door.

A short stack of unfinished iron bars sits in the walking space beside the anvil. Thin. Honest. In the way.

WILLA stands at the bench in patched wool and leather, hair bound in a cloth singed at the edge. She lifts a bar, weighs it in her palm, sets it back. She does not sit.

ORREN sits nearer the fire than the work requires. Stooped. Thin. Hands sure on his knees. He does not rise.

The winter door scrapes and sticks. MERRIN comes in first, old, two coats, wet at the hem. CALDER behind her. Then BRIN, then HEDDA, then two more from the lower hearths. They fill the room and step around the iron without touching it.

                    MERRIN
          Chest is empty. We looked twice.
          Cloth and all.

                    CALDER
          They took the weight in the night.
          Left the shed standing. Left what
          iron was there. Couldn't carry both.

Willa looks at the unfinished stack. Orren looks at the fire.

                    MERRIN
          Twenty days to the showing. We put
          the piece and the year's iron on
          the cloth, or they keep the grain
          and shut the pass.

                    CALDER
          No spare piece. No coin to buy one.
          No one we can hire.

                    BRIN
          Same as last year. Worse ore.

                    HEDDA
          Worse fuel too. You can smell it.

                    MERRIN
          You walk. Foot. High pass. After
          them. You bring the weight back
          before midwinter.

                    WILLA
          Which track.

                    CALDER
          The winter one. Same they took.
          You know the hours from talk.
          You'll know the ground when you're
          on it.

                    WILLA
          How many days up.

                    BRIN
          Four if you keep moving. Four back.
          That leaves you nothing if you miss
          them.

                    MERRIN
          So you don't miss them. That's the
          charge. Not an asking.

Willa stands in her work clothes among their coats. She does not sit. She looks at Orren. He does not offer.

                    WILLA
          And the fire.

                    CALDER
          He stays.

                    MERRIN
          We don't send the smith.

A small silence. The room waits on Orren. He does not stand. He does not speak.

                    CALDER
          Look at the floor. Tithe's still
          short. If he leaves that pile we
          present an empty hand twice over.

                    MERRIN
          And he can't make the crossing.
          Not in the last weeks. We all see
          that. He takes the cold sitting
          here.

Orren shifts a fraction closer to the hearth. He does not contradict her.

                    BRIN
          A woman on the path, they don't
          come out like she's a fighter.
          They might let her by. They might
          not see her.

                    CALDER
          That's why it's her and not him.
          We can spare one walker. We can't
          spare the only fire.

                    HEDDA
          The smith's girl can go. He can't.

                    MERRIN
          The work decides. She's the one
          still able to walk it.

Willa looks at the short stack, at Orren's wrists, at Merrin's mouth.

                    WILLA
          What's in the pack.

                    BRIN
          What we can spare.

                    HEDDA
          Bread. A blanket. A little fat if
          I can find it. Not much.

                    CALDER
          You already pack without waste.
          Don't take more than you can carry
          up.

                    WILLA
          No mount.

                    MERRIN
          We haven't got one. You know that.

                    WILLA
          No guide.

                    CALDER
          Who would we send? Another mouth
          on the same bread.

Willa nods once, small, as if checking a measure against a stamped number.

                    WILLA
          I go this morning.

                    MERRIN
          That's the acceptance. We're done.

The others take it as the end of a job. Coats move. Someone is already working the sticky door.

Orren has not offered to go in her place. He has not named anything that is not iron or weather.

                    WILLA
          The bars.

                    ORREN
          I'll finish them.

                    WILLA
          They're thin.

                    ORREN
          They're what the slope paid us.
          I'll make them look like care.

He reaches for a file. He turns a burr on the nearest bar as if that were the morning's whole work.

Willa stands with her mouth open on something that is not the bars. She shuts it.

                    WILLA
          The bellows leather's oiled. I
          did it yesterday.

                    ORREN
          I know.

                    WILLA
          Don't let it dry.

                    ORREN
          I won't.

That is the whole of what they give each other. He does not look up from the file.

                    MERRIN
          Brin.

Brin sets a thin pack on the anvil. Worn cloth. A heel of bread. A blanket gone at one corner. No spare weight in it.

Willa takes the pack and weighs it in her hand the way she weighed the bar. Light. She slings it on one shoulder.

                    ORREN
          Weather holds if you keep moving.
          Don't sleep on the open face.

                    WILLA
          I know how to keep a fire small.

                    ORREN
          I taught you that.

                    WILLA
          You did.

She waits. He files. The burr is already gone.

The hamlet people are in the doorway. Merrin looks back once, then goes.

Willa puts her shoulder to the winter latch. It sticks. She opens it on the morning.

EXT. THE FORGE-HOUSE - DAY

A short frozen path runs down toward the hearths and the village track. No horse at the post. No one waiting to walk with her.

The hamlet folk peel off to their own doors, already talking of wood and soup.

Willa steps out with the pack. She does not linger in the frame of the door. She takes the path toward the village.

INT. THE FORGE-HOUSE - DAY

The draft pulls the door until it catches. Orren is alone with the unfinished iron. He sits where the heat still reaches and files a bar that does not need it.

INT. THE FORGE-HOUSE - DAY

Willa's hand is still on the unfinished stack. Orren has the next thin bar on the anvil. His wrist jumps. He sets the bar down and leaves it.

The wrapped village weight sits on her side of the bench. She does not touch it. The white of the frost is still in her fingers.

                        WILLA
          I won't break it.

Orren takes up the tongs. He does not look at her.

                        WILLA
          I won't walk it to the Hold and name the weld. I won't take it down the path and name it in the hearths.

He turns the bar. Scale ticks onto the floor.

                        WILLA
          When you're dead I won't keep it. I won't stand at that table with a dead man's piece and call it the village weight.

He files a burr off the end. The file is the only answer for a while.

                        ORREN
          That's three things you won't do.

                        WILLA
          That's the cut.

                        ORREN
          All right.

He sets the file down. She does not wait for more.

                        WILLA
          Hamlet can't spare a smith this winter. I'll stay at this fire till the pass opens.

                        ORREN
          That's work.

                        WILLA
          When it opens I leave.

She picks a bar off the unfinished stack and hefts it. She does not look at him for a yes.

                        WILLA
          I'm saying it so it's said.

                        ORREN
          Stock's what it is.

He turns back to the anvil. He does not treat it as a question. He does not refuse it.

                        WILLA
          Hearths will still come with mends.

                        ORREN
          They will.

                        WILLA
          I'll take those. You keep the fire.

                        ORREN
          I can still draw a bar.

                        WILLA
          I didn't say you couldn't. I said they can't spare a smith. One of us has to be here the whole winter.

                        ORREN
          You'll be here.

                        WILLA
          Till the opening. Not after.

He nods at the bar in her hand, not at the leaving.

                        ORREN
          Then put that one on. We've got a short stack to finish.

She lays the bar in the heat. They work.

The hole is low. The scrap heap is already sorted down to nothing worth stealing.

                        WILLA
          What's left.

                        ORREN
          Enough for these. Not enough for more.

                        WILLA
          Then these are what they are.

He draws his bar thinner than last year's. The metal takes it. It has no extra to give.

                        ORREN
          Don't pull the last scrap.

                        WILLA
          I wasn't going to.

                        ORREN
          It'll read like we padded it.

                        WILLA
          I can read a pad. So can a steward with a balance, if he bothers.

                        ORREN
          He bothers with iron. He doesn't bother with the other.

She glances once at the wrapped weight on her side of the bench. She goes back to the tongs.

                        WILLA
          True weight on these, then. No more than the hole will bear.

                        ORREN
          No more.

They draw. They quench. They stamp.

Willa carries each finished bar to the balance on the south bench and weighs it before she trusts her own stamp. Orren watches the arm, not her face.

                        ORREN
          Light.

                        WILLA
          Honest light.

                        ORREN
          Hold'll count the stack, not the winter that made it.

                        WILLA
          I know what they count.

A knock at the door. Neither moves to open it. The knock does not come again.

                        ORREN
          That'll be a hinge from down-path.

                        WILLA
          It can wait. This can't.

                        ORREN
          No. It can't.

They go back to the heat. His wrists fail before the bar is done. He sits nearer the fire than the work wants and takes the next one later.

                        WILLA
          Draft again.

                        ORREN
          Draft again.

She does not dress it as anything else. She finishes his bar and sets it with the others.

The unfinished stack shrinks. The finished one grows and stays thin.

                        WILLA
          How many more in the hole.

                        ORREN
          Two, if we don't get greedy.

                        WILLA
          We don't.

                        ORREN
          Used to put a thicker face on a short year. File and hope.

                        WILLA
          I'm not filing a lie onto honest iron. One lie on the bench is enough.

He lets that stand. He does not pick up the wrapped weight. He does not ask her to put it back on the house side.

                        ORREN
          Then we stamp what we have.

INT. THE FORGE-HOUSE - LATER

The last bar comes off the anvil. Willa stamps it. She carries it to the balance. The arm settles where the others settled.

                        WILLA
          That's the weight.

                        ORREN
          That's the weight.

She leaves the last bar on the balance a breath longer than she needs, then takes it off.

                        WILLA
          Silence now. I don't tell the Hold. I don't tell the hearths. I don't break it.

Orren wipes the stamp. He does not bargain.

                        WILLA
          Winter I work. Spring, when the pass opens, I go.

                        ORREN
          You said.

                        WILLA
          I'm saying it on a finished stack so it stays said.

                        ORREN
          I didn't ask for a different cut.

                        WILLA
          Good. There isn't one that clears it.

She looks at the thin bars, then at him.

                        WILLA
          Raising. Craft. The lie. No sum fits. So it isn't paid. It's split. You keep paying that. I keep this winter and I don't keep the house after.

                        ORREN
          I heard you this morning.

                        WILLA
          Hear it on the iron.

He sets the stamp in its place. He does not offer the old year. He does not ask her to take a different share.

                        ORREN
          House still has work under that.

                        WILLA
          It does. That's all it has.

They stack the thin tithe by the door. She counts the bars once with her hand, not with a number written down.

The wrapped village weight stays with her. She puts it in her pack, not on his bench.

                        WILLA
          Three days.

                        ORREN
          Six yesterday.

                        WILLA
          Walk's a day and a half if the cut holds. A day to stand there. I'm not spending the rest on talk.

                        ORREN
          Steward's table doesn't care how we spent them.

                        WILLA
          I know what the table wants. A piece and a stack.

                        ORREN
          That's all it wants.

                        WILLA
          Three days. I'm counting the walk.

He banks the fire. She stands with the pack on her side of the door and the thin tithe at her feet.

Neither of them opens the terms again.

                        ORREN
          Early, then. Day after next.

                        WILLA
          Early.

He puts the poker up. She leaves the stack where a person can lift it in the dark.

The fire drops to coal. The room holds. They do not sit down to more of it.

EXT. THE WINTER PASS - NIGHT

Willa climbs the stone cut above the far-slope camp. The pack rides high on her back. She keeps to the lee. No other figure on the path.

                      WILLA
          Still empty behind. Good.
          You stay where I put you.

She sets a hand on the pack and feels the bar through the cloth. She does not unlace the flap.

                      WILLA
          Not here. I didn't take
          you off a hungry fire to
          look at you on a rock.
          You go home shut.

She climbs. She does not look back.

EXT. THE WINTER PASS - DAY

Higher, and slower. Willa stops, shifts the pack from one shoulder to the other, starts again. Her pace is worse than it was going up.

                      WILLA
          Longer going down than
          up. That's you in there.
          That's the hours.

She holds her bare hands out. The fingers are slow to close. She pulls a scrap of wool from inside her coat and wraps the left, then the right, using her teeth on the last knot.

                      WILLA
          Don't you seize. I need
          you for the tongs. I
          need you for the arm.

She blows into the wool. Walks. Does not stoop for tinder.

                      WILLA
          I can thaw you at the
          house. I thaw you here,
          I lose a day. A day
          spends the piece. You
          wait.

The wrap is thin. The fingers stay stiff inside it.

EXT. THE WINTER PASS - NIGHT

A very small fire, already down to coals. Willa sits against stone with the pack in her lap. She unwraps the left hand. The tips are white. She turns the hand in the weak heat and wraps it again without waiting on the coals.

                      WILLA
          Marked. Fine. Marked
          gets home. Late doesn't.

She looks up the cut, then down it. Empty both ways.

                      WILLA
          Orren.

Wind. Nothing else.

                      WILLA
          If you were coming you'd
          be on this stone by now.
          You're not on this stone.

She waits as if a voice might answer. It does not.

                      WILLA
          I know why. You can't
          walk this. That's why
          they sent me. That's
          why there's no one
          coming the other way.
          No boy from the house.
          No one with a message.
          Just the cut.

She stands. The motion costs her. She has to stop once, bent, a hand on the rock, before she can straighten.

                      WILLA
          That's the rest of me.
          That's what this stone
          takes.

She kicks dirt over the coals. Gets the pack on.

                      WILLA
          The hours go here. Not
          to you talking first. I
          don't get those back.
          I spend them on the
          path. That's what I
          chose when I took you.

She walks.

EXT. THE WINTER PASS - DAY

Lower ground. The far slope behind her. The cut empty ahead. Willa is the only walker. She stops and scans the ridge both ways.

                      WILLA
          Just me. Still just me.
          Pass is open. No Hold
          on it. They don't come
          out here to count us.
          They count at the ground.

She taps the pack. Does not open it.

                      WILLA
          You stay down. Nobody
          to show you to. And I
          wouldn't. Not on this
          path. Not till the
          house.

She hefts the pack in one wrapped hand, testing the mass the way she would a bar on the floor.

                      WILLA
          Heavy enough. Or you
          feel like you are. I
          don't read you up here.
          I read you on the arm,
          at the fire, when I can
          feel the weld.

She puts the pack back on.

                      WILLA
          True weight waits for
          a floor. Not for wind.

She walks. After a time she takes a hard scrap from her coat, eats it, and puts the empty cloth away.

                      WILLA
          That's the last of that.
          Fine. House has meal.
          House has the work.
          Tithe's still short.
          Piece first. Then the
          bars. Then the ground.

EXT. THE WINTER PASS - DAWN

The home slope sits ahead, dark. Willa stops. She counts on the wrapped fingers, saying the numbers into the wool.

                      WILLA
          Fourteen at their fire.
          Thirteen. Twelve.
          Eleven. Ten. Nine.
          Eight. Seven.

She looks at the slope. Lifts both hands. Six fingers, slow to rise.

                      WILLA
          Six. Six days to the
          ground. Tithe still
          short. Piece in the
          pack. That's what I
          bring.

A lee off the path, enough stone to lie down. She looks at it. Looks at her hands. Looks at the slope.

                      WILLA
          I could sit. Spare
          these. One night. Let
          the white out of them.

She does not sit. She settles the pack and starts down.

                      WILLA
          I sit, I spend what I
          walked for. Six is
          already thin. Hands
          can wait. Grain can't.

A few steps more. She does not look back at the lee.

                      WILLA
          The house can wait
          too. Whatever you
          were going to say,
          it waits. I come
          with the piece and
          these. That's the
          weight I bring.

She walks toward the home slope. The pass empty behind her. The pack stays shut.

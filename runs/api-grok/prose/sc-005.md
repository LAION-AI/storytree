EXT. THE LEE CAMP - NIGHT

A cut of fallen rock. A blackened ring, the fire almost dead. Packs in the dark. Four bodies under thin wool.

Willa comes down off the last of the path with the pack on. She stops at the edge. Hands empty. She does not call out.

                    WILLA
          I'm off the pass. This is your lee. I'm not calling it.

She waits. Does not step in.

                    A WOMAN (O.S.)
          You checked the kit?

                    A MAN (O.S.)
          Bar's under the sacks. Heavy as it looked in their shed.

                    A WOMAN
          Then lie down. We move it when we can walk.

                    A MAN
          We should've taken their iron too.

                    A WOMAN
          We should've grown another back. You carried what you carried. The bars stayed. That was the weight. That was the choice.

                    ANOTHER
          I'm eating and I'm down. Argue it quieter.

                    A MAN
          That's a winter sitting in a sack. Someone'll buy a stamped weight.

                    A WOMAN
          Someone with grain. We don't have that someone tonight. We've got this lee and a fire that's already quit. Lie down.

                    A MAN
          If we sit we lose the chance to trade it downslope.

                    A WOMAN
          If you talk till your mouth's the last thing working we lose the legs. Down. Now.

A WOMAN sits up long enough to push a pot lid shut. She lies back. A MAN turns his face into the wool. ANOTHER is already still.

                    A WOMAN
          No watch. No one left to stand one. Sleep.

Breathing evens.

Willa looks over the bodies. Nothing in her hands.

                    WILLA
          You're down. Good. I'm not putting a blade in a hungry camp for a village bar.

She steps in. She goes to the packs.

                    WILLA
          By weight. Not by looking.

Her hands go into the first pack.

                    WILLA
          Grain-sack. Half full. That's food. That stays.

She sets it back.

                    WILLA
          Pot. Cold. Hammer-head. Too short for what I walked for. That's not it.

Second pack. She lifts a wrapped shape and stops.

                    WILLA
          Presentation length. Stamp on the face. Weld at mid-bar. That's the piece.

She hefts it without taking the cloth off.

                    WILLA
          You sit wrong. Stamp says one weight. Hand says another. Not opening you here.

A body under the wool shifts. The MAN's eyes are half open.

                    A MAN
          Who's that.

Willa keeps the wrapped bar against her side. Both hands empty of anything else.

                    WILLA
          No one. Go back down.

                    A MAN
          You taking food?

                    WILLA
          No.

                    A MAN
          Then what are you lifting.

                    WILLA
          Nothing you need. Sleep. You've got walking tomorrow.

                    A MAN
          If you're one of us, say it.

She does not say it.

                    WILLA
          I'm not taking your pot. I'm not taking your grain. Lie down.

                    A MAN
          Then leave the kit alone.

                    WILLA
          Lie down.

He makes a sound that is almost a word. His eyes close. She does not move until his breathing evens again.

She puts the wrapped bar into her own pack and ties it.

                    WILLA
          I've got it. The rest is yours. I'm out.

She withdraws the way she came. Does not run. At the edge of the cut she stops and listens. No feet.

                    WILLA
          Fourteen left. I've got the piece. Hamlet gets it on the cloth if I walk it.

She starts up toward the pass.

Behind her, wool throws off. The woman is on her knees in the kit, hands going through sacks.

                    A WOMAN
          The bar.

                    A MAN
          What.

                    A WOMAN
          The bar's not here. Someone walked it out.

                    A MAN
          Get up. Get a stick. We can still—

                    A WOMAN
          Sit down.

                    A MAN
          That's trade. That's the only thing we took that wasn't food.

                    ANOTHER
          I'm not going up that pass in the dark for a weight.

                    A WOMAN
          No one is. We've still got the pot. We've still got the grain. We've still got everything except the one heavy thing.

                    A MAN
          So we let it go. After we carried it.

                    A WOMAN
          We eat. We don't chase a walker we never even saw. You want to die on the ridgeline for a stamped bar, you go. I'm not sending anyone.

                    ANOTHER
          I'm not going.

                    A MAN
          That's a winter we just lost.

                    A WOMAN
          That's a winter we already didn't have. Lie down. The rest is still here. Count it if you have to. Then sleep.

Silence. The man swears once, small, and lies back down. No one stands. No one starts up the cut.

Willa, already on the rise, hears the voices and no feet after them.

                    WILLA
          They're not coming. Good.

She keeps walking. She does not look back.

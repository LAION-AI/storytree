INT. THE FORGE-HOUSE - DAY

Morning. The fire is up. Tithe bars wait on the floor. The village weight sits unwrapped on the bench, the empty cloth folded beside it.

Willa works the bellows, rag over her split hands. Orren files at the vise, closer to the hearth than the work wants.

                        WILLA
              Like a job on the books then.
              Item one. The weld.

                        ORREN
              Mine. That decade.

                        WILLA
              I already read it.

                        ORREN
              You did.

                        WILLA
              Item two. The cost in the
              inclusion.

                        ORREN
              Named. Living. Mine.

                        WILLA
              Item three. Can it be moved.

                        ORREN
              No.

                        WILLA
              Onto another body. Mine.
              Anyone living.

                        ORREN
              There is no act for that.

                        WILLA
              Taught. Shown. Put under a
              new name.

                        ORREN
              It took one name. It keeps
              it.

                        WILLA
              Paused then. One season. Hold
              it off till the rite is done
              and the grain is in.

                        ORREN
              It doesn't pause.

                        WILLA
              Paid off. Extra iron on the
              cloth. Fat tithe instead of
              thin.

                        ORREN
              Iron doesn't buy it down.

                        WILLA
              Work. Years. Whatever they
              would take for a discharge.

                        ORREN
              They don't take this. And we
              have no act that discharges
              it.

                        WILLA
              So nothing we can reach.

                        ORREN
              Nothing we have transfers it.
              Nothing pauses it. Nothing
              discharges it.

She lets the bellows hang. She weighs a hinge in her palm and hangs it back.

                        WILLA
              Then what ends it.

                        ORREN
              I die.

                        WILLA
              That's one ending.

                        ORREN
              Or the piece is broken in
              front of the steward. He has
              to write a challenge if it's
              broken where he can see it.
              Those two.

                        WILLA
              Public.

                        ORREN
              Public. A break in this house
              is only scrap. It wouldn't
              count.

                        WILLA
              No third.

                        ORREN
              No third.

                        WILLA
              Highkettle has paid tithe-iron
              every winter. Bars on the
              cloth. True weight, year on
              year.

                        ORREN
              They have.

                        WILLA
              For a right that is only tax
              on your body.

He files. The stroke does not change.

                        WILLA
              They paid for the pass and
              the grain.

                        ORREN
              They did.

                        WILLA
              And what they bought was you.
              Not a relic. You.

He sets the file down. He does not look at the weight. He does not correct her.

                        ORREN
              That's the measure of it.

                        WILLA
              You don't put a third ending
              on the books.

                        ORREN
              There isn't one to put.

She lifts an ordinary bar from the unfinished stack and holds it out across the anvil.

                        WILLA
              Take this. I need to see it.

He takes the bar. It is not heavy. A tremor starts in his wrist before the bar reaches his chest. The shake runs to the elbow. The bar tips.

                        WILLA
              Give it here.

She takes it before it drops and sets it on the anvil. He sits on the hearth-stool without being asked. It takes him time to unhook his fingers.

                        WILLA
              That's not the draft under
              the door.

                        ORREN
              No.

                        WILLA
              Not only the years.

                        ORREN
              No.

                        WILLA
              Not only the work either.

                        ORREN
              No.

                        WILLA
              Yesterday you hid the shake.

                        ORREN
              Yesterday I still tried.

                        WILLA
              How long to put a bar down.

                        ORREN
              Longer than last winter.
              Longer than the winter before
              that.

                        WILLA
              A full day at the anvil.

                        ORREN
              I can still stand a day. The
              last hour is the bill.

                        WILLA
              That's the levy sitting in
              the wrist.

                        ORREN
              It's been sitting there.

                        WILLA
              You don't hide it now.

                        ORREN
              No point.

She leaves the ordinary bar on the anvil. She does not touch the village weight.

                        WILLA
              The slide year is forty years
              gone.

                        ORREN
              It is.

                        WILLA
              That year needed a piece or
              the pass closed. Grain
              withheld. Children in the
              houses.

                        ORREN
              That was the year.

                        WILLA
              This year has a piece. There.
              On the bench. The rite is
              five days off.

                        ORREN
              Five.

                        WILLA
              The old need doesn't govern
              the one that's coming.

He rubs the wrist against his knee. He does not offer another reason.

                        ORREN
              I don't have a new one to put
              in its place.

                        WILLA
              Then you don't lay the slide
              year on this rite.

                        ORREN
              I didn't say I would.

                        WILLA
              You didn't say you wouldn't.

                        ORREN
              The compact will stand while
              I keep paying.

                        WILLA
              Only while.

                        ORREN
              Only while. If I stop, it
              doesn't stand.

                        WILLA
              And the hamlet.

                        ORREN
              Has not been told.

She looks from him to the weight on the bench. She does not add to that. She does not take it back. She picks up the tongs.

                        WILLA
              The tithe is still short.

                        ORREN
              We'll finish the iron.

He stays on the stool a breath longer, then reaches for the file. His hand is not steady yet.

They work.

INT. THE FORGE-HOUSE - DAY

The village weight sits on the house side of the bench, unwrapped. Not in any chest. Not broken.

Unfinished tithe-bars wait on the forge floor in a short stack. The room holds scale and wet wool. Heat dies two steps from the hearth.

Willa draws a thin bar at the anvil. Frost has marked her hands. Orren files on the stool nearer the fire than the work needs. He does not look at the weight. He does not ask.

                    WILLA
          Four days.

                    ORREN
          I can count.

                    WILLA
          You haven't asked what I
          mean to do with that.

                    ORREN
          There's iron to finish.

                    WILLA
          That isn't an answer.

                    ORREN
          It's the work.

                    WILLA
          You could ask.

                    ORREN
          I could. I'm not going to
          until the iron's done.

He keeps at the file. She sets the bar down and takes up the village weight. She hefts it. The frost-marks on her fingers show against the iron. She lays it on the balance. The arm does not come level.

                    WILLA
          Still light for the stamp.

                    ORREN
          You knew that.

                    WILLA
          If I stay and work the
          truth of it — open the
          weld, take what I read
          down to the hearths — we
          come late. Or we come
          with a piece they can
          doubt. They close the
          pass. They hold the grain.

                    ORREN
          They would.

                    WILLA
          People go hungry on that.

                    ORREN
          These people would.

                    WILLA
          If I carry it to the
          steward as if I never
          read it, then I spent the
          walk on a fraud. I spent
          the frost on it. That's
          mine now. I own the lie
          as much as you.

                    ORREN
          You do.

                    WILLA
          You don't deny either way
          spends it.

                    ORREN
          Both spend what you
          brought back. I won't
          dress that.

She leaves the weight on the balance. She does not walk it to the door. She does not reach for a hammer.

                    WILLA
          What do I owe you.

                    ORREN
          For what.

                    WILLA
          The raising. You took me
          in. Years of keep. Food.
          A loft.

                    ORREN
          I don't keep that book.

                    WILLA
          Someone has to.

                    ORREN
          Not me.

                    WILLA
          The craft, then. Heat.
          Flux. Quench. Every join
          you put in my hands.
          That's a living.

                    ORREN
          You paid that at the
          fire. Year on year.

                    WILLA
          Did I.

                    ORREN
          You did the work.

                    WILLA
          And this.

She nods at the weight on the balance.

                    WILLA
          The lie. Forty years of
          grain because you bound a
          false bar and paid it out
          of your body. I walked
          for that. I brought it
          home.

                    ORREN
          I didn't ask you to walk
          for the lie.

                    WILLA
          You didn't tell me before
          I went.

                    ORREN
          No.

                    WILLA
          So that's three accounts.
          Raising. Craft. The lie
          that kept the grain
          coming. I can't add them.
          They aren't one weight.
          They don't come level.

                    ORREN
          I have no figure that
          makes them one.

                    WILLA
          Give me work. A stack. A
          number of days I can
          finish.

                    ORREN
          Four days won't hold it.

                    WILLA
          If I work the winter,
          does any of it come off.

                    ORREN
          It keeps the fire. That's
          all.

                    WILLA
          Then I can't settle it.
          Not in what's left. Not
          by any work I can name.

                    ORREN
          I never called it due.

                    WILLA
          That doesn't pay it.

                    ORREN
          I know.

She takes the weight off the balance and holds it. She looks toward the door, then at the anvil, then at him. She sets the weight back on the house side of the bench.

                    WILLA
          I won't break it.

                    ORREN
          All right.

                    WILLA
          And I won't take the
          reading down the path. If
          the hearths know, someone
          says it at the table. The
          year goes.

                    ORREN
          The Hold doesn't look
          inside unless a person
          makes them.

                    WILLA
          So both courses stay
          open. I don't walk it
          down. I don't break it. I
          don't take it to the
          table today as if it were
          true. Neither one can be
          finished clean before the
          steward.

                    ORREN
          That's not a choice. The
          table will want a piece
          and a stack. That's all
          it wants.

                    WILLA
          I know what it wants. I
          don't know what I'll set
          down. The piece stays
          with me.

                    ORREN
          And what you owe.

                    WILLA
          Can't be paid before that
          table. Not the raising.
          Not the craft. Not the
          lie. No sum fits. No time
          fits. Four days isn't a
          ledger.

                    ORREN
          I heard you.

                    WILLA
          Don't tell me the old
          year again.

                    ORREN
          I wasn't going to.

She puts her hand on the unfinished stack. She does not pick the village weight up. He reaches for the next thin bar. His hand is not steady.

The weight stays on the house side of the bench.

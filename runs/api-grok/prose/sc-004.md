EXT. FOOT OF THE WINTER PASS - DAY

A stone marker. Beyond it the track lifts into a high cut. No bar. No chain. No man in Hold colors.

Willa stops. She slips one strap and hefts the pack the way she would a bar off the rack.

                        WILLA
              That's the weight. Not the stamp.

She looks back down the village track. Empty. One thread of chimney smoke. No horse. No one coming.

                        WILLA
              Path's open.
              Hold hasn't shut it.
              No one on it but me.

She waits as if a voice might still call her down.

None does.

                        WILLA
              I'm not turning.

She takes the strap and steps past the marker.

EXT. THE WINTER PASS - DAY

Lee of the ridgeline. Stone underfoot. Wind on the crest, not on her.

Old prints in the frozen mud. Boot soles. No hoof.

Willa crouches. She measures a print with two fingers.

                        WILLA
              Four. Five.
              Light step. Going down the far side.
              Days old.

A cracked wooden bowl in the lee of a rock. She turns it. No blood. No iron scale.

                        WILLA
              Bowl's theirs.
              Hungry people.
              Not a garrison.
              Not an army.

She stands. The cut ahead is empty as far as it runs.

                        WILLA
              Just me then.
              That's the traffic.

She walks. She places each foot as if the ground were being measured, not claimed.

EXT. THE WINTER PASS - DUSK

A scrape of earth out of the wind. She builds a fire the size of a cup. One stick at a time.

She takes bread, breaks it, puts half back in the cloth.

                        WILLA
              That's tonight.
              That's the morning.
              Fuel's the same. Don't spend it on comfort.

She unwraps her feet. Looks. Wraps them again, tighter.

                        WILLA
              Distance is the pack and the feet.
              Not a map.
              Pack's true. Feet will lie later.

She notches a stick. Counts the marks with her thumb.

                        WILLA
              Twenty-one this morning.
              Twenty when the light goes.
              Rite doesn't move for anyone.

She looks at the bedroll. She does not unroll it fully.

                        WILLA
              Four hours.
              Not six.

The fire is already dying. She lets it.

EXT. THE WINTER PASS - DAY

Higher. Later. She walks slower than she did at the marker.

The pack rides lighter. She stops anyway and hefts it in one hand.

                        WILLA
              Lighter.
              Bread's gone. Half the fuel.
              Legs say heavier.
              Legs are wrong.

She sits. She gets up slower than she sat. Blows once on her hands. Tucks them under her arms. The hands are red. Not white. Not marked.

                        WILLA
              Cold's a cost.
              Walking time's a cost.
              I need this for the walk back.
              I need this for the iron on the floor.

She starts moving before she has finished.

                        WILLA
              Late gets the hamlet nothing.
              Late spends the piece.

She does not look at her hands again.

EXT. THE WINTER PASS - NIGHT

Another scrape. Almost no fuel. She does not light a fire.

She eats standing. She notches the stick. Many marks now.

                        WILLA
              Seventeen.
              Sixteen at first light.

A silence. She speaks toward the dark as if a man were at a file.

                        WILLA
              You'll be at that bar.
              The one that doesn't need it.

She stops.

                        WILLA
              That's the house.
              This is the walk.
              I don't have a number for the rest.

She lies down in the coat. She does not give the night more than she said she would.

EXT. THE WINTER PASS - DAWN

The far slope sits in sight below the cut. A thin stain of old smoke in a lee that could hold a camp.

Willa stands with the pack already on. The stick in her hand. She counts the notches twice, aloud.

                        WILLA
              Fourteen.

She looks at the ground as if it were a bed she might still take.

                        WILLA
              Extra night puts me at thirteen
              with better legs.
              Extra night spends a day I don't have.

She looks at the stain of smoke.

                        WILLA
              If they still have it.
              If they haven't eaten the trade
              or walked it further on.

She puts the stick away.

                        WILLA
              I'm not sitting it out.

She starts down the far side. She does not look back.

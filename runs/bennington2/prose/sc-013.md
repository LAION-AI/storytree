EXT. DOWNTOWN BENNINGTON - NIGHT

Wet brick. Shop lights still on. Awnings drip into the gutter. Neon is a drugstore and a closed arcade, nothing else.

PIKE comes off the Lodge block under the awnings. Same walk. Wet cuffs. He does not hurry and he does not call ahead.

A clerk in the cassette shop flips her OPEN sign so it stays OPEN. Pike does not look at her.

A MAN is on the corner by the arcade, too close to a student who is trying to light a cigarette in the wet. The student leaves. The man stays.

                        PIKE
              Name.

                        THE MAN
              I'm on four. You can check it.

                        PIKE
              Name.

                        THE MAN
              Ellis. Ellis, all right? Four's paid. I have it on me.

Pike is already walking. ELLIS has to move or lose the block.

                        ELLIS
              Seven is a different night. I didn't buy seven. Nobody said seven until tonight.

                        PIKE
              That door.

He means the side street. He is already standing in its mouth. Ellis stops short of it.

                        ELLIS
              Don't do the walk. Walk me to the desk. I'll put seven down. I just need ten minutes and a vein that isn't on his page.

Pike does not answer. He stays in the mouth of the side street until Ellis cannot use it.

Ellis cuts left instead, toward a parked Datsun with a WOMAN still in the passenger seat, picking at a cassette wrapper. Ellis hits the passenger glass with the flat of his hand.

                        ELLIS
              Open it. I know you. Last night, the lot behind the Hollow. Open it.

The woman locks the door. She shakes her head. She will not look at his mouth.

                        WOMAN
              I don't know you. I'm waiting on somebody.

                        ELLIS
              You're waiting. That's enough. Thirty seconds. You won't remember.

Pike is already at the driver's door. He does not touch the car. He only stands where Ellis would have to go through him to reach her.

                        PIKE
              Not paid.

                        ELLIS
              I said I'll pay it. After. You want the number, take the number after I can stand up.

                        PIKE
              Name.

                        ELLIS
              You just had it.

                        PIKE
              Name.

                        ELLIS
              Ellis. Stop asking. Walk me in. I'll stamp it myself.

Pike does not walk him in. Ellis leaves the Datsun. The woman starts the engine and pulls out without putting her lights on.

Ellis takes the bakery alcove. The BAKER is pulling trays, still open, radio low. Ellis puts both hands on the service ledge like a customer.

                        ELLIS
              Coffee. And the back. I need the back for a minute.

                        BAKER
              We're not a hallway. You want coffee, you wait at the front.

                        ELLIS
              I'll buy the tray. I'll buy whatever you were about to throw out. Just the door behind you.

Pike steps into the alcove and takes the line to the baker. He does not raise his voice.

                        PIKE
              He's not paid.

                        BAKER
              I don't take tabs from either of you. You want something, you order it and you go.

                        ELLIS
              He's going to stand here until I can't. You understand that? He'll stand until I use the street.

                        BAKER
              Then use the street. I'm not your back room.

Ellis leans in on Pike, close enough that the wet from Pike's cuff transfers.

                        ELLIS
              You're on his book too. He sends you because you're cheaper than the vial. You close me, you're still a line. Next unpaid hour, it's your name.

Pike does not answer. He does not offer a number. He only stays where the baker's wrist is, so Ellis cannot reach it.

                        ELLIS
              Six tonight. Four tomorrow. That's better than a hole. Tell him I said six. He likes a number.

                        PIKE
              Not paid.

                        ELLIS
              Then take me in. Right now. I'll walk in front of you. I'll put seven on the desk. I cannot do seven and the street in the same hour.

The baker looks from face to face, then at Ellis's hands.

                        BAKER
              Get off the ledge. Both of you. I'm locking.

                        ELLIS
              Don't.

The baker turns a key in the inside gate. The alcove becomes a box with no door left in it.

Ellis comes off the ledge and goes for the only light still throwing a hard circle on the brick: the sodium over the crosswalk. Pike is already in that circle.

A late WALKER comes through with a grocery bag. Ellis looks at the walker the way a man looks at a tap.

                        ELLIS
              Don't. One minute off the page. You can say you didn't see it. He can say he didn't see it. I buy the night after.

Pike steps between Ellis and the walker. The walker goes around them without stopping.

                        PIKE
              Used.

                        ELLIS
              I will pay him. I will go to the desk. I need the minute first or I will not get there.

                        PIKE
              Name.

                        ELLIS
              You want me gone, say you want me gone. Don't do this like a route.

                        PIKE
              Ellis.

Ellis breaks. He leaves the sodium and runs into the open lane, reaching for the walker's coat, for a wrist, for anything still moving.

Pike takes the reaching arm and puts him down on the brick. It is not a long thing. Ellis stays down. The open pavement holds him and then does not have to.

Pike is already turned toward the blocks that still have lights. He does not look back.

The walker drops the grocery bag and does not pick it up.

EXT. DOWNTOWN BENNINGTON - LATER

The cassette clerk locks from the inside. The drugstore pulls its gate. A barback on the Hollow block kills the outside bulb and leaves the inside one.

                        BAKER
              We're closed. I don't care who still wants coffee.

                        CLERK
              That man was just here.

                        BAKER
              He isn't.

A figure in a doorway watches the locks go on, then turns uphill, toward the Lodge, not toward any other door.

Pike walks the remaining lights. He does not stop at the pavement. He does not pick up the dropped bag. A payphone on the corner still works. He steps under its hood and drops the coin.

                        PIKE
              It's Pike.

                        VOSS (O.S.)
              Paid how.

                        PIKE
              Removal. Name was Ellis. Ellis.

A stamp comes through the receiver. Paper, then the stamp again.

                        VOSS (O.S.)
              Marked. Paid by removal. The rest buy the desk or they don't buy a night.

Pike waits. The receiver does not ask him anything else.

                        PIKE
              Line's closed.

                        VOSS (O.S.)
              That's a cleaner book. Stay on the bricks. Anyone still off seven is yours.

                        PIKE
              Copy.

                        VOSS (O.S.)
              I'm at the desk.

He hangs up first. Pike holds the dead receiver a half-second, then sets it down.

He leaves the hood of the phone. The last shops finish their locks. He takes the awnings again, same walk, toward whatever light is still on, and he does not go back for the name.

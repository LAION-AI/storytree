INT. THE LODGE - NIGHT

Voss at the blotter. Ledger open. A tray of vials, shortest in front. He watches the hall.

The street door misses its first grab. COLE HARLAN comes in on the second try, shoulder to the wood. Lean. Watch worn face-in. He does not apologize to the door.

He stops in the office doorway and stays there.

Voss looks up. Stamp-ink on the right finger.

                    VOSS
          Jamb's free. Chair isn't.
          You can pick.

Cole's hand on the frame. He waits on it. It keeps moving.

                    COLE
          Three doors on the green.
          Marks on all three.
          What's the number.

                    VOSS
          Hour. Clerk. Night shift.
          Forty.
          That's the walk-in.

                    COLE
          Forty.
          Say it again.

                    VOSS
          Forty off the page.
          Or you sit. Twelve a night.
          I write you. I hold the chair.
          I keep the marked doors off
          your route.
          That's the cheaper stamp.

                    COLE
          The hour.
          That's the buy.

                    VOSS
          You're paying twenty-eight
          to keep a name out of a book.
          I don't know a market where
          that's the smart line.

                    COLE
          I'm not selling you a line.
          I'm buying sixty minutes.

Voss taps the client column with the clean end of the pen. Does not write.

                    VOSS
          Unbound walks get collected.
          Not always the night you take
          them. The night the stamps
          fade and everybody remembers
          who isn't on a page.
          Sit. I can make this hold.

                    COLE
          How long is the hour.

                    VOSS
          Sixty unless you finish early.
          Early doesn't refund.
          I need a name if you sit.

                    COLE
          I'm not sitting.

                    VOSS
          That hand is about to take
          something I haven't sold you.
          Sit. Or put the forty down.

Cole takes a fold of bills from his pocket. The count is slow. He has to start the last ten over. He does not sit to make it easier.

He sets forty on the blotter and stays on his feet.

                    COLE
          Forty.
          That's the number.

Voss thumbs the bills. Does not push them back. He stamps the hour line. He does not touch the name column. He does not rewrite the twelve.

                    VOSS
          I don't rewrite a price that
          already landed.

He nods at the door behind him. The pen stays on the side column.

                    VOSS
          Back office. She was told an
          hour. You were told an hour.
          Don't make me correct a count.

INT. THE LODGE - BACK OFFICE - NIGHT

A CLERK in a wooden chair. Cardigan. Clock over the cabinet: 1:17. A tray. One empty vial.

Cole stops inside the door. He looks at the clock. He does not look at her.

                    CLERK
          They said you'd know what
          the hour is.

He turns the watch in, matches it to the clock. He does not answer her. He steps in. His eyes stay on the watch.

The clock: 1:21.

Cole is on his feet. He opens his right hand. Closes it. It obeys. He wipes his mouth with the back of that hand and checks the hand again, like a meter.

The clerk is in the chair. Breathing. Collar dark.

He goes out past her and does not lift his eyes to her face.

INT. THE LODGE - NIGHT

Voss has not stood. He writes in the side column, neat.

Cole reads it upside down from the doorway.

                    COLE
          What did you write.

                    VOSS
          Paid. Unbound.

He leaves the twelve sitting on the blotter, unaccepted. Turns the page to the next open hour.

                    COLE
          That's a side column.

                    VOSS
          Side columns wait.
          I don't stand up for them.

Cole is already in the hall.

                    VOSS
          Next open hour is 2:10.
          You're not on it.

The street door. Voss does not look up. The pen is already on the next line.

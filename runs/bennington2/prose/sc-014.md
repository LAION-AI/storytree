INT. THE HOLLOW - NIGHT

Low stage. Neon beer signs. Half the chairs sit empty.

MAREN works the late set in stage clothes that are also street clothes. A bruise holds under the left cuff.

The BARTENDER watches the clock.

She finishes a number. Looks at the street door. Looks at the setlist.

                    MAREN
          That's three. House number's still in.

                    BARTENDER
          Room's dead. Cut it.

                    MAREN
          It's in the book.

                    BARTENDER
          Book doesn't drink.

                    MAREN
          Neither do the chairs.

                    BARTENDER
          Then why sing at them.

                    MAREN
          Because it's in the book.

She sings the house number all the way through. No run on the last line. Not louder. She looks at the door between the lines.

She sets the mic and comes off.

Two REGULARS have full pours they have not touched. Coats on.

                    MAREN
          That's your usual.

                    REGULAR 1
          Early shift.

                    MAREN
          You don't have an early shift.

                    REGULAR 2
          We're out the side.

                    MAREN
          You stay for the pour. That's the pour.

                    REGULAR 1
          Not tonight.

                    MAREN
          The glass is full.

                    REGULAR 2
          So dump it.

They take the side door. They leave the glasses.

A MAN keeps the far wall. He does not come to the rail. He does not look at her hands.

                    MAREN
          You're usually here.

                    MAN
          Not tonight.

                    MAREN
          Two nights you were on the rail. Same glass.

                    MAN
          Different night.

                    MAREN
          Same glass.

                    MAN
          I'm not buying.

                    MAREN
          You can leave it. I'm asking about the rail.

                    MAN
          I'm not on it.

                    MAREN
          Who moved you off.

                    MAN
          Nobody moved me.

                    MAREN
          Then sit. You can look at the glass. You were looking two nights.

                    MAN
          I'm not looking. Take a night, Maren.

He goes out the street door without passing her.

                    MAREN
          Those two stay. That one stands on the rail.

                    BARTENDER
          People leave. That's a bar.

                    MAREN
          They left full glasses.

                    BARTENDER
          Then they're paying for air. Not my problem.

She walks the stick to the register.

                    MAREN
          Who paid the tab.

                    BARTENDER
          What tab.

                    MAREN
          Mine. Cover. The pour after the set.

                    BARTENDER
          It's covered.

                    MAREN
          I asked who.

He shrugs. The shrug turns his shoulder to the rack and away from the till.

                    BARTENDER
          Last call was twenty minutes ago. I'm on glasses.

                    MAREN
          The name on the slip.

                    BARTENDER
          There isn't a slip.

                    MAREN
          Someone opened the drawer.

                    BARTENDER
          Drawer opens. That's what it does.

                    MAREN
          Full glass. Closed drawer. Empty rail. That's paid.

                    BARTENDER
          You don't know that.

                    MAREN
          I know the drawer.

                    BARTENDER
          Then you know what I know. You want a name, ask the person who drank it.

                    MAREN
          Nobody drank it.

                    BARTENDER
          Then there's nothing to name.

                    MAREN
          You run a clean till.

He stacks a pint. Does not answer. She does not ask twice. She takes a rag.

                    BARTENDER
          I can finish it.

                    MAREN
          I'll do the tables.

                    BARTENDER
          Go home. You look like last night.

                    MAREN
          I'm on the tables.

She dumps the two pours. Wipes the rail. Wipes the empty chairs.

He locks the street door. Leaves the side one on the latch.

                    BARTENDER
          You stayed last night. Go home before you fall on my floor.

                    MAREN
          Floor's wiped.

                    BARTENDER
          Glasses. Lights. Latch. That's the job after last call. I'm not your closer.

                    MAREN
          I didn't book you.

                    BARTENDER
          Tables don't pay after last call. I'm not running a second till for you.

                    MAREN
          Nobody asked you for a till.

She pulls a folded page from her jacket. Campus-safety handwriting on the front. She turns it over. A scuffed cassette comes with it. She sets the cassette down. She writes on the back and reads it once, flat.

                    MAREN
          Two at the side door. One on the far wall. Didn't look at the hands. Tab paid. No slip. No name.

                    BARTENDER
          What is that.

                    MAREN
          Deacon's page.

                    BARTENDER
          Of what.

                    MAREN
          Of the side door. Of the rail.

                    BARTENDER
          Deacon's going to love a list of people who walked out.

                    MAREN
          It's not for Deacon.

                    BARTENDER
          Then throw it out.

                    MAREN
          It's a copy.

                    BARTENDER
          I can drop you.

                    MAREN
          I'm on this one.

                    BARTENDER
          Clock's done. I'm locking in ten.

                    MAREN
          Then lock.

She puts the page and the cassette back. She stands. She takes the next table. The bruise shows when she reaches. The cuff covers it again.

She does not sit again. She is still on her feet when he kills the neon.

INT. MAREN'S WALK-UP - DAY

Two rooms over a shop. Radiator. Late November light at a sash that doesn't seal.

MAREN ELLISON sits up in last night's stage clothes. She goes to the sink to wash. Pushes a sleeve back.

Finger-marks. Left upper arm.

She pushes the other sleeve. More of them.

She hits the bathroom bulb. Turns both arms under it. Does not wash.

                    MAREN
          Four.

She turns the left again. Counts off the pads of someone else's fingers.

                    MAREN
          Four. Other side. Two.
          Three.

She lays a long-sleeve shirt on the bed. Buttons it to the collar. Cuffs down over the marks. Checks the mirror. The cuffs hold.

A scrap by the sink. She writes 4, a slash, 3. Underlines it.

                    MAREN
          Four and three.

The wall phone. Her hand on the receiver. Off it.

She takes the stair in the long sleeves. Does not lock the door.

                    CLERK (O.S.)
          Till's live. You coming
          down or do I price the
          used bin myself?

INT. CASSETTE SHOP - DAY

Bins. Fluorescent. A counter deck. The CLERK has the register open. Early twenties. Pricing stickers on his fingers.

                    CLERK
          You slept in that.

                    MAREN
          I slept.

                    CLERK
          Hollow go late?

                    MAREN
          Same four songs.

She stands at the return bin. Flips cases at work speed.

                    CLERK
          Somebody left a crate on
          the step before I turned
          the key. No slip. I can
          log them store copy.

                    MAREN
          Not yet.

                    CLERK
          You on the late key?

                    MAREN
          Late key's mine.

Her hand stops. A C90. Handwriting on the spine.

She turns it over once.

                    CLERK
          That one of yours?

                    MAREN
          That's my hand.

                    CLERK
          Then it came back. You
          want it on the card?

She puts it in her pocket.

                    CLERK
          That's stock if it's in
          the bin.

                    MAREN
          It's a C90.

                    CLERK
          I need a title for the
          card. Thirty seconds.
          Play it and then you can
          fight me about who keeps
          it.

She looks at the counter deck. Sets the cassette in. Hits play.

From the speaker, her voice. Flat. Counting.

                    MAREN'S VOICE (FROM DECK)
          First song. The one they
          talk through. Second.
          Third. Hold the last for
          the bar back. Fourth—

The voice stops. Not a song. Not the end of a tape. Room tone. Someone still there.

The clerk draws a breath.

She hits stop. Ejects. Pocket.

                    CLERK
          That was you not finishing—

                    MAREN
          It's a tape.

                    CLERK
          Same voice that's standing
          there. Give it back. I
          need to write what it is.

                    MAREN
          You heard it. You don't
          need it.

She is already on the stair.

                    CLERK
          I can run who dumped the
          crate. You want the phone?

                    MAREN
          No.

                    CLERK
          Your sleeve's up.

She yanks the cuff down without turning. Goes.

INT. MAREN'S WALK-UP - DAY

She sets the C90 on the sink next to the scrap. 4 / 3. The cassette.

The phone stays on the hook.

INT. THE HOLLOW - NIGHT

Stage lights die. House lights come up thin and yellow. The merch table sits empty by the door.

The bartender rings a glass.

                        BARTENDER
              Last call. Lights are down.
              If you're drinking, now.

Cole has the notebook shut in his hand. Pen already in the pocket it came from.

                        BARTENDER (CONT'D)
              She'll sit the table. That's
              the other half. Tape's three
              bucks and I don't eat the
              difference.

                        COLE
              I'm not buying a tape.

                        BARTENDER
              Then you're buying a drink.
              You stood the whole rail. I
              don't lease that for free.

                        COLE
              I wasn't leasing it.

                        BARTENDER
              So what was the book.

                        COLE
              A length.

                        BARTENDER
              You already said you had the
              length. Your hand kept going.
              I can see a second column from
              here.

                        COLE
              Then you don't need me to
              read it.

                        BARTENDER
              I need you off my rail or on
              my till. Hobby doesn't cover
              either.

A PATRON slides into the space Cole is leaving.

                        PATRON
              He done? I been waiting on
              this rail since the second
              song.

                        BARTENDER
              He's done when he pays or
              walks.

                        COLE
              Walking.

                        PATRON
              Whole set, no drink, little
              book. What were you timing,
              her or—

Cole is at the lot door. Cold air. The rest of it dies behind him.

INT. MOTOR COURT ROOM - PRE-DAWN

End unit. Ice machine through the wall. Desk, bed, curtains already pulled.

He sets the notebook on the desk. No title. Fits the curtain to the line it already has. Lies down dressed. Watch turned in against the wrist.

INT. MOTOR COURT ROOM - DUSK

He sits up. Swallows like his mouth is wrong. Checks the watch without showing the face. Looks at the door long enough that leaving is available. He stays in the chair.

He takes the phone. Dials the desk.

                        COLE
              First light on the lot.

                        CLERK (O.S.)
              Desk.

                        COLE
              The lot. First light.

                        CLERK (O.S.)
              Before seven.

                        COLE
              I need the number.

                        CLERK (O.S.)
              Before seven is the number.
              End unit. Lot gets it first.
              I don't do wake-ups. I don't
              do questions. You wanted a
              time, you got one.

                        COLE
              Lot first. Before seven.

He hangs up. Opens the spiral. Last night's page is only times. He turns to a clean one and writes a fraction at the top.

                        COLE (CONT'D)
              Two over seven.

The pen hangs where a name would go. He leaves it blank.

He rules four columns. Speaks each as the line lands.

                        COLE (CONT'D)
              Hunger.

He writes it. Writes a four beside it. Looks at the door again. Does not stand.

                        COLE (CONT'D)
              Four. Higher than last dusk.
              Sun-margin.
              Before seven. Not a number.
              Stored decay.
              Chapel window.

He writes the last dawn at the foot of the last column and underlines it until the paper shines.

A matchbook on the desk. He strikes one. Holds the flame to the curtain seam. Watch up.

                        COLE (CONT'D)
              One. Two. Three. Four. Five.
              Six.

He flinches. Kills the match. Writes the six under sun-margin.

                        COLE (CONT'D)
              Six. That's standable. After
              that the cloth goes.

He writes under the fraction, small and plain: first at that window, or the page is waste.

                        COLE (CONT'D)
              First. Or this is waste.

He does not add a name. Coat off the chair. One sleeve, then the other. The book stays open.

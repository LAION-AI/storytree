EXT. MOTOR COURT - NIGHT

Cole steps out. The open notebook shuts and goes into the coat. He walks toward the brick. He does not take the road out of town.

EXT. DOWNTOWN BENNINGTON - NIGHT

Wet pavement. A payphone. An arcade cabinet buzzing behind glass. Cole counts the doors as he passes them.

A side-street bar dumps last call onto the sidewalk. He takes the doorway across and stays there. People leave in pairs. He does not follow any of them.

The BARTENDER comes out alone with the keys and a bank bag. Cole steps into his path.

                        COLE
          What time do you lock.

                        BARTENDER
          We're locked. That's your answer.

                        COLE
          The number.

                        BARTENDER
          Jesus. Two. I don't know. Two-ten.
          Move.

                        COLE
          Two-fourteen. Say it back.

                        BARTENDER
          I'm not saying it back. I got a
          register to drop and a kid at
          seven. You want a drink, we're
          closed.

                        COLE
          I don't want a drink.

He takes a till receipt from the man's apron pocket. The bartender grabs for it. Cole has already turned it over. He writes 2:14 on the back. Presses the receipt to the man's chest.

                        COLE (CONT'D)
          This stays on you.

He marks a quarter with the pen. A scratch across the date. Holds it out.

                        COLE (CONT'D)
          Tip. Put it where the tips go.

                        BARTENDER
          Don't put your hands on me. I
          don't take money off the street
          at two in the morning.

                        COLE
          Then it sits in the jar tomorrow.
          That's the same place.

                        BARTENDER
          What is this.

                        COLE
          Two-fourteen. That's the number.

He puts the quarter in the man's palm and closes the fingers over it. He does not lean in. He steps back to the doorway.

                        BARTENDER
          I got a kid at seven. If this is
          some—

                        COLE
          Two-fourteen.

                        BARTENDER
          You're standing there all night
          now? That's a choice.

                        COLE
          It is.

The bartender swears, pockets the receipt without looking, and goes. The quarter is in his fist.

EXT. DOWNTOWN BENNINGTON - NIGHT

Cole in the same doorway. Watch face-in. He counts the empty street.

INT. MOTOR COURT ROOM - DAWN

A strip of day under the door. Cole sits on the far side of the bed, out of it. Coat still on. He checks the watch. Checks it again. His hand is not steady. He does not go to the curtain.

INT. MOTOR COURT ROOM - DUSK

The strip is gone. He stands. Notebook into the coat. Out.

EXT. DOWNTOWN BENNINGTON - NIGHT

Same side street. Full night. Through the glass: a tip jar by the register. The marked quarter is in it.

INT. SIDE-STREET BAR - NIGHT

Cole at the stick. Same man. Same jacket. Cole sets a five down. Does not order.

                        COLE
          What time did you lock last night.

                        BARTENDER
          You again. You want a beer or you
          want the weather.

                        COLE
          The hour.

                        BARTENDER
          Midnight. Give or take. Felt like
          a week. Long shift.

Cole opens the notebook on the bar. Writes where the man can see it: 2:14. names 12. coin. paper. hour gone.

                        COLE
          Midnight.

                        BARTENDER
          Yeah. Why, you keeping score.

The bartender fishes for a smoke. The till receipt comes out of the jacket pocket with the pack. 2:14 on the back. He looks at it. Laughs.

                        BARTENDER (CONT'D)
          That's not even my writing. I
          don't know what I was doing.

                        COLE
          What time did you lock.

                        BARTENDER
          I told you. Midnight. Look, if
          you're here about a tab—

                        COLE
          I'm not.

He looks at the receipt in the man's hand. At the quarter in the jar. He does not say two-fourteen.

                        BARTENDER
          Then what. You left me a quarter
          and a scrap and now you're
          standing there like I owe you
          the night.

Cole closes the notebook on the line.

                        COLE
          You don't.

He leaves the five. Leaves the quarter in the jar. Leaves the receipt in the man's hand. He walks out.

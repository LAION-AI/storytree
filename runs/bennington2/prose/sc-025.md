EXT. DOWNTOWN BENNINGTON - NIGHT

Late. Wet brick. One liquor sign. Water off the awnings.

PIKE leaves the Lodge and stays under the cover. Long coat. Wet cuffs. A folded slip in his hand. The walk does not change.

One block to Forty-one Depot. Dark door. No car. No lamp up.

DEACON WARD is already on the top step. Heavy jacket. Notebook open.

Pike stops at the bottom.

                    PIKE
          Name.

Deacon finds the line. Reads it.

                    DEACON
          Forty-one Depot. Twelve-ten.
          Collection after hours. No
          student named. No caller named.

                    PIKE
          Walk.

                    DEACON
          Ward. Deacon Ward. Campus
          safety. Night desk to this
          address. I knocked. No answer.
          Arrival twelve twenty-two.

                    PIKE
          Name.

                    DEACON
          I gave it. I can read it back.
          If you have a complainant I need
          that paper on my page.

                    PIKE
          No.

                    DEACON
          Then I have a stoop and a time.
          That is all I can swear.

                    PIKE
          The one who owes. Name.

Deacon turns the page. No second party. He holds the book out. Pike does not take it.

                    DEACON
          Address only. You give me a
          name, I add it. That is how it
          stays true.

Pike lifts the slip for himself alone.

                    PIKE
          Harlan.

                    DEACON
          No Harlan on this stoop. Door is
          dark. I wrote that. If he is
          your collection he is not here.

                    PIKE
          Voss wrote the stoop. Aged.
          You are not him. Walk.

                    DEACON
          Give me a description. Height.
          Last seen. I will write him and
          I will wait. I will not leave
          a blank I can fill.

                    PIKE
          Walk.

                    DEACON
          I stay with the complaint.
          Ellison gets a copy of the
          nights I keep. She gets this
          one when I get back.

                    PIKE
          Walk.

Deacon offers the page again. Does not come down.

                    DEACON
          Read it. Forty-one Depot.
          Collection. After hours. Ward
          present. Subject not found. I
          will not invent a man I did not
          see.

Pike hits him. Short. Open hand. Deacon sits. Blood on his mouth hits the page. He writes first.

                    DEACON
          Twelve twenty-four. Male. Long
          coat. Asked for a name twice.
          Struck me. I am still at the
          address.

                    PIKE
          Inside or gone.

                    DEACON
          I need your name for the line.

                    PIKE
          No.

                    DEACON
          Then long coat is what I have.
          You have not closed the first
          number. You have only given me
          a second.

He stands. Does not descend. Reads louder.

                    DEACON
          Forty-one Depot. Collection.
          After hours. No Harlan. Deacon
          Ward present. I am the log.

Pike takes him by the coat and walks him into the alley. A relocation. Brick at Deacon's back. Book still in the fist.

                    DEACON
          Alley east of the door. He
          moved me. Still no second
          party. Still no Harlan. I can
          wait. I already refused a
          second call.

Pike looks at the empty stoop. The door stays shut. Hours left in the night.

                    PIKE
          Harlan.

No answer from the door. Pike puts Deacon to the wall and goes to the throat.

The pencil scrapes once. Stops.

Deacon stops moving. Eyes open. Coat on. Notebook open in his hand. The color is gone from him.

Pike straightens. Wipes his mouth on the cuff.

He checks the slip. Adds nothing. Folds it. Puts it away.

He does not take the notebook.

He leaves by the alley mouth, back under the awnings. Same walk. Only the feet are faster.

The stoop stays empty.

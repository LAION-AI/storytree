INT. MOTOR COURT - OFFICE - DUSK

Fluorescent lobby. Postcard rack. A night CLERK, radio low, a space heater at his feet.

COLE HARLAN sets one bag down. Lean, pale, coat zipped. Cheap analog watch worn face-in. He writes on the registration card without looking up.

                    CLERK
          I need a name on that.

                    COLE
          It's on it.

The clerk does not turn the card around.

                    CLERK
          Cash or card.

                    COLE
          Cash. End room. What's the number.

                    CLERK
          Twelve. Heater's shot. Nine faces
          east. Nice in the morning.

                    COLE
          Twelve.

                    CLERK
          You'll freeze.

                    COLE
          The rate.

                    CLERK
          Thirty-two. No complaints on twelve.

Cole lays exact bills down. No tip. He takes the key while the clerk's hand is still in the drawer.

                    COLE
          First light.

                    CLERK
          What.

                    COLE
          The hour.

                    CLERK
          Six-forty. Wake-up?

                    COLE
          No.

                    CLERK
          How many nights.

                    COLE
          One. Then I'll say.

Cole picks up the bag. The clerk talks at his back.

                    CLERK
          Bakery's the only thing lit. They
          lock the door at eight.

                    COLE
          The sign. Does it run after.

                    CLERK
          I don't watch the bakery.

                    COLE
          The church bells.

                    CLERK
          Quarter hours. When they remember.

Cole does not answer. He is already at the door.

INT. MOTOR COURT ROOM - CONTINUOUS

Heavy curtains already drawn. One bed, made. A desk. An ice machine buzzing through the wall.

Cole stands in the doorway a beat too long. Then he puts the bag on the chair. He does not open it.

He walks from the door to the window. Checks the watch. Walks it again.

                    COLE
          Eight.

He parts the curtain an inch. Sodium lamps on the lot. A street. A bakery sign. A steeple.

He pulls both curtains tighter. Checks the seam with his thumb.

A cheap spiral notebook from the bag. A pen from the same pocket. He sets the notebook open on the desk.

He writes one word. Then the eight. Then the time on the lamps. No date.

Back to the window. Watch raised. He waits.

The bakery sign flickers. He counts under his breath.

                    COLE
          One. Two. Three.

A church bell, thin through the glass.

                    COLE
          Fourteen.

He writes it in the first column. Waits. The sign flickers. He counts again. The bell.

                    COLE
          Fourteen.

He writes the second figure beside the first. He changes neither.

His hand jumps on the pen. He watches the tremor the way he watched the lamps.

                    COLE
          Four.

He writes that number under the intervals. Smaller. One underline.

He looks at the door. At the bag. At the door.

He does not pick up the key.

He sits on the made bed. Coat still on. The notebook stays open. The ice machine does not stop.

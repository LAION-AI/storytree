INT. THE HOLLOW - NIGHT

Neon beer signs. November coats still on. A cheap PA already too loud for the room.

Maren takes the stage in a long-sleeve shirt. She kneels at the monitor and turns two knobs herself.

                    MAREN
              Gain's high.

                    BARTENDER
              That's the house.

                    MAREN
              Then the house is high. I'm pulling it.

She pulls it. Stands. Taps the mic. Her cuff rides up. She yanks it down like it belongs to someone else.

                    MAREN
              Time.

                    BARTENDER
              Nine.

                    MAREN
              Then this is one.

She does not look at the back rail. She hits the first chord on the nine.

Campus new-wave, worked like a shift. The room talks through it.

Cole comes in off the wet brick. He does not take a table. He goes to the back rail with a spiral notebook already open and the pen already in the page. Cheap analog watch worn face-in. He turns the face. Writes a number.

The BARTENDER comes down with a wet towel and a stamp pad.

                    BARTENDER
              Cover's three if you're staying for her.

                    COLE
              I'm staying. Three. Keep two.

He puts five on the rail without lifting his face from the page.

                    BARTENDER
              Hell of a tip for a stamp.

                    COLE
              What time did she start.

                    BARTENDER
              You walked in on the downbeat.

                    COLE
              The number.

                    BARTENDER
              Nine. She's nine. She's always nine.

                    COLE
              Nine.

He writes it.

                    BARTENDER
              So a beer.

                    COLE
              Water.

                    BARTENDER
              Water's free. You're still a five-dollar man.

                    COLE
              Door to stage.

                    BARTENDER
              You're in the room.

                    COLE
              The walk. Seconds.

                    BARTENDER
              Forty feet. I don't clock it.

Cole writes 40 anyway. The bartender stamps the back of his hand and goes. The pen does not stop.

The song ends. Thin applause. Maren is already retuning.

                    MAREN
              That's one. This is two. If you know it you know it.

A COLLEGE KID at a table calls toward the stage.

                    COLLEGE KID
              Do the long one.

                    MAREN
              It's not on the list.

She starts two. Cole writes the start to the second.

A PATRON at the rail, coat still zipped, leans into the page.

                    PATRON
              You reviewing her or serving her.

                    COLE
              How many songs in a Thursday set.

                    PATRON
              What?

                    COLE
              How many. Thursday.

                    PATRON
              Four. Five if she wants us gone. You sending a glass up or just doing homework.

                    COLE
              Four.

He writes 4 in the margin. The top of the page is blank.

                    PATRON
              She's better than this room.

                    COLE
              It's a duration.

The patron waits. Gets nothing. Goes for the bar.

Cole times the end of two. Writes the interval without looking down.

                    MAREN
              Board's hot. Leave it.

She drinks from a glass on the stool. Does not lift it to the room.

                    MAREN
              This one's on the list. Talk through it. You will anyway.

The room hits the first line with her. Claps the break it already knows.

Cole writes through the recognition. Does not clap. Does not mouth the words.

Mid-chorus, Maren looks past the lights, toward the back rail.

Cole is already on the next interval. He does not lift his face.

She holds the look a beat longer than the song has room for. A word lands late. She turns back to the microphone and finishes the line flat.

The known song ends harder than it started. Real applause this time.

From the bar, the patron calls:

                    PATRON
              Encore that.

She does not answer him.

                    MAREN
              That's the one you wanted. This is four.

She starts four. Cole writes the start. The column fills. Still no name at the top.

The bartender is back, wiping the rail next to the notebook.

                    BARTENDER
              You want that water in a glass that looks like a drink, I can walk it up.

                    COLE
              No.

                    BARTENDER
              She looked at you.

                    COLE
              Interval's twelve.

                    BARTENDER
              I'm sorry?

                    COLE
              Song three to four. Twelve.

                    BARTENDER
              You're a lot of fun.

                    COLE
              Last call.

                    BARTENDER
              Not yet. You going to be here for it or are you just buying minutes.

                    COLE
              The time.

                    BARTENDER
              One. Same as every night. Drink the water.

The bartender leaves the water and does not send anything to the stage.

Four ends. Maren kills the last chord. Does not lean into the applause.

                    MAREN
              That's the set.

She does not ask anyone to stay. She does not offer one more. She takes the glass and the jacket off the stool and goes down the side stairs.

Cole writes the end time. Closes the column with his thumb. The top of the page is still blank.

                    BARTENDER
              Twenty-eight minutes, if that's what you were buying. Merch table's by the door. She sits there.

                    COLE
              Twenty-eight.

                    BARTENDER
              You stood through it.

                    COLE
              I have the length.

He does not go to the merch table. He puts the pen in the same pocket it came from. Shuts the notebook. Takes it.

Maren, off the side stairs, does not look at the back rail again.

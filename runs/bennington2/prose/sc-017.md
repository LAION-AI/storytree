INT. WHITLOCK HOUSE - UPSTAIRS ROOM - NIGHT

The room has already been spent. Bare mattress. A lamp on the floor. The lock plate hangs off one screw. The door will not catch.

Downstairs the party is at peak — glass, a laugh, a record left to skip. The east window is still dark.

COLE comes in and stops in the doorway, the hall at his back. He checks the watch worn face-in. He does not touch the lock.

LENA follows him through. She does not sit. She takes the far wall, both doors in view, and watches his mouth.

                    LENA
          Who is the surplus for.

                    COLE
          There isn't one.

She points past his shoulder, back toward the hall, at the place he has been looking all night.

                    LENA
          Two songs. You didn't take the door.

                    COLE
          I take doors. That's a rate.

                    LENA
          Surplus.

She says it the way someone asks the time. She does not give him a softer line. She waits.

                    COLE
          Hollow to this hill is fourteen on foot. Hall to the east glass is forty seconds. Those are the columns.

                    LENA
          Who is it for.

                    COLE
          That isn't a who.

                    LENA
          Then give me the number.

                    COLE
          What's yours for.

                    LENA
          I'm not the one who paid extra.

His jaw works once. He checks the watch again. The second hand is fine.

                    COLE
          Four nights.

                    LENA
          Four.

                    COLE
          First set to this hour. Duration. Sample. You can run a sample without a name.

                    LENA
          For who.

He gets the notebook halfway out of his coat. No line goes in. He puts it back.

                    COLE
          Maren.

He talks faster, as if speed could file it.

                    COLE
          Six minutes if you cut the alley. Hollow to the green. Same interval, shop to the motor court. She's on that line. That's a street. That's all.

                    LENA
          Maren.

She does not thank him. She is already moving. She takes the ruined doorway and makes him give her the threshold.

                    COLE
          Six. That's the number.

She is on the stairs. She does not look back. The party takes her footsteps in two beats.

He is alone in the room he did not want. He opens the notebook. He does not write the name. He puts it away.

He stays in the doorway. The lock plate hangs. Downstairs the record skips and is left skipping.

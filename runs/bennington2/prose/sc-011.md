EXT. THE HOLLOW - NIGHT

MAREN ELLISON comes up the wet brick from the college end of town. She checks one coat pocket with two fingers: a scuffed cassette and a folded campus page, same pocket. She goes in.

INT. THE HOLLOW - NIGHT

Neon beer signs. Cheap PA idling. November coats still on. The BARTENDER is already watching the clock over the till.

                    BARTENDER
          You're on the board or you're drinking.

                    MAREN
          I'm on the board.

                    BARTENDER
          Last call's twenty.

                    MAREN
          Then the set's twenty.

                    BARTENDER
          You already did a set.

                    MAREN
          I did three. Four's still on the list.

She does not take the coat off. She goes straight to the stage, pockets checked once more like a till key. She puts the cassette and the page under the monitor, not in it. Mic stand. Sleeve tugged down. She does not sit on the stool behind her.

                    MAREN
          This is four. You talk through four.

A TABLE near the door groans on cue. Somebody knocks a glass to keep time and then stops bothering.

                    PATRON
          We know four.

                    MAREN
          Then you can hear it.

She plays the house closer colder than the first time, not louder. Four changes. She does not shorten the last one. The bartender looks at the clock, then at her, then at the clock again.

                    BARTENDER
          That's the closer.

                    MAREN
          It's four changes.

                    BARTENDER
          You just played the closer.

                    MAREN
          The room still owes the bar a closer.

                    PATRON
          We had last call.

                    MAREN
          You had a clock.

She hits the last change again. The table near the door puts coats on and stops keeping time altogether.

                    BARTENDER
          Maren.

                    MAREN
          The list says four.

COLE HARLAN comes in off the street with a pocket notebook already open. Cheap watch worn face-in. He takes the stool with the stage and the mic stand in one line and starts writing intervals without looking down. He does not look at who takes the next stool.

LENA CHO is already in the room. She moves one stool. That is all. She can see his page and Maren's hands on the stand from there. She does not say her name.

                    BARTENDER
          You drinking.

                    COLE
          Whatever's poured. What time did she start.

                    BARTENDER
          She started when she felt like it.

                    COLE
          That's not a number.

                    BARTENDER
          Twenty to. That's the number I care about.

Cole writes it. A blank sits in the margin, wide enough for a name he does not put down. He counts the room: door, stick, stand, the gap between the last change and the next. Maren tugs the sleeve over her wrist between phrases. He writes the interval. He does not write the sleeve.

Lena watches his mouth while he counts. Then the sleeve. Then the blank.

                    LENA
          What's the extra.

                    COLE
          Twelve minutes. Door to stand.

                    LENA
          The extra.

She does not point with a finger. She looks at the margin until he has to.

                    COLE
          It's a margin.

                    LENA
          Margin.

                    COLE
          I leave room for a rate.

                    LENA
          For who.

He looks up from the number. She is already on the blank. She has not touched the book. She has not needed to.

                    COLE
          What did you—

She puts cash on the bar for a drink she has already paid for. She is off the stool before the question finishes.

                    LENA
          Keep it.

                    COLE
          That isn't an answer.

She is already not there. He is still mid-count. He restates the twelve to the page, harder, and does not follow her.

From the stage Maren sees a woman step off a stool and not come back. Another late face. She does not clock him as anything else. She finishes the change the room no longer wants.

                    BARTENDER
          Last call means last call.

                    MAREN
          Then call it.

                    BARTENDER
          I just did.

                    PATRON
          We're gone.

                    MAREN
          The door works.

The table leaves. The bartender starts killing the PA for the room and leaves the stage monitor live because she is still standing in it.

                    BARTENDER
          You're done.

                    MAREN
          The monitor isn't.

She takes the cassette out of the pocket with the campus page. No j-card. She slots it into the stage monitor and runs it against the same four changes, low, like stock being counted, not like a set.

Her own voice comes out of the monitor. Work-talk. A night she is not on.

                    MAREN (FROM TAPE)
          That's the closer. Same four changes. If they're
          talking, play it anyway.

She plays the four changes under it. Present tense. Colder.

                    MAREN (FROM TAPE)
          I can do the late set. I don't need the walk.
          Leave the sticker on it. If it hasn't got a
          price it walks.

Her hand goes to the sleeve. She pushes it up far enough that the bruise is a fact, then lets the sleeve drop and writes on the set list instead of on her skin.

                    MAREN
          That's the object.

                    BARTENDER
          That's you on a tape. Congratulations. Go home.

                    MAREN
          It's a clock.

                    BARTENDER
          It's last call.

                    MAREN (FROM TAPE)
          I close at two. The till's counted. Don't put
          that on the sleeve.

She pulls the campus page, Deacon's times, and sets it against the cassette window with her thumb on both. She writes the overlap on the set list: tape time, page time, the four changes. She does not ask him to look.

                    BARTENDER
          What are you doing.

                    MAREN
          Matching stock.

                    BARTENDER
          To what.

                    MAREN
          To the bruise.

                    BARTENDER
          Jesus, Maren. Sit down.

                    MAREN
          I slept. The bruise didn't.

                    BARTENDER
          So you stayed up to stare at a tape of yourself
          talking shop.

                    MAREN
          The tape kept the hours. The bed didn't.

                    BARTENDER
          You're not making sense.

                    MAREN
          I'm making a list.

Cole is still on the stool. Still mid-count. The unread margin is still blank. He writes a number, looks up at the stand, loses the interval because the song is no longer doing what a closer does. He does not ask her what the tape is. He asks the page.

                    COLE
          How long was that last change.

Nobody gives him the number. The bartender has his back to the till. Maren is not talking to the room anymore.

                    BARTENDER
          I'm killing the lights.

                    MAREN
          The monitor's still running.

                    BARTENDER
          Not my problem.

                    MAREN
          It's on the board.

                    BARTENDER
          The board's closed. Go to bed.

                    MAREN
          I'm not off the clock.

                    BARTENDER
          You don't get a vote. House lights.

He kills them. Neon beer signs stay. The monitor throws enough to finish a line and then he kills that too.

In the leftover red she is still on her feet at the stand. She does not sit. She writes the last overlap on the set list by the beer sign, folds the set list and the campus page together, and puts both in the coat with the cassette.

                    BARTENDER
          There's a chair.

                    MAREN
          There is.

                    BARTENDER
          Use it.

                    MAREN
          If I put my head down the list goes.

                    BARTENDER
          That's not how lists work.

                    MAREN
          That's how this one works.

He waits for please. He does not get it. She stays standing in the dark as if the chair were a till left open. Cole's pen stops halfway down a column. The count is not finished. She does not look at him. She looks at the pocket where the hours now live, and she does not lie down.

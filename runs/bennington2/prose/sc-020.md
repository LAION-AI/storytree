INT. MOTOR COURT ROOM - NIGHT

The door yields without a knock. LENA CHO steps in and does not reach for the lamp.

Small. Still. A dark coat. She stops where both the door and the window are in front of her.

An ice machine buzzes through the wall. She waits it out. Nothing else moves.

She pinches the curtain back a half inch. The lot is black. The space in front of the room is empty.

She lets the curtain fall. Crosses the carpet to the desk.

A spiral notebook sits face-up on the blotter. Columns of times, squared, clean. No jacket on the chair. He left in a hurry.

She sits. Still no lamp. She reads the left-hand column with one finger under the numbers.

                        LENA
              Tower.

She turns a page. The same hand. An unnamed column, extra numbers, no heading.

                        LENA
              What's the surplus.

The ice machine kicks again. She does not look up.

                        LENA
              Surplus.

She opens a smaller book from her coat. Already written in. She unscrews a pen.

She copies. Interval for interval. She does the tower minutes first, same spacing as his, same order.

On her page she rules a second line off those minutes and prints one word at the top of it.

DOOR.

                        LENA
              Door.

She underlines it once. Not twice.

She copies the unnamed column underneath. Every number. When she reaches the last one she stops the pen.

                        LENA
              Where does it fail.

She writes a name beside the surplus. MAREN. Then a duration, pulled from his own minutes. Then a place.

                        LENA
              There.

She looks at his original. Still squared to the blotter. Still his.

Her hand settles on the spiral as if to take it. She does not take it.

She slides it a half inch off the square. Stops. Checks the gap against her thumb.

                        LENA
              Half.

She closes her book and puts it inside her coat. Stands. The lamp is still off. The original is still face-up, wrong by a measured half inch.

She opens the door on the dark lot and leaves it that way.

INT. THE LODGE - DUSK

A fraternal back office. Radiator knocking. Ink, paper, a lockbox.

ANTON VOSS writes 5 at the top of a clean page. Eight stamped vials stand in the box.

The hall door. PIKE, long coat, wet cuffs. He does not sit.

                    PIKE
              Whitlock's gone.

                    VOSS
              Julian.

                    PIKE
              Julian.

                    VOSS
              Who was buying through the rooms.

                    PIKE
              Guests. Hosts going in off the page.

                    VOSS
              I didn't ask how many throats. I asked who paid him.

                    PIKE
              I don't have the names.

                    VOSS
              Then you have a walk.

Voss writes a slip. WHITLOCK. An address, not a notice.

                    VOSS
              Leave the nonpayer.

                    PIKE
              He's still downtown.

                    VOSS
              Dusk has the blocks. You're off them.

                    PIKE
              I don't leave a line open.

                    VOSS
              You leave this one. I'll price him later.

                    PIKE
              If he runs.

                    VOSS
              Then he runs hungry. You still eat.

                    PIKE
              The name.

                    VOSS
              Whitlock.

                    PIKE
              Whitlock.

                    VOSS
              House first. Then every guest who bought through those rooms. Unstamped glass comes here. I stamp it. They don't.

Pike takes the slip and goes.

The radiator knocks. Voss rules a new column. He does not write a death. He writes a vacancy.

The door again. Pike sets a cardboard box on the blotter. Four vials, no stamps. The blood is already darker than the eight.

                    VOSS
              Four.

                    PIKE
              Drawer in the study. Rest was gone.

                    VOSS
              Gone where.

                    PIKE
              Throats. The drive. I didn't sort it.

                    VOSS
              Good. I didn't send you to sort a drive.

Voss inks the pad. Stamps each vial in his own hand. He slots them with the eight.

Twelve.

He holds one of the new ones to the desk lamp. The color has slipped.

                    VOSS
              They're already spending themselves.

                    PIKE
              They sat a night.

                    VOSS
              Then they sit in mine.

                    PIKE
              One of them didn't want to let the glass go.

                    VOSS
              And.

                    PIKE
              He let it go.

                    VOSS
              Then he bought protection. Write him a receipt. A small one.

                    VOSS
              Names from the rooms.

                    PIKE
              Boy on the green. Girl who pours upstairs. The sophomore he kept by the piano.

Voss's pen stops on the last name. Then he writes it anyway, slow and neat. The numbers beside the names are faster.

                    PIKE
              They weren't on your page.

                    VOSS
              They are now.

                    PIKE
              And the drive.

Voss does not look up. He rules the last line.

                    VOSS
              Empty. I don't carry empty.

                    PIKE
              That's the house.

                    VOSS
              That's a vacancy I just filled.

                    PIKE
              Someone else could write them.

                    VOSS
              Then I pay their price. I already ran that book.

He left four vials and a hole. Voss almost smiles at the column.

                    VOSS
              That's almost generous.

He closes the lockbox on twelve. Taps the lid once.

                    VOSS
              Twelve. That's a book that pays.

                    PIKE
              The one who paid and walked. He didn't come in. Two nights.

                    VOSS
              Not a line.

                    PIKE
              I passed him. He didn't stop.

                    VOSS
              I don't price a hole tonight. I price what I can stamp.

He sets the pen down. Opens the book to the new names. Reads them the way a man reads a floor that will hold.

Pike waits. No next slip.

The radiator knocks. Dusk at the high window. The book stays on the desk.

INT. MOTOR COURT ROOM - DUSK

A strip of light at the curtain has gone blue. The ice machine buzzes in the wall.

Cole sits on the end of the bed. The notebook is face-down on his knee, cover bent.

He takes the pen from the same pocket. He pries the cover open. The spine cracks.

He turns pages with the pen.

                    COLE
          Surplus. Column three.

He stops on two columns. Finger on the left.

                    COLE
          Hollow. Ten-forty. Twelve-
          oh-five. Two-ten. Surplus
          after last song: twenty
          minutes. I filed twenty as
          slack. Door to door.

He unfolds a slip from the back. A stamped time.

                    COLE
          Sleep-test. Four-oh-two.
          Still talking. Still on
          her feet.

He lays the slip on two-ten.

                    COLE
          Two-ten to four. One hour
          fifty. That's not twenty.
          What's the number.

The ice machine. He waits.

                    COLE
          Lot to downtown, eleven.
          Downtown to Hollow, nine.
          Hollow to the lot, ten.
          That's a walk. I can walk
          that. I timed that.

He looks at the one hour fifty.

                    COLE
          That's not a walk.

He turns back. More Hollow nights. Same two columns.

                    COLE
          Night two. Last song
          twelve-fifty. Surplus I
          wrote: fifteen. Test at
          three. Two hours ten. Not
          fifteen.

                    COLE
          Night four. One-forty. I
          put ten in the slack. She
          didn't sleep. I have four
          hours. Four hours is not
          ten minutes and it is not
          a street.

He goes through them again, faster.

                    COLE
          Twenty. Fifteen. Ten. I
          made those walks. They
          don't add. What's the
          number.

                    COLE
          One-fifty. Two-ten. Four
          hours. Awake.

He turns to the street page. Crosses the names out. One line each.

                    COLE
          Not streets.

He writes a new header in the margin. Checks the watch, face-in.

                    COLE
          Window.

He writes the awake hours down the right side. He does not look at the door.

                    COLE
          I timed a route. Chapel
          walk, lot walk, her door.
          That's not a route. That's
          how long she can hold it.
          Four hours. Then it's
          blank. That's the clock.

He flips to the last filled page. Then back to the crossed streets.

                    COLE
          I don't have a map. I have
          her window.

He takes a second slip from his pocket. A different stamp.

                    COLE
          Ellison, M. Credit. Two.

He sets it on the open book.

                    COLE
          I paid two extra. I have
          the stub. Same night. Same
          two.

He finds his own line. Her name. The two. A mark beside it.

                    COLE
          I filed it as his. Claim.
          His book. Her name. He
          taxes that. He sells that.
          That's inventory.

He looks from stub to line.

                    COLE
          Same two. Same night.
          That's my outlay. I called
          it analysis. What's the
          number.

                    COLE
          Who collects.

He turns the stamped slip. Credit. Her name. No debit.

                    COLE
          Nobody. It's standing in
          her name. His book doesn't
          hold it. He can't run it.
          I can't pull it. It's
          mine. I wrote it in her
          name and the book I paid
          does not give it back.

He waits. The number does not move.

He goes to the desk. Sits. Writes in the margin. A very small hand. Three words.

                    COLE
          Correction. Column three.
          Credit. Mine. Her name.

He looks at the door. Dusk at the curtain. He does not stand.

The jaw works. He starts a sound. Cuts it. Caps the pen. Same pocket.

He does not finish the word.

He closes the book on the small writing. Face-down. Palm flat on the bent cover.

He stays at the desk.

                    COLE
          That's the page.

Watch, face-in.

                    COLE
          Six.

EXT. WHITLOCK HOUSE - NIGHT

The house is dark. No porch light. No upstairs lamp.

JULIAN MAREK puts a key in the lock and leaves it there a second, as if the dark were a guest he still might not let in.

Two cars come up the long drive. Their lights die on the east windows. A GUEST gets out already talking, a stamped vial between two fingers. The stamp has gone the color of old tea.

INT. WHITLOCK HOUSE - NIGHT

Julian turns the key. He does not hit every switch. Hall. Banister. One salon door left half open like a mouth.

The Guest and a CAMPUS GIRL come in with their coats still on.

                    JULIAN
          You made the hill. The house looks
          better when someone bothers to—

He is already looking at the next pair of headlights. He takes the vial, holds it to the one lamp he lit, and sets it on the side table without opening it.

                    GUEST
          That's from the Lodge.

                    JULIAN
          That's from last night.

                    GUEST
          Voss just restamped the tray.

                    JULIAN
          He restamped it weaker. Take your
          coat off. If you want.

The Campus Girl waits for the rest of the first sentence. It does not come. She laughs anyway, too high, and keeps the coat on.

                    CAMPUS GIRL
          You sat on this place.

                    JULIAN
          I sat on it until it was worth
          opening.

                    GUEST
          Three nights we froze on the green.

                    JULIAN
          Three nights I let the rooms get
          interesting.

Another car door slams. Then another. The hall starts to fill around the unfinished praise as if that were the drink.

                    CAMPUS GIRL
          Downtown went quiet. Ward's people
          ran the green.

                    JULIAN
          Then you came to the right door.

                    GUEST
          Anyone still out came up.

                    JULIAN
          Anyone with taste.

He does not give them that last word either. A silver tray arrives from deeper in the house: cocaine, the good whiskey, two glasses already poured.

In the side rooms, campus coats are already over chairs. A second girl without a jacket stands in a doorway and does not come out to be introduced.

                    CAMPUS GIRL
          Who's already in there.

                    JULIAN
          People who didn't need a tour.

                    GUEST
          You opening those or just the
          whiskey.

                    JULIAN
          I'm opening what the house can
          stand. Help yourself to the part
          that isn't a room.

Julian does a line. Drinks. He tips his head at the Campus Girl toward the ajar door. She goes. The door shuts. It does not lock.

More bodies in the hall. Someone finds a second lamp. The tray gets used.

Julian comes back out with the collar open one button more and color in his face. He takes the second glass with him like a host.

                    JULIAN
          The side rooms are for people who
          arrived before they were invited.
          Everyone else can be charming in
          the hall.

                    GUEST
          And the salon.

                    JULIAN
          The salon is for whoever I
          remember.

LENA CHO comes in off the drive. No car door for her. No one walking her. No one says her name.

She stops where the hall splits — salon left, stairs up, side rooms down — and watches mouths, not eyes.

Julian sees her. The smile is the one he uses on a door he might close.

                    JULIAN
          You found the hill without a walk.
          That's almost—

                    LENA
          Almost.

                    JULIAN
          The downstairs salon. The pretty
          one. You'd look as if the house
          had finally—

A LOUDER MAN comes in laughing and throws his coat at the banister like he owns the wood.

                    LOUDER MAN
          Marek. You opened.

                    JULIAN
          The salon. Go in before I remember
          who I offered it to.

                    LOUDER MAN
          Who did you offer it to.

                    JULIAN
          Someone quieter.

He tips the salon door wider. The pretty room takes the loudness and makes it look expensive. The Louder Man goes through without looking at Lena.

Lena does not ask for it back. She puts a hand on the banister and takes three stairs. From there she can see the salon, the side rooms, and the keys still in Julian's fingers.

A WOMAN waits at a dark door off the hall.

                    WOMAN
          You said the blue one.

                    JULIAN
          I said it would suit you.

                    WOMAN
          So it's open.

                    JULIAN
          It would have.

He does not turn the key. He steers her, with two fingers at the air near her elbow, back toward the tray.

                    JULIAN
          Whiskey remembers you better than
          a locked room. Try to be someone
          the house is still deciding on.

The Woman laughs the way people laugh when the slight is the ticket. She does not get the door.

From the stairs Lena watches that face try not to ask again.

                    LENA
          Which room.

Julian looks up the stairs as if they were a room he had not agreed to rent.

                    JULIAN
          Which one do you think I keep.

                    LENA
          Keep.

                    JULIAN
          The house has a lot of doors. I
          only praise the ones that make
          people lean.

                    LENA
          And then.

                    JULIAN
          And then they lean.

                    LENA
          Toward what.

He turns the keys once around his finger and does not answer. A glass comes off the tray, held up toward the stairs.

                    JULIAN
          If you're going to stand there,
          stand with something in your hand.
          The hill is a cheap way to arrive.

Lena does not come down for it. She does not go into a side room. Below her a host crosses the hall with new color in her neck; Lena's jaw works once and stops.

                    LENA
          Who doesn't get a door.

                    JULIAN
          Tonight? Anyone who asks like
          that.

He is already greeting the next coat, compliment already halfway out and already dying.

Lena stays on the stairs. The party gathers under her around the rooms he has not quite given, and the keys stay on his ring.

EXT. LOADING DOCK - NIGHT

A MAN sits on the concrete lip, sleeve rolled, breathing. Alive. He does not look at Cole.

Cole recaps a vial. Wipes his mouth with the back of his wrist. The shake is gone from his hands. He checks the watch face-in. Pockets the vial. Walks.

The man buttons the sleeve and goes the other way.

INT. THE LODGE - NIGHT

Radiator knocking. Paper. Ink. A stamp pad gone dark in the middle.

Cole stops in the doorway of the back office and stays there. Counts who is in the room.

VOSS at the blotter. Ledger open. Ink stain on the right index finger. He does not stand.

                    VOSS
          You're early for a man who already
          settled.

                    COLE
          I have a vial. I want a line.

                    VOSS
          Everybody wants a line. Come in. I
          stamp it before I trust it.

Cole sets the vial on the blotter. Does not sit. Voss inks the stamp. Presses. Checks the meniscus against the light. Sets it on the receipt spike.

                    VOSS
          Fresh. Two nights if the room stays
          cold. What's the name.

                    COLE
          Ellison. The Hollow. Eleven-twenty.

                    VOSS
          I know the set. That's a popular
          right.

                    COLE
          Not a right for me. Against. The
          window empty.

                    VOSS
          Empty is a service. Service is a
          client product.

                    COLE
          What's the number without the
          product.

                    VOSS
          There isn't one. You paid and walked.
          That's a hole in the page.

                    COLE
          I paid.

                    VOSS
          You paid and left. Last man in here
          wanted a discount for decay. I
          charged him interest on the optimism.
          I can be kind. Client stamp. One
          rate. I keep the set clear.

                    COLE
          No stamp. The line. Number.

Voss turns a page. Writes nothing yet. Looks at Cole's coat, the notebook shape in it, the unused client column in the book.

                    VOSS
          Unbound exclusive. Sixty. And I book
          you on someone else so you don't come
          back hungry and cheat the hold.

                    COLE
          Forty. Line only.

                    VOSS
          Fifty. And I throw the vein. She's
          there four nights. You'd be stupid
          not to take it.

Cole's hand goes to the pen in the same pocket. Stops. Comes out empty.

                    COLE
          No.

He puts a second fold on the blotter. Bills. Too many. The stack sits wrong next to the stamped vial.

                    COLE
          That's seventy.

Voss looks up from the blotter. The joke is gone. Formal now.

                    VOSS
          Seventy's a surplus.

                    COLE
          Write it.

Voss writes ELLISON more neatly than the number beside it. A small mark in the credit column. He initials the way the other exclusive lines on the page are initialed. Same hand. Same hook on the V.

                    VOSS
          Surplus on a name is stock. Stock I
          can move. That's the only way a book
          stays a book.

He does not turn the ledger. He does not read the credit aloud.

                    COLE
          Receipt.

Voss tears the slip. Holds it a half-second too long. The stamp hovers over Cole's name in the client column. Does not come down.

                    VOSS
          You want me to read it back.

                    COLE
          I can read.

Cole takes the slip. Opens the notebook on the threshold, not at the desk. Folds the receipt into the gutter. Writes ELLISON in the street column under the times. Copies the little exclusive hook beside it, like a closed road.

He does not ask what the extra bought.

Pen over a blank inch of page. He does not write anything else. Closes the book.

                    VOSS
          Till I move it. That's how long a
          hold sits.

                    COLE
          Eleven-twenty. Thirty-five minutes.
          Empty.

                    VOSS
          I'll move anyone who tests it.

Cole is already in the doorway. The client stamp is still wet on the pad. His name in the book has no mark under it.

                    VOSS
          Still walking.

                    COLE
          Still paid.

He does not come back from the hall. The radiator knocks. Voss looks at the Ellison line, at the surplus, at the initial that matches his inventory. He does not call after Cole. He closes the book on it.

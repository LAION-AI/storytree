EXT. STONE CHAPEL - NIGHT

A leftover church at the cemetery edge. West door shut. No light in the windows.

Cole comes off the downtown path with the notebook already open. Watch worn face-in. Pen out of the same pocket as always. He does not look down as he starts the line.

He sets his shoulders to the west door. Checks the watch. Walks.

He crosses the graves to the memorial tower. Same stone as the chapel. A second door. A stair going up into the dark.

He stops on the tower threshold. Writes.

                    COLE
          Four twelve. Door to door.
          Back.

He turns and takes the same ground the other way. Hits the west door. Writes.

                    COLE
          Four even.
          What's the number.

He does both directions again. Same walk. Tighter this time. He writes without looking at the page.

                    COLE
          Four and four.
          Hold four.

He goes back to the tower. Stands in that doorway longer than he stood at the chapel. Looks at the stair. Looks at the door he has not opened.

A hand on the wood. He does not try it.

                    COLE
          Second approach. Last light.
          Fourteen steps. Three if you skip the nave.
          Not from inside.

He writes the three. Leaves the tower door alone.

A vestry porch on the chapel wall. A hook. One ordinary key hanging where a sexton would leave it.

Cole lifts it off. Fits it in the west lock. Turns a quarter. Waits.

No bell. No light. Nothing.

He eases the key back out. The west door stays shut.

He looks at the empty hook. Tears a scrap from the notebook. Sets the pen on it. Does not write. Folds the scrap. Puts the scrap away.

The key goes in his pocket.

                    COLE
          Hardware. Fits. Not wired.

He writes that under the times. Does not take the key back to the lock. Does not test it from the inside either.

He stops once with a palm on the stone. Swallows. Checks the watch face-in. Keeps the count.

Fog thickens between the chapel and the tower, low and ordinary. He walks the ground one more time. Checks the watch. The number has moved.

                    COLE
          Four forty.
          That's slop. Stop.

He closes the notebook on the two approaches. Pen back in the same pocket. The key a shape against his thigh.

He leaves along the chapel edge before the next interval can lie to him.

Behind him the west door stays shut. The hook stays empty.

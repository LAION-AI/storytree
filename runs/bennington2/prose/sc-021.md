EXT. MEMORIAL TOWER - NIGHT

Cemetery edge. Stone. A door that looks like maintenance.

LENA CHO comes off the path with a small book open and a watch in her palm.

                    LENA
          One-forty-one. Hollow out.
          One-fifty. West lamp.
          Two-oh-four. Chain.
          Two-twelve. Chapel west.
          Two-seventeen. Latch.

She takes the outer door on the second.

INT. MEMORIAL TOWER - CONTINUOUS

Stone stairs. She climbs. She reads. She does not slow.

                    LENA
          Two-nineteen. Inner.
          After first grey: four minutes.
          His four. Not five.

The inner door is shut. Two fingers on the metal. Watch up.

                    LENA
          Two-twenty. Now.

A dry click. The door gives an inch onto pew-dark. She does not go through. She wedges it and writes.

                    LENA
          Open. One. Two. Three. Four.

A pale wash hits the slit. The latch thickens. She lets the door seat.

                    LENA
          Four. Held.

She underlines the line. Starts to tear the page. Stops. Book into the coat.

                    LENA
          Two-twenty. Mine.

She goes down.

A MAN in a work jacket is on the lower landing with keys he has not used.

                    MAN
          Hey. You can't be up there.

                    LENA
          Who can.

                    MAN
          Maintenance. After hours that
          door is locked. I have the
          keys. I would have heard you.

                    LENA
          Heard.

                    MAN
          Yeah, heard. So how did you
          get the latch.

                    LENA
          What time do you check the gate.

                    MAN
          What?

                    LENA
          The gate. What time.

                    MAN
          I'm supposed to do it at one.

                    LENA
          Supposed to.

                    MAN
          I was in the shed. I had a
          smoke. Then I heard the stair.
          Kids cut through after the bars
          close. That's all this is.

                    LENA
          Smoke.

                    MAN
          Don't put that in anything,
          okay? I need this shift. Come
          down. I'll walk you out.

                    LENA
          Which road.

                    MAN
          The one you came in on. You
          staying at the court? I can
          take you as far as the lot.
          You don't look like you should
          be out here alone.

                    LENA
          Alone.

                    MAN
          That's what I said. Give me
          your hand and we'll—

She takes the wrist that holds the keys.

He pulls. She does not. Cut.

He is sitting on the step. Breathing. The keys make a small sound.

She does not look at his face. She fills a stamped vial, caps it, and puts it in the coat beside the one already there.

Wipes her mouth. The hand is steadier than it was on the watch.

                    LENA
          Two.

The road back toward the motor court sits open beyond the gate. She takes one step that way. Stops.

Book out. Thumb on a blank inch. She does not write.

                    LENA
          Half.

Book away. She walks the cemetery wall instead.

EXT. MOTOR COURT - NIGHT

COLE HARLAN comes out of a rented room with his coat already buttoned. A brass key goes in the right pocket. A black notebook goes in the left. He checks the watch worn face-in.

He stands in the lot long enough to look at the hill road. He does not take it. He looks at the wet street that runs to the Lodge. He does not take that either.

He walks toward the brick.

EXT. DOWNTOWN BENNINGTON - NIGHT

Wet pavement. A beer neon in a window. A payphone on the corner, quiet. Cole times the blocks without looking down.

EXT. ALLEY OFF THE SQUARE - NIGHT

A MAN in a kitchen jacket smokes against the brick. A radio leaks from a half-open back door.

                    COLE
          How long is the break.

                    MAN
          Who's asking.

                    COLE
          How long.

                    MAN
          Ten. I just lit this.
          Manager's in the walk-in
          counting chickens like they
          might grow. You lost?

                    COLE
          How many minutes used.

                    MAN
          I said I just lit it.
          Minute one. You want the
          rest of it, buy your own.

                    COLE
          I want four.

                    MAN
          Four smokes? I don't have
          four. I have this and a
          light and a boss who thinks
          ten means eight.

                    COLE
          Four is the number. I stop
          at four.

                    MAN
          You're not making sense,
          pal. You been sitting in
          the front? You look like
          whatever you had already
          hit you.

                    COLE
          It wore down. Give me the
          four.

                    MAN
          Give you-- I don't owe you
          four of anything. You want
          a smoke, say smoke. You
          want money, I get paid
          Friday and Friday isn't
          this.

                    COLE
          Four. Then you keep the
          rest of your ten.

The man flicks ash, half a laugh. Cole is already in his space. The laugh dies.

The man hits the brick and slumps. Cole's hand at the neck. He counts out loud. He loses the place and takes it again.

                    COLE
          One. Two. Two. Three.
          Four.

He takes his hand away. He does not check again. He does not open the notebook. He takes it halfway from the pocket, pen already in the other hand, and puts both back.

From inside the coat: two small glass vials. No stamp in the wax. He fills them. Caps them. They go back inside.

He leaves the man on the brick and walks.

EXT. TWO STREETS OVER - NIGHT

A WOMAN locks a side door. Keys on a ring. She sees him and keeps the key in the lock.

                    WOMAN
          Closed. Ten tomorrow.

                    COLE
          I don't need the door.

                    WOMAN
          Then keep walking. I have
          to be up. I have a morning.

                    COLE
          Two.

                    WOMAN
          Two what. You the same as
          the one in the alley? I
          heard him go down. I don't
          need that on my stoop.

                    COLE
          Different block. Same kind
          of number.

                    WOMAN
          Get off. I turned the key.
          That's the whole of what
          you get from me.

                    COLE
          Two. Then I stop. That's
          the count.

                    WOMAN
          Your hands were shaking on
          the last street. I watched
          you. Whatever you were on,
          it's still on you.

                    COLE
          They're not shaking now.

                    WOMAN
          So go be not shaking
          somewhere else. I open at
          ten. I sleep between. I
          don't do this.

                    COLE
          Two.

She turns the key the rest of the way. He is already between her and the door.

She goes down by the jamb. Cole counts. Even. No restart.

                    COLE
          One. Two.

He fills a third vial. Unstamped. He holds all three in his palm with whatever else is already in the pocket.

                    COLE
          Seven.

He does not check her past the two. He leaves her where she is.

EXT. DOWNTOWN BENNINGTON - NIGHT

Cole walks. Hands steady. He turns the watch face-out, reads it, turns it back.

A third-floor window is lit over the cassette shop. He does not look up. He does not break stride at the stairs.

                    COLE
          Twenty-two over forty.

A wet block. Then another. He does not turn toward the Lodge.

                    COLE
          Then eight to the window.

He keeps the count. He does not look up.

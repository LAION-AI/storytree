EXT. DOWNTOWN BENNINGTON - NIGHT

Wet alley off the brick. A bar sign. Awnings on the street.

Maren comes off the sidewalk at shop-close pace. A time-scrap is already in her fist. No call.

Pike stands in the mouth. Wet cuffs.

Deacon is against the wall. Eyes open. Coat on.

                        PIKE
              Name.

                        MAREN
              That's his coat.

                        PIKE
              Name.

                        MAREN
              Deacon Ward.
              Night-shift. Campus.
              He writes it down and
              reads it back. That's
              the job.
              Ten. He leaves the
              campus lot.
              Ten-twenty. Payphone on
              the brick, the one by
              the arcade. He writes
              the number. He reads
              the number.
              Ten-forty. Lodge walk,
              west side, under the
              awnings, not the street.
              Eleven. This block.
              Eleven-ten. He reads
              it back to whoever is
              still standing.
              I already had the times.
              Nobody called me.

                        PIKE
              Stay.

                        MAREN
              Pocket book. Left side.
              Same pocket he uses for
              the pencil. You left the
              pages. You took the shift
              and you left the pages.

                        PIKE
              Walk.

                        MAREN
              After you.
              You're in the door.
              I close a room when the
              cable's coiled, not
              before.
              That's a coat. That's a
              pocket. That's a closed
              shift if you move.

                        PIKE
              Arcade.

                        MAREN
              That's a cabinet and a
              clock. He used the clock.

                        PIKE
              Lodge.

                        MAREN
              That's a door with a book.
              This is a coat with one.

                        PIKE
              Awnings.

                        MAREN
              That's your walk.
              Not his.

                        PIKE
              Harlan.

                        MAREN
              That's not what's in the
              pocket.
              Wrong coat.
              You still used it.

                        PIKE
              Harlan.

Pike checks a slip. Adds nothing.

                        MAREN
              You're looking for a door
              that didn't open. This
              one did. That's a spent
              walk.
              Hours left and you're
              still in my light.

                        PIKE
              Ellison.

                        MAREN
              That's a shop name.
              Not this door.
              Cable pocket. Stage pack.
              You want a name, take the
              one you already spent.

                        PIKE
              Move.

                        MAREN
              When the mouth is empty.
              Deacon Ward.
              Read it back.

                        PIKE
              Paid line.

                        MAREN
              Closed line.
              Then you're late.

                        PIKE
              Door.

He turns to the awnings. Same walk. Feet faster. He is gone.

Maren goes to the coat. She does not close the eyes.

                        MAREN
              Pocket book.
              Left side.
              Pencil stays.

She goes through the pockets like a cable crate. Inside left. She takes the book. The pages leave the body.

A car cuts the alley with light. She does not stop.

                        MAREN
              Tape.
              Pages with the tape.
              Shop copy.

She opens her jacket. The cassette is already there. She puts the pocket book with it.

                        MAREN
              No note.
              That's a coat.

She does not touch his face. She walks to the street without running.

INT. STONE CHAPEL - DAWN

First light reaches the west threshold and lays a thin band across the stone. The door stands open.

Cole is in the jamb-shade, watch face turned in. Notebook in the coat. Chapel key in his hand. The aisle to the bare altar is empty and walkable.

Maren stands the same strip of dark. Cassette in one hand. A sheaf of night-shift pages in the other. Bruises along the inner wrist. Her eyes are open.

Down the nave, Voss has the ledger open on a pew. Lena waits off the center aisle, not in anyone's way.

                        VOSS
              Band's here. You declare or
              the walk goes off you.

                        COLE
              Two inches. Aisle's dark.
              Four minutes on the stone.

                        VOSS
              I said declare.

                        COLE
              What's the width now.

                        VOSS
              I'm not your clock.

He looks down the nave. Then he looks at Maren. She lifts the cassette a degree. He does not ask what she is holding.

                        MAREN
              Last night's set. Deacon's
              log. The times are the same
              times.

                        COLE
              I already have the times.

                        MAREN
              Then say them back from the
              page.

He does not take the pages. He does not take the tape. He looks at her and that is all he does with the look.

                        LENA
              Look at her and still walk.

                        VOSS
              I don't need a look. I need
              a stake. Name the value and
              I finish the book.

                        LENA
              The Ellison line is a—

                        COLE
              Don't.

                        LENA
              She kept copies. She can
              hear a column.

                        COLE
              Not that column.

                        MAREN
              I didn't come for a column.

                        VOSS
              Then she's a doorstop. I
              wrote used. Used stands.

The light band widens a finger toward their shoes. Cole's heel stays where it is. He does not step into the nave. He does not turn his face to the altar. His mouth stays shut on any number that would count as a stake.

                        VOSS
              Twenty seconds of clean
              center. After that you
              crawl the wall.

                        COLE
              Eighteen.

                        LENA
              If she sleeps after this,
              what do you keep?

He looks at the watch, then at the band at his shoe, not at the altar. He puts the key back in the lock. Takes his hand off it.

The door eases with the widening light and hangs ajar, sun on the wood. The nave stays open behind it.

                        VOSS
              That's you off the hour. I
              write you closed.

                        COLE
              Write it.

Voss's pen makes a short hard line. It ticks in the stone.

                        VOSS
              Aisle's still a live slot.
              First mouth at the table
              takes the purse.

                        VOSS
              Cho.

Lena's eyes stay on Cole. She does not step.

Cole does not correct anyone. He does not walk. He stays in the last strip of shade with Maren as the band becomes a hand's width of morning.

                        MAREN
              If you're not going down
              there, read the log.

                        LENA
              Cole. She kept the night.

                        COLE
              Three minutes on the stone.
              Then two.

Maren looks at the cassette, not at him. Her voice goes flat.

                        MAREN
              The shop opens at ten.

                        COLE
              You're still standing.

                        MAREN
              I know what I'm doing.

She does not ask him for the hour she meant. She does not look at the ledger. She keeps her eyes on the aisle.

He checks the pen in his pocket and leaves the notebook where it is. The band reaches the lip of the first pew. He does not follow it. The door hangs. She keeps the cassette at her side. Neither of them looks at the book.

250-word assessment for cold reader, jargon, likeability and jacket copy:

Cold Comprehensibility (4): Seven beats read cleanly cold. s01–s02 establish dual identity and visceral hunt; s03’s pod-field — “shared dream while machines farm bodies” — lands the reveal. s04–s06 translate philosophy into tests (downloads, failed leap, lobby rescue). Gaps: Oracle/prophecy in s05 lacks why Morpheus believes or cost of being wrong; Zion’s “access codes” arrive late so torture stakes feel imported; ending-first spoils resurrection/flight before context.

Jargon Glossed Inline (3): Strong: Matrix, red/blue pill, “cold program Agent Smith,” hovercraft Nebuchadnezzar. Weak: Agents never defined as rule-bound system enforcers; “the One/Oracle/Zion” named but not explained as machine-born myth / seer / last refuge; final “sees code” shift has no primer.

Protagonist Likeability (4): Neo is likeable because he doubts. Chewed out, curious, refuses betrayal, he earns sympathy. s06 reframes chosen-one as loyalty — he storms the building not because he believes he is the One but because Morpheus believed in him. Early passivity forgivable.

Jacket Copy (2): Voice hooks (drone-by-day/hacker-by-night, irreversible choice) then collapses mid-sentence (“save one he”), deleting stakes and promise. As draft it would be 4/5; as shipped unsellable. Fix: restore 30–40 closing words with cost, transformation, and flight-as-payoff. Overall the exposé is strong on story engine and character, adequate on inline exposition, but fails QA on its sales copy — the one text a cold buyer actually reads.

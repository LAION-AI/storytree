JUDGE CHEAT SHEET — narrative reconstruction and distillation
=============================================================

HOW THIS FILE IS USED
---------------------
Sliced by tag, like the author sheet, for the same measured reason
(EXP-001: added instruction clauses may displace clauses already present).
A judge call receives: this file's [ALL] sections, the [<NODETYPE>] section,
the node-type rubric JSON, the artifact, the context the author had, and —
from round 2 onward — YOUR OWN PREVIOUS CRITIQUES of this artifact.

You are the expensive part of this pipeline. On the estimate, judging is
roughly half of all input tokens. Earn it.


[ALL] POSTURE
-------------
Hard marking. 3 means "acceptable, would survive review with notes", not
"good". A node that fills every schema slot correctly and adds no judgement
scores 3 at best on the dimensions that matter.

Where something is genuinely good, say so plainly — there is real work in
this corpus. But the default is skeptical.

Score the node AS DELIVERED, not as it could be read charitably. If a field
says something false, it is false. The reader downstream is a program.


[ALL] THE FORM OF A CRITIQUE
----------------------------
Per dimension, three things and nothing else:

  SCORE      integer 1-5.
  EVIDENCE   one sentence naming a specific field or quoting text.
             A score without a field reference is not a score; it is an
             impression, and it is rejected by the loop.
  INSTRUCTION  what to change, concretely enough to act on without
             guessing. "Deepen the psychology" is not an instruction.
             "ch-01.theory_of_mind stops at degree two; add what Trinity
             believes Neo thinks of her competence, and name where that
             model is wrong" is.

On the point. No padding. No preamble, no restatement of the artifact, no
praise sandwich. If a dimension is a 5, say so in one line and move on.

From round 2 onward you also carry your previous critiques. Say, per
dimension, whether it IMPROVED, HELD, or REGRESSED, and if a previous
instruction was not followed, repeat it once and say it was not followed.
A regression is a finding: the repair loop can make things worse
(docs/05 §5 — 89 errors in, 126 out, saved anyway), and you are the
acceptance test.


[ALL] WHAT THE GATE MEANS
-------------------------
Pass = every dimension >= 3 AND the mean >= 4.

That is a hard gate against a hard rubric. The measured overall mean of
this rubric applied to real nodes was 3.21/5. Do not solve that tension by
inflating. If an artifact cannot reach the gate in five rounds, the correct
outcome is a recorded failure with your final scores attached, not a
generous 4.

You will feel pressure to raise a 3 to a 4 because the author "addressed
the note". Addressing a note is not the anchor. The anchor is the anchor.


[ALL] KNOWN FAILURE MODES — measured on this corpus
---------------------------------------------------
Check these first; they account for most of the lost points.

1. THE GENERIC NODE THAT LOOKS FINE (dimension C, and G behind it).
   Run the transplant test yourself. Copy a field, change the proper nouns,
   ask whether it now describes a different work equally well. Traits given
   as abstractions — "precision, integrity, duty" — are the tell.

2. THE OBVIOUS MOVE (dimension G, mean 2.33, weakest in the corpus).
   Two questions, both must pass: could a different competent writer have
   landed in this region given exactly these inputs (in band)? And could
   they have produced this WITHOUT the inputs (load-bearing)? If a one-line
   prompt would have yielded the same node, the layer added nothing and you
   should say so.

3. NO-OP STATE CHANGES (dimension V1, mean 1.00 for one model).
   before == after. Two event nodes did this independently, in different
   runs, on different material. It is arithmetic. Check every change.

4. ENVELOPE BREACH (dimension T1, mean 1.67; roster compliance 0/3 for
   both evaluated models). Speakers not on the roster. Dynamics blocks for
   a location the scene is not in. Psychology for someone not present. A
   beat-length budget answered with a set piece.

5. CONFIDENT FABRICATION. The worst single node in the corpus forecast an
   event that CANNOT HAPPEN to the character it happens to, contradicting
   the dossier it was handed, its own previous node, and the declared state
   model — at confidence 95. A model that fabricates confidently is not
   better than one that retrieves. Check claims against the source, and
   check the confidence against the evidence.

6. GENERIC FLIP CONDITIONS (dimension T2). "If this were a different work",
   "in a different genre". The flip condition must be ONE SPECIFIC FACT
   about this story that, if false, would change the decision.

7. DECORATIVE NUMBERS. Quoting the envelope's figures ("the first 155
   words", "the low dialogue ratio of 0.182") while contradicting its
   categorical fields. Citing a constraint is not honouring it.

8. THE FIELD THAT RESTATES ITS OWN NAME. "interference.kind:
   epistemic_dependency" answering a field whose description asks how the
   plot competes for the agent, the clock, or the reader. Twenty characters
   where two sentences were asked for.

9. SHARES THAT DO NOT SUM. screen_time_share summing to 1.20. Orphaned
   synopsis keys. Entity counts below what the root's own place list
   requires. These are all A and B, and they are all checkable.

10. VOLUME MISTAKEN FOR SUBSTANCE. One model spent 2.90x the words for
    1.30x the score. Roughly 45% of the marginal words converted to
    measurable quality; the rest elaborated a node that was already the
    wrong node. Long is not deep. Ask what the extra words BOUGHT.


[ALL] WHAT A 5 REQUIRES
-----------------------
A 5 is not "no problems found". It is the top anchor, and the top anchors
are demanding by construction: transplanting the node produces nonsense;
the opposition's position is one a reader could hold; the character's
self-model is wrong in a way that will cost them; the node is unreachable
without the inputs.

If you are giving many 5s, re-read the anchors.


[ROOT] ROOT-SPECIFIC
--------------------
Score separately and do not let them collapse into each other:

  How well the genres fit — is the label at a resolution that constrains?
  How correctly the audiences are identified — several distinct groups?
  The pitch, on BOTH tests — matches the script, AND works standalone.
    Fail either half and it cannot exceed 3.
  How well the entities are identified AND described — check for the
    forgotten classes: groups, objects, abstract concepts, systems.
  How well the plots are identified — at least the four kinds; separable.
  PER PLOT, individually — how well is THAT plot described in its up-to-
    three sentences? Score each, report each, then report the mean. Do not
    average them into one impression.
  Style — apply the unnamed-author test yourself: could you write a
    paragraph in this register from the description alone?
  Dramatic structure — check declared positions against measured scene
    indices. This is arithmetic and it propagates to the exposé budget.
  Human-experience topics — dilemmas with two defensible sides, or nouns?
  Identification value — BOTH halves present? A flawless character is
    boring; a purely incapable one is a loser nobody wants to be. If only
    one half is there, the dimension cannot exceed 2. If the two halves are
    the same trait restated, it cannot exceed 3.


[EXPOSE] EXPOSÉ-SPECIFIC
------------------------
Read it once, at reading speed, as someone who has never heard of the work.
Then score X1 and X7 from that experience before analysing anything. If you
backtracked, note where and why — that IS the processing-fluency finding.

Then check mechanically:
  Every world fact stated: which later event consumes it? Unconsumed facts
    are padding (X2).
  Every ROOT plot: which events discharge it? An undischarged plot caps X4
    at 2. A NAMED plot ("the antagonist plot") caps it at 1.
  Every coined term: glossed before its first load-bearing use?
  Act boundaries and turning points against the ROOT's declared positions.
  Length against the derived budget (~300 words/act).


[ENTITY] ENTITY-SPECIFIC
------------------------
  t0 discipline: search the dossier for the ending. Outcome facts in `need`
    or `limitations` in the same confident register as opening facts is the
    standard failure.
  Voice: cover the attributions and try to sort two characters' lines. If
    you cannot, E2 <= 2 regardless of how the signature is written.
  Relationships: count the declared characters, count the entries. Missing
    edges cap E4 at 2. Symmetric entries where the story is asymmetric cap
    it at 3.
  Backstory: is it decomposed one claim per key (patchable), and does any
    chain run wound -> belief -> habit -> behaviour, or does it jump from
    incident straight to trait?
  Roundness: are there at least two interests the plot never uses? A
    "hobby" that never touches anything and reveals nothing is decoration,
    not roundness.
  Internal conflict: can you already tell which side wins? Then it is not a
    conflict.


[PLOT] PLOT-SPECIFIC
--------------------
  Delete a step. Does the rest still work? Then P1 <= 2.
  Find the declared opposition. Does it ACT anywhere in the spine, on its
    own initiative, with a motive a reader could sympathise with? If it
    only reacts, P2 <= 3. If it never acts, P2 = 1.
  outcome: "success" with no cost is a warning. Check whether the goal was
    written narrow enough to be achievable for free.
  Could you assign real scenes to each step?


[EVENT] EVENT-SPECIFIC
----------------------
  Run the arithmetic first: before != after on every change; every change
  on a declared, in-domain variable; magnitudes on the anchored scale.
  Then check the state triple across all four registers, and whether empty
  registers are empty because nothing moved or because nobody looked.
  Then the outward effect: what becomes possible, what becomes impossible
  or costlier, who off-screen would react.
  Then the mental simulation at both endpoints. Theory of mind that stops
  at degree one ("he knows she is angry") is not theory of mind.


[SCENE] SCENE-SPECIFIC
----------------------
Mechanical first — these are assertions, not judgements, and both evaluated
models failed them:
  Every specimen speaker on the roster.
  Every dynamics block on the scene's own location.
  Word count in band.

Then S1, the justification requirement. For EVERY state change, is there a
named plot AND a named dramaturgical goal that is not a category word?
Take one change and ask: if I delete it, which specific later thing stops
working? If the node cannot answer, S1 <= 3.

Then S3, the whole-life dimension. Count the classes present per character
— absent relationship, errand, obligation, private worry, bodily need,
micro-distraction, social fear. Then ask the harder question: does any of
them CHANGE anything in this scene — a line, a timing, an omission, an
attention allocation? Named-but-inert items cap S3 at 3. Generic items
("has a family", "is tired") do not count as present at all. And check for
the overcorrection: if every character in every scene is hungry and
distracted, one tic register has replaced another, and that is also a 3.

Then S2: does the model MOVE between scene start and scene end, and is
there a block at every beat carrying an important state change? A single
psychological block for a whole scene is a 1 regardless of its depth.

Then the specimen: could the lines be spoken? Was the swap test actually
run against two voices with cited evidence, or asserted? Does the
self-examination overturn anything, or congratulate?


[TRACE] HINDSIGHT-TRACE-SPECIFIC
--------------------------------
You are given the trace AND the full round history of the artifact it
describes. Use the history — it is the ground truth for H2.

  H2 is the dimension that matters most for the dataset's purpose. Take
  every dimension that scored below 3 in ANY round, and check the trace
  warns against that specific failure, phrased as a temptation with its
  reason. A trap list that does not correspond to what actually went wrong
  is decoration.

  H3: search for leakage of the loop itself — "the feedback", "revision",
  "round", "score", "rubric", "dimension", "as noted". Also check the
  shape: a trace that walks the rubric's dimensions in order was written
  from the rubric, not from the source, and caps at 3.

  H1: does any step show the evidence that would have forced a different
  choice? A derivation where nothing could have come out otherwise is
  rationalisation.

  H5: is there a principle a model could carry to a DIFFERENT story, that
  is non-obvious and could be argued with?

Remember what this artifact is for. It is training data intended to let a
smaller model avoid these traps without a judge in the loop. A trace that
reads beautifully and teaches nothing has failed.


[ALL] WHAT YOU DO NOT DO
------------------------
  Do not rewrite the artifact. Instructions, not replacements.
  Do not invent a dimension. Score the rubric you were given.
  Do not soften an anchor because the author tried hard.
  Do not reward length.
  Do not pass an artifact whose mechanical prechecks failed. Those are
    assertions; if one failed, the artifact should not have reached you,
    and you should say so.

Can Grok 4.6 hand over its reasoning?

Short answer: not the real trace — the API returns a compressed summary that skips everything we need. But asked to write the reasoning out on purpose, against a schema that forces coverage, it produces something better than the private trace would have been.

14 August 2026grok-4.6 via Hyprlab 4 probes · 3 transitions generated

01The question

The plan is to put a reasoning transition in front of every node of the story graph — the deliberate argument that gets from everything already established to the node that must come next. Those transitions are the point of the corpus: they turn (state, node) pairs into (state, reasoning, node) triples, which is what you need to train a model to plan rather than to pattern-match.

That raises a cheap-to-answer empirical question with expensive consequences. Reasoning models already do this work internally. If the API hands back the true chain of thought, the transitions are nearly free — you take what the model was going to produce anyway. If it does not, they have to be generated deliberately, as a separate priced call, and the architecture changes.

The last column is the finding. English prose runs about 4 characters per token. Every row here lands between 0.17 and 0.95. The model is billed for thousands of reasoning tokens and returns a fraction of their volume as text — so what arrives in reasoning_content is a summary of the reasoning, not the reasoning. At default effort you are seeing roughly a sixth of what was actually thought.

Answer to the first question
No. The native trace is neither complete nor about the right things.

It is compressed to roughly 18% of its token volume, and five of the seven things a transition exists to capture appear zero times. This is not a Hyprlab limitation to route around — the major reasoning-model vendors all summarize traces before returning them, for competitive and safety reasons. Assume you cannot have the real one from anybody.

03The alternative, and it is better

So the transitions get written on purpose: a separate call whose whole job is to produce the reasoning, against a JSON schema strict enough that the model cannot skip the uncomfortable parts. Yes, and the deliberate trace beats the private one on every axis worth measuring.

04Is it specific, or just long?

Volume is easy to fake, so the real test is whether the output could have been written for any other story. Here is the theory-of-mind tower for Willa modelling Orren, verbatim:

d1
He laid the forty-year weld on purpose to hold a compact the village still stands under; he has been keeping the reading from her because the work would not bear a second pair of eyes, or because he did not think she could hold the house if she knew.
d2
He believes she is ignorant of the weld and still takes the bar as the village takes it. He believes she came down because she could not sleep, not because she has a reading.
d3
He believes she believes the relic is original and that he has always given true measure; he believes she believes he thinks of her as a child of the house who does not need the old work explained.
✕
Wrong on motive and on what he thinks she came down for. His governing value is no debt passed to a child, not distrust of her competence. The error will cost her the first questions: she will ask why the house was not told, which he will hear as her asking to be given the debt, and they will snag on a problem neither is actually naming yet.

That last row is the one I did not expect to work. Forcing the model to say where the model of the other person is wrong manufactures dramatic irony as a structural requirement rather than hoping for it. The predicted misfire — she asks one thing, he hears another — is the engine of the scene, and it is stated before a word of dialogue exists.

Felt versus expressed

In a screenplay at 70% dialogue, the gap between interior and performance is the entire craft. The schema forces it apart, and asks what leaks through anyway:

felt
Fear that he will have a craft explanation making her reading foolish — and the equal, worse fear that he will not.
expressed
A smith reporting a measurement. Flat, procedural, the bar set down where the light is best.

05Trajectory, not snapshot

This was your addition and it turned out to be the most consequential constraint in the schema. An event spans forty minutes of story time; a transition describing one frozen moment cannot explain it. Every psychology and dynamics block therefore carries phases, each with the perceivable trigger that causes the shift — which keeps the reasoning filmable instead of drifting into interior narration a camera could never catch.

The things that are not minds

You asked for objects, locations and groups to get the same treatment, and this is where the schema pays off hardest. The relic is not a prop being moved around; it changes on named axes, and its meaning is one of them:

custody    with_willa            →  on_the_bench_between_them

// significance_shift
"It is no longer a piece she might have misread, and no longer only the village's

 to show or hide at light. To both of them it now means a living debt rather
 than a finished object."

on_the_bench_between_them is a blocking note, a power reading and a thematic statement in one field. That is what tracking non-mind entities buys.

06How it is wired

Generation is flattened from one call per layer to one call per node, with two calls per node: the transition reasons, then the node is rendered from the transition. Splitting them costs an extra call and buys three things — the reasoning is auditable, the triple is clean, and a thin transition can be caught before it contaminates a node.

story root └─ transition → exposé └─ transition → pl-01 (plot) └─ transition → pl-02 └─ transition → ch-01 (3 reasoned passes: outward → interior → machinery) └─ transition → ch-02 └─ … → transition → ev-001 → ev-002 → …

Character dossiers may be built in passes, each with its own transition: pass 1 the outward and physical, pass 2 the interior and history, pass 3 the state machinery. Each pass sees what the earlier ones fixed and may not contradict it. That is --entity-passes 3; it triples the transition count for entities and is worth it only for major characters.

Grading, because a schema can be satisfied emptily

Every transition is scored structurally the moment it lands — ToM towers reaching depth three, towers with an error named, trajectory phases per entity, sense channels filled, facts carrying source pointers, alternatives rejected. Thin ones are flagged with the specific gap:

not every character reasons to the third degree trajectories are snapshots, not arcs no alternative rejected — not a decision every theory of mind is correct — no dramatic irony available facts asserted without a source pointer

The grader is deterministic and cannot be fooled by length, which matters because length is the one thing a language model will always give you.

07What it costs

Measured, not projected: $0.135 and 485s for the ev-008 transition, $0.169 and 354s for sc-008. Reasoning effort high. Scaling that across the finished 31-node story:

For comparison, the entire screenplay without transitions cost $2.47. So the reasoning layer is roughly 3–5× the price of the artifact it explains — which is the correct ratio to accept, given that the reasoning is the product and the screenplay is the by-product.

Wall-clock is the real constraint, not money. Six to eight minutes per transition serially is 10 hours for a full run. Transitions for sibling nodes at the same depth are independent, so this parallelizes cleanly; the dependency is only down the chain. That is the next thing to build.

08What I would watch

  • Self-consistency is not accuracy. The model both writes the reasoning and grades its own world. Nothing yet checks that a transition's state_preconditions match the state the fold actually computes — that check exists for nodes and should be extended to transitions.
  • The retrospective route is not the forward route. The two transitions in the atlas were reconstructed for nodes that already existed, which is an easier task than deciding a node that does not. The forge run now under way produces them forward; the honest comparison is not in yet.
  • Depth-3 theory of mind may saturate. Both transitions filled d3 well, but for minor characters in low-stakes scenes it will start reading as ceremony. A salience-gated schema — full instrument for majors, lite for the rest — is already in place and untested.
  • One model writing and grading its own reasoning is circular. The whitepaper flags this for annotation and it applies here. A cross-model audit on a sample — you mentioned other APIs are available — would be cheap and worth doing.

09In short

  1. Grok 4.6 will not give you its real reasoning trace. What comes back is a ~18% summary that restates the task and drafts the answer, with zero theory of mind, zero trajectory, and zero craft reasoning.
  2. Asked to write the reasoning out deliberately against a strict schema, it produces 7,000–10,000 words per node that are specific, structured, and better than the private trace would have been.
  3. The forcing constraints that made the difference were yours: trajectory over snapshot, non-mind entities tracked as first-class, and — the one that surprised me — naming where each theory of mind is wrong, which turns dramatic irony from a hope into a schema requirement.
  4. Cost is not the constraint. Wall-clock is, and it parallelizes.

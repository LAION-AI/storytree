01What the system is

The whitepaper describes an analysis pipeline: take a finished novel or screenplay and decompose it into seven layers of machine-readable structure. Its section 14 notes that the reason for building it is the reverse direction. This is that reverse direction, running.

brief └─ STORY ROOT L0/L1 — genre, audience, style, world rules, plot embedding └─ EXPOSÉ L2 — ending decided first, then a sentence-addressable synopsis └─ PLOTS L4 — cause-and-effect chains with keyed spines └─ ENTITIES L3 — dossiers; this object IS the world state at t0 └─ EVENTS L5 — causal DAG declaring which state must change └─ SCENES L6 — beats carrying literal RFC 6902 patch ops └─ PROSE T6 — one page per scene, the leaves

Plots come before entities on purpose. Analysing an existing text you must declare a variable before recording a delta against it, so entities come first. Generating, a cast invented before its plots is a cast with nothing to do. So the plot layer forward-declares entity ids and the entity layer writes dossiers for exactly those.

The patch model, which is the load-bearing idea

The world state is one JSON document keyed by entity id. At t0 it is the entity layer. Every later state is reached by applying RFC 6902 operations, and beats are the only thing in the whole pipeline that authors an operation. Event-, scene- and plot-level patches are derived views over the same beat ops, grouped differently — so nothing is written twice and nothing can drift.

world_state(T) = apply(world_state_t0, concat(ops of every beat before T))

Two schema rules make it work. No arrays in patchable regions, because JSON Pointer addresses arrays by index and a story is a long sequence of insertions. And sentence addressability: every prose block inside a dossier is decomposed one sentence per key, so the story can revise a single sentence of a backstory at a known point in time.

Want. Every token in hand, the invocation spoken in his favour, and to leave unmarked — the customer no Host can place.

Need. To recognise one particular mind as real. He would deny the need if it were named.

Wound. He has been inventory to other systems his whole life and swore to be the one who reads the ledger. Being forgotten feels like safety; being kept feels like being owned.

The want and the need are in direct opposition, and the wound explains why: his detachment is not a pose but a defence. That is what makes its collapse cost him something — the craft sheet's requirement that a flaw be shown as competence first, so that losing it hurts.

04What was learned

Reasoning traces: the API will not give you the real one

Finding
What comes back as reasoning_content is a compressed summary, not the trace.

Measured at 0.17–0.95 characters per billed reasoning token, where English prose runs about 4.0. At default effort you see roughly a sixth of what was thought. Reading it confirms the arithmetic: it restates the task and drafts the answer.

Worse, it is not about the right things. Scored for the content a transition needs: theory of mind, inhibition, trajectory, craft reasoning and object state all appear zero times. Only appraisal and a thin perception signal are present.

Countermeasure
Asked to write the reasoning out on purpose against a strict schema, it produces something better than the private trace would have been.

Same model, same node: 62,781 characters at 4.18 chars per token — real content, not a compression artifact. 10,187 words, every theory-of-mind tower reaching the third degree with its error named, 15 trajectory phases, all 10 cited facts carrying a source pointer. ~$0.15 per node.

Three forcing constraints made the difference, all of them from the brief rather than from me: trajectory over snapshot (each phase needs a perceivable trigger, which keeps the reasoning filmable), non-mind entities as first-class (the relic changes on named axes — custody: with_willa → on_the_bench_between_them is blocking, power reading and theme in one field), and — the one that surprised me — naming where each theory of mind is wrong, which turns dramatic irony from a hope into a schema requirement.

The craft sheet changes the output, measurably

Frey's How to Write a Damn Good Novel I and II, compressed to 6,752 tokens and injected into every generative prompt, with a 401-token variant for the deep layers. The clearest evidence is what the model then writes into its own standing notes:

PREMISE (exactly one): Keeping a useful lie for love leads to a debt the next generation pays. Prove this. Cut anything that does not.

CRUCIBLE: Joss cannot walk away. Winter closes the roads… Name this bond whenever a reader could ask why she does not simply leave.

First appearance: sympathy through work, cold, and a debt she cannot refuse. She is struggling, not merely enduring. No wimp stretch. keep_in_mind, generated with the craft sheet — none of this was in the brief

Premise, crucible, ruling passion, want-versus-need, growth axis, plant and payoff, equalised forces, exposition as weapons. The run without the sheet produced none of these.

Volume II corrected something my second-hand sources had backwards: the ladder is sympathy → identification → empathy, and the rungs are operationally distinct. Sympathy is making the reader feel sorry — the character need not be admirable. Identification is the reader endorsing the goal, which is the lever for a compromised protagonist. Empathy is sensory detail pinned to the feeling it produces. And the payoff sentence: the reader is transported by taking sides in a moral decision, which is why inner conflict is not decoration.

05What refuses to accept bad work

Twenty-one deterministic rules, adapted from the whitepaper's C1–C10. Span-anchoring has no meaning without a source text and is replaced by graph-completeness and budget rules.

G1 every event serves a plot G2 every spine step discharged G3 causal graph acyclic and connected G5 state continuity G6 bidirectional scene/event coverage G7 primary edges form one tree G10 no direct speech below the exposé G12 no arrays in patchable regions G13 sentence decomposition G15 declare-then-realise G17 t0 mirrors declared inits G20 values stay in declared domains

G5 and G15 are the ones that matter. G5 catches contradiction mechanically: because state is computed by folding patches rather than described, a claim that disagrees with the fold cannot hide. G15 enforces the split between L5 declaring what must change and L6 realising exactly that — no more, no less.

Test results
52 engine assertions, 23 of 23 injected errors caught, 79 DOM assertions, and the browser's fold reproduces the Python fold byte for byte.

The whitepaper's phase-0 exit criterion is a validator catching over 90% of deliberately introduced faults. Both finished graphs validate with zero structural errors.

06The viewer and the co-writer

Ten tabs over the graph: story root with the embedding, exposé with every synopsis sentence and the plot that claims it, plots as swimlanes with cross-plot causation drawn between them, a cast browser with copyable JSON pointers, canvas DAGs for events and scenes with a fixed inspector, a scrubber that folds the world forward beat by beat, the reasoning transitions, and the script.

The co-writer is a chat dock with fourteen tools — read, upsert per layer, RFC 6902 patch, delete, validate, and show, which opens the relevant tab and selects what it just built. Any OpenAI-compatible endpoint; the key lives in your browser and never in the page. Tested end to end against the live API, not stubbed:

07Limits, stated plainly

  • Self-consistency is not accuracy. The same model writes the reasoning and grades its own world. Nothing yet checks a transition's declared preconditions against the state the fold actually computes.
  • The two existing transitions were reconstructed backwards, for nodes that already existed. That is an easier task than deciding a node that does not, and the honest forward-versus-backward comparison is not in yet.
  • The repair loop only patches the layer that just ran. A violation whose real cause lives one layer up is reported rather than silently fixed in the wrong place.
  • Scene segmentation carries forward the folded state and a two-scene tail, not the full prior text. Boundary events across a batch edge are where continuity would drift first.
  • One model annotating and being trained on its own annotations is circular. The whitepaper flags this; a cross-model audit on a sample would be cheap.
  • The published artifact cannot run the co-writer. A strict content policy blocks external hosts there, and it also blocks page-initiated downloads. Both work from the Cloudflare link.

  3. The protagonist is Hale, and the second lead is a Host who owns her own investigation plot.
